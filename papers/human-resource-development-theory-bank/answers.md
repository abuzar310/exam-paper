# MODULE 1: INTRODUCTION TO HRD – 2 MARKS

## Q1. Define Human Resource Development (HRD). (Q.P. 50374) (Model Nov 2026)
**Human Resource Development (HRD)** is a planned and continuous process of developing employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and values so that they contribute effectively to organisational objectives and achieve personal growth. In simple words, HRD means improving the abilities and potential of employees for better performance.

## Q2. What are the outcomes of HRD? (Q.P. 50374)
**Outcomes of HRD** are the results that the development of people brings. Nationally they are a skilled workforce, employment and economic development; in the organisation they are competent employees, better quality, leaders, satisfaction, lower turnover and effectiveness.

## Q3. Mention any two qualities of an HRD professional. (Q.P. 50374)
1. **Communication and interpersonal skills:** An HRD professional must explain, listen and build good relations with employees at every level.
2. **Coaching and training ability:** He must be able to train, coach and guide employees to learn new skills and grow.

## Q4. State any two objectives of HRD.
1. **Improve skills and competencies:** HRD raises employees' skills, productivity and performance.
2. **Develop future leaders:** HRD prepares employees for higher responsibilities and helps the organisation adapt to change.

## Q5. What is macro-level and micro-level HRD?
1. **Macro-level HRD:** The development of human resources at the national or societal level, through education, skill development, vocational training and digital literacy.
2. **Micro-level HRD:** The development of employees within an organisation, through training, performance appraisal, career planning, leadership development and counselling.

## Q6. What are HRD subsystems?
**HRD subsystems** are the interconnected activities designed to improve employee and organisational capabilities. They include training and development, performance appraisal, career planning, potential appraisal, employee counselling, rewards and recognition, and succession planning.

## Q7. What is succession planning?
**Succession planning** is the HRD subsystem that prepares employees for future key positions. It makes sure that trained people are ready to fill important posts when they fall vacant.

## Q8. What is Human Resource Planning? (Model Nov 2026)
**Human Resource Planning (HRP)** is the process of estimating future human resource requirements and ensuring that the right people are available at the right place and time. In short, it means the right person, in the right job, at the right time and in the right number.

## Q9. What is meant by potential?
**Potential** means an employee's future capability to perform higher or more complex responsibilities. It shows what the employee can become, not only what he is doing now.

## Q10. What is potential appraisal? (Model Nov 2026)
**Potential appraisal** is a systematic process of evaluating an employee's future capability, leadership qualities and competencies to take up higher-level responsibilities. It asks "what can the employee achieve?" and supports promotion, succession planning and career development.

## Q11. What is an assessment centre? (Model Nov 2026)
An **assessment centre** is a systematic method of evaluating employees' competencies through simulated job situations such as group discussion, role play, case study, presentation, psychometric tests and in-basket exercises. It is used for selection, promotion, potential appraisal and leadership development.

## Q12. What is an in-basket exercise?
An **in-basket exercise** is an assessment centre technique in which the candidate deals with a set of letters, memos and tasks within a fixed time. It tests decision-making and prioritisation.

# MODULE 1: INTRODUCTION TO HRD – 5 MARKS

## Q1. Explain the developmental perspective of HRD. (Q.P. 50374)
1. **Employees as assets with potential:** The developmental perspective views employees as people whose knowledge, skills and potential can be continuously developed.
2. **Continuous learning:** Learning is a lifelong process; employees keep learning new skills to meet changes in work and technology.
3. **Skill, competency and career growth:** It builds the competencies needed for the present job and plans a career path for future growth.
4. **Leadership development:** It identifies and prepares employees for future managerial and leadership roles.
5. **Organisational learning and long-term growth:** Individual learning spreads across the organisation, so both the employee and the organisation grow in the long run.

## Q2. Briefly explain the importance of HRD in the present context. (Q.P. 50374)
1. **Improves employee performance:** Training raises the skills, productivity and quality of work.
2. **Adapts employees to technological change:** HRD helps employees learn new technology and digital tools.
3. **Develops leadership capabilities:** It builds a pipeline of future leaders from within the organisation.
4. **Increases motivation, engagement and retention:** Employees who see growth and career development feel valued and are less likely to leave.
5. **Encourages innovation and competitive advantage:** Skilled and learning employees bring new ideas, which keep the organisation ahead of competitors.

## Q3. Trace the evolution of HRD from personnel management. (Model Nov 2026)
1. **Personnel management (first stage):** The traditional, administrative stage; its focus was record keeping, wages, discipline and welfare, and its aim was to maintain employees.
2. **Human resource management (second stage):** The managerial stage; employees were seen as a resource to be managed and utilised for present performance.
3. **Human resource development (third stage):** The developmental stage; the focus is on developing the knowledge, skills, attitudes and potential of employees.
4. **Change in focus:** Administration (PM) → managing people (HRM) → developing people (HRD).
5. **Change in orientation:** Short-term maintenance (PM) → present performance (HRM) → long-term growth of the employee and the organisation (HRD).

## Q4. Explain the subsystems of HRD. (Model Nov 2026)
1. **Training and development:** Improves knowledge and skills for the present job and prepares employees for future roles.
2. **Performance and potential appraisal:** Performance appraisal evaluates present performance; potential appraisal identifies future capability for higher posts.
3. **Career planning and succession planning:** Maps the employee's career path and prepares employees to fill key positions in future.
4. **Employee counselling and feedback:** Helps employees with work-related and personal concerns and tells them how to improve.
5. **Rewards, recognition and organisation development:** Rewards encourage the desired performance, and OD builds a culture of openness, trust and teamwork.

## Q5. Distinguish between macro HRD and micro HRD.
| Basis | Macro HRD | Micro HRD |
|---|---|---|
| Level | National or societal level | Organisational level |
| Initiated by | Government and societal initiatives | Company initiatives |
| Develops | The national workforce | The organisation's own employees |
| Activities | Education, skill development, vocational training, employment generation, digital literacy | Training and development, performance appraisal, career planning, leadership development, counselling |
| Result | National economic development | Organisational effectiveness |

## Q6. State the qualities and competencies of an HRD professional.
1. **Communication and interpersonal skills:** Explains clearly, listens and builds good relations at every level.
2. **Leadership, decision-making and problem-solving:** Guides people, takes sound decisions and handles problems calmly.
3. **Ethics, integrity and emotional intelligence:** Is fair and honest and understands people's feelings.
4. **Strategic thinking and change management:** Links HRD to business goals and helps the organisation accept change.
5. **Coaching, training and performance management:** Acts as trainer, coach, mentor, facilitator, counsellor, performance evaluator and change agent.

## Q7. Explain the objectives and steps of Human Resource Planning.
1. **Objectives:** Forecast manpower needs, avoid shortage and surplus, improve employee utilisation, and support recruitment, training and career planning.
2. **Analyse objectives:** Study the organisation's objectives to know what work must be done.
3. **Forecast demand and supply:** Estimate the number and kind of employees needed, and the employees who will be available.
4. **Identify HR gaps:** Compare demand with supply to find the shortage or surplus.
5. **Recruit, train and evaluate:** Recruit and select employees, provide training and development, and evaluate the plan.

## Q8. Explain the objectives and methods of potential appraisal.
1. **Meaning and objectives:** Potential appraisal evaluates an employee's future abilities and growth potential, to identify future leaders, support promotion and succession planning, and find training needs.
2. **Performance review:** The employee's past performance record is studied to judge readiness for a higher job.
3. **Psychological tests and interviews:** Tests measure aptitude and personality, and interviews explore the employee's abilities and ambitions.
4. **Assessment centres:** The employee is observed in simulated job situations such as group discussion, role play and in-basket exercises.
5. **360-degree feedback:** Views on the employee are collected from superiors, peers, subordinates and others who work with him.

## Q9. Explain the assessment centre and its techniques.
1. **Meaning:** An assessment centre evaluates employees' competencies through simulated job situations; it is used for recruitment, promotion, leadership development and potential appraisal.
2. **Group discussion and role play:** Group discussion tests communication and teamwork; role play tests leadership and interpersonal skills.
3. **Case study and presentation:** A case study tests problem-solving and analytical ability; a presentation tests communication and confidence.
4. **Psychometric tests and in-basket exercise:** Psychometric tests measure personality and aptitude; the in-basket exercise tests decision-making and prioritisation.
5. **Advantages and limitations:** It gives a systematic evaluation of many competencies and identifies future leaders, but it is expensive, time-consuming and needs trained assessors.

## Q1. Explain the subsystems of HRD with examples. (Q.P. 50374)
### Planning and appraisal subsystems
1. **Human resource planning:** Forecasting the number and kind of employees needed and making them available at the right time. Example: a bank plans the staff needed for 20 new branches.
2. **Performance appraisal:** Assessing an employee's present performance against standards. Example: a salesperson is rated against his target and his weak presentation skill is identified.
3. **Potential appraisal:** Assessing an employee's capacity for future, higher roles. Example: a good sales executive is assessed through an assessment centre for the sales manager post.
4. **Feedback and performance coaching:** Telling employees how they are doing and helping them improve. Example: a manager guides an employee to structure his reports better.
5. **Career planning:** Mapping a career path and the competencies needed for it. Example: executive → supervisor → branch manager, with training at each step.
### Development and support subsystems
6. **Training and development:** Building skills for the present job and the future. Example: training staff on new CRM software, and a leadership programme for future managers.
7. **Succession planning:** Preparing employees to fill key posts when they fall vacant. Example: a deputy manager is groomed to replace the retiring plant head.
8. **Organisation development:** Planned efforts to improve the culture, teamwork and openness of the whole organisation. Example: team-building workshops that build trust and collaboration (OCTAPAC).
9. **Rewards and recognition:** Rewarding desired behaviour and good performance. Example: an 'employee of the month' award, incentives and promotion.
10. **Employee welfare, counselling and HR information system:** Looking after the well-being of employees and keeping HR records that support decisions. Example: health check-ups, counselling services and a digital employee database.

## Q2. Define HRD. Explain HRD at the macro and micro levels, and the qualities and competencies required of an HRD professional. (Model Nov 2026)
### Meaning and levels of HRD
1. **Definition:** HRD is a planned and continuous process of developing employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and values for organisational objectives and personal growth.
2. **Macro-level HRD:** The development of human resources at the national or societal level, through government and social initiatives.
3. **Macro-level activities:** Education and skill development, vocational training, employment generation and digital literacy, leading to national economic development.
4. **Micro-level HRD:** The development of employees within an organisation, through company initiatives.
5. **Micro-level activities:** Training and development, performance appraisal, career planning, leadership development and counselling, leading to organisational effectiveness.
### Qualities and competencies of an HRD professional
6. **Communication and interpersonal skills:** Explains clearly, listens and builds good relations at every level.
7. **Leadership, decision-making and problem-solving:** Guides people, takes sound decisions and handles problems calmly.
8. **Ethics, integrity and emotional intelligence:** Is fair and honest and understands people's feelings.
9. **Strategic thinking and change management:** Links HRD to business goals and helps the organisation accept change.
10. **Coaching, training and performance management:** Acts as trainer, coach, mentor, facilitator, counsellor, performance evaluator and change agent.

## Q3. Explain Human Resource Planning, potential appraisal and the assessment centre.
### Human Resource Planning
1. **Meaning:** HRP is the process of estimating future human resource requirements and ensuring that the right people are available at the right place and time.
2. **Objectives:** Forecast manpower needs, avoid shortage and surplus, improve employee utilisation, and support recruitment, training and career planning.
3. **Steps:** Analyse organisational objectives, forecast HR demand and HR supply, and identify HR gaps.
4. **Further steps:** Recruit and select employees, provide training and development, and evaluate the plan.
### Potential appraisal
5. **Meaning:** Potential is an employee's future capability for higher responsibilities; potential appraisal systematically evaluates future abilities, leadership qualities, competencies and growth potential.
6. **Objectives:** Identify future leaders, support promotion decisions, succession planning and career development, and identify training needs.
7. **Methods:** Performance review, psychological tests, interviews, assessment centres and 360-degree feedback.
### Assessment centre
8. **Meaning:** A systematic method of evaluating employees' competencies through simulated job situations, used for recruitment, promotion, leadership development and potential appraisal.
9. **Techniques:** Group discussion, role play, case study, presentation, psychometric tests and in-basket exercise.
10. **Advantages and limitations:** Systematic evaluation of many competencies, identifies future leaders and supports better promotion decisions; but it is expensive, time-consuming and needs trained assessors.

# MODULE 2: HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT SYSTEM – 2 MARKS

## Q1. What are HRD mechanisms?
**HRD mechanisms** are the formal and informal systems, processes and practices used by an organisation to develop employees. They are the tools through which HRD actually operates, such as appraisal, training, feedback, career planning and job rotation.

## Q2. Mention any two HRD mechanisms. (Model Nov 2026)
1. **Performance appraisal:** The systematic assessment of an employee's performance against set standards, which also identifies training needs.
2. **Training and development:** Training improves skills for the present job; development prepares the employee for future roles.

## Q3. What is job rotation?
**Job rotation** means moving an employee periodically between different jobs or departments. It broadens experience, reduces monotony and prepares employees for management (for example HR → Finance → Marketing → Operations).

## Q4. What is meant by HRD climate? (Q.P. 50374)
**HRD climate** is the employees' perception of whether their organisation provides an environment that supports learning, development, openness, trust and growth. It is shaped by the general climate, the OCTAPAC culture and the HRD mechanisms of the organisation.

## Q5. Expand OCTAPAC.
**OCTAPAC** stands for Openness, Confrontation, Trust, Autonomy, Proactivity, Authenticity and Collaboration. These seven values create a developmental organisational culture.

## Q6. What is organisational climate? (Model Nov 2026)
**Organisational climate** is the employees' shared perception of the present work environment, such as openness, trust, support, fairness and freedom. It is "how the organisation feels" to its members, and it can change faster than culture.

## Q7. What is the model of employee behaviour?
The **model of employee behaviour** is: individual factors + environment + situation → behaviour → performance and outcomes. It shows that behaviour comes from the interaction between the person and the environment, not simply from the employee being "good" or "bad".

## Q8. State any two internal factors influencing employee behaviour.
1. **Personality:** It decides how an employee responds to situations; for example, an emotionally stable employee stays calm under pressure.
2. **Motivation:** It decides the direction, intensity and persistence of behaviour; a motivated employee puts more effort towards goals.

## Q9. State any two external factors influencing employee behaviour. (Q.P. 50374)
1. **Leadership:** The style of the manager or leader strongly influences how employees think and act.
2. **Reward systems:** Employees behave according to the rewards and recognition the organisation gives.

## Q10. What is learning?
**Learning** is a relatively lasting change in knowledge, skills, attitudes or behaviour resulting from experience, practice, instruction or interaction with the environment. Training is useful only when employees actually learn and transfer that learning to their jobs.

## Q11. What is meant by a learning style? (Model Nov 2026)
A **learning style** is a person's preferred way of taking in and processing information while learning, such as visual, auditory, reading/writing or kinesthetic (learning by doing). Trainers should use a mix of methods rather than label employees by one style.

## Q12. What is instructional psychology?
**Instructional psychology** studies how learning environments and instructional methods can be designed to produce effective learning. It asks "what should we do to help this person learn?" and works through the learning goal, the learner's starting point, the instructional conditions and assessment.

## Q13. What is cognitive psychology?
**Cognitive psychology** studies the mental processes of attention, perception, memory, thinking, reasoning, problem-solving, decision-making and learning. It views employees as active processors who interpret, organise, store and retrieve information.

# MODULE 2: HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT SYSTEM – 5 MARKS

## Q1. Write a short note on HRD mechanisms. (Q.P. 50374)
1. **Performance appraisal:** The systematic assessment of an employee's performance against set standards; it identifies strengths, weaknesses and training needs.
2. **Potential appraisal:** It evaluates an employee's capacity to take up higher-level or future responsibilities, and supports promotion and succession.
3. **Training and development:** Training improves skills for the present job; development prepares the employee for future roles.
4. **Feedback and counselling:** Feedback tells employees how their performance compares with standards; counselling helps them solve performance and career problems.
5. **Career planning and job rotation:** Career planning maps the employee's career path, and job rotation moves him through different jobs to broaden his experience.

## Q2. Distinguish between performance appraisal and potential appraisal.
| Basis | Performance appraisal | Potential appraisal |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Current or past performance | Future capability |
| Question asked | "How well is the employee doing?" | "What could the employee become?" |
| Job | Current job | Future responsibilities |
| Orientation | Performance-oriented | Development-oriented |
| Supports | Rewards | Promotion and succession |

## Q3. Explain the OCTAPAC culture.
1. **Openness:** Employees can communicate ideas, concerns and information freely.
2. **Confrontation:** Problems are faced directly rather than ignored; it means discussing the real problem and solving it, not fighting.
3. **Trust:** Employees believe that others will act fairly and responsibly.
4. **Autonomy and proactivity:** Employees have reasonable freedom to decide within their responsibilities, and take initiative instead of waiting for instructions.
5. **Authenticity and collaboration:** What people say matches what they do, and employees work together rather than in unhealthy competition.

## Q4. Distinguish between organisational climate and organisational culture.
| Basis | Organisational climate | Organisational culture |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Employees' perception of the current workplace | Deeper values, beliefs, assumptions and norms that guide behaviour |
| Key idea | How the organisation feels | Why the organisation behaves that way |
| Visibility | More visible | Often less visible |
| Change | Relatively easier and quicker to change | Deeper and slower to change |
| Example | An open-door policy that makes staff feel free to talk now | Openness as a value held for many years |

## Q5. Explain the external and internal factors influencing employee behaviour. (Model Nov 2026)
1. **Internal – personality and perception:** Personality decides how a person reacts to situations, and perception decides how they interpret events.
2. **Internal – attitude, motivation and values:** A positive attitude, strong motivation and values such as honesty and fairness lead to better behaviour.
3. **Internal – ability and learning:** The capacity to do the task and past experience shape how an employee behaves.
4. **External – leadership, culture and climate:** The leader's style and the organisation's culture and climate strongly influence how employees act.
5. **External – rewards, job design, group, policies and technology:** Rewards, the nature of the job, co-workers, fair policies, technology and the economic environment also shape behaviour.

## Q6. Explain the learning principles in HRD. (Q.P. 50374)
1. **Motivation:** People learn better when they have a reason to learn, for example to get promoted.
2. **Readiness:** The learner must be mentally and physically ready; a tired or uninterested employee learns little.
3. **Practice and active participation:** Repeated practice and taking part through cases, discussions and role plays make learning stronger than a lecture alone.
4. **Feedback and reinforcement:** Learners need to know what they did right and wrong, and behaviour that is praised or rewarded is repeated.
5. **Meaningfulness and transfer:** Learning should be linked to real work, and what is learned must be applied on the job; only then does performance improve.

## Q7. Explain the principles of learning and how learning can be maximised. (Model Nov 2026)
1. **Motivation and readiness:** People learn better when they have a reason to learn and are mentally and physically ready.
2. **Practice and active participation:** Repeated practice and taking part through cases, discussions and role plays make learning stronger than a lecture alone.
3. **Feedback and reinforcement:** Learners need to know what they did right and wrong, and behaviour that is rewarded is repeated.
4. **Meaningfulness and transfer:** Learning must be linked to real work and applied on the job; only then does performance improve.
5. **Maximising learning:** Set clear objectives, keep content relevant and difficulty right, give practice and repetition, a supportive environment, follow-up after training and evaluation of results.

## Q8. Explain how organisations can maximise employee learning.
1. **Clear objectives and right difficulty:** Learners know exactly what they must achieve, and the training is neither too easy nor too difficult.
2. **Relevant content and active practice:** Content relates to real job requirements, and learners perform tasks rather than only hear about them.
3. **Feedback and repetition:** Feedback allows correction, and important skills are practised again and again.
4. **Application and a supportive environment:** Learners get chances to use their knowledge, and managers and co-workers support the learning.
5. **Follow-up and evaluation:** Learning is reinforced after formal training, and the organisation checks whether it actually improved performance.

## Q9. Explain the recent developments in instructional and cognitive psychology.
1. **Active and experiential learning:** Learners take part instead of only listening, and learn through experience and reflection.
2. **Simulation:** Employees practise realistic situations in a safe environment.
3. **Microlearning and e-learning:** Content is split into short, focused units (for example a 7-minute module on one Excel function) and delivered digitally.
4. **Adaptive, social and self-directed learning:** Content adjusts to the learner's progress, employees learn by observing others, and they take responsibility for their own learning needs.
5. **Evidence-based design and cognitive load:** Methods supported by research are used, and complex material is broken into manageable units so learners are not overloaded.

# MODULE 2: HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT SYSTEM – 10 MARKS

## Q1. Discuss the influence of employee behaviour on organisational effectiveness. (Q.P. 50374)
### How employee behaviour affects effectiveness
1. **Work performance and productivity:** Committed and skilled behaviour gives more output and better quality, which directly raises organisational effectiveness.
2. **Attendance and punctuality:** Regular attendance keeps work on time; absenteeism and late coming reduce output and raise costs.
3. **Cooperation and teamwork:** When employees cooperate across departments, work flows smoothly and goals are reached faster.
4. **Communication:** Open and clear communication reduces mistakes and conflicts and speeds up decisions.
5. **Adaptability to change:** Employees who accept new technology and methods help the organisation grow; resistance slows it down.
6. **Organisational citizenship behaviour:** Employees who do more than their duty, help colleagues and protect the company's name build a strong organisation.
7. **Customer orientation and responsiveness:** Employees who take ownership solve customer complaints quickly, which raises customer satisfaction and sales.
### Factors and the role of HRD
8. **Negative behaviour harms effectiveness:** Poor attitude, conflict, fear of blame and frequent job-leaving raise costs and lower performance; high turnover means a constant loss of trained staff.
9. **Behaviour depends on internal and external factors:** Internal factors are personality, perception, attitude, motivation, ability and values; external factors are leadership, culture, climate, rewards, job design and policies.
10. **HRD improves behaviour and results:** Training, feedback, a good HRD climate and fair rewards change behaviour, which leads to better performance and organisational effectiveness. Example: after an HRD programme at a retail company, turnover fell from 24% to 17% and customer satisfaction rose from 76% to 84%.

## Q2. Explain the HRD mechanisms. Discuss the role of organisational climate and culture in HRD. (Model Nov 2026)
### HRD mechanisms
1. **Performance appraisal:** Assesses performance against standards and identifies strengths, gaps and training needs.
2. **Potential appraisal:** Assesses the capacity for future, higher roles. Example: can a good sales executive become a sales manager?
3. **Training and development:** Training builds present competence; development builds future capability.
4. **Feedback and counselling:** Specific, timely and constructive feedback; counselling aimed at improvement, not punishment.
5. **Career planning, job rotation, coaching and rewards:** Career paths, movement across departments, guidance from seniors and recognition of good work.
### Role of organisational climate and culture
6. **Meaning:** Climate is how the organisation feels to employees now; culture is the deeper values, beliefs and norms that explain why it behaves that way.
7. **HRD climate:** The employees' perception that the organisation supports learning, openness, trust and growth; it is built by top management support, fair personnel policies and line manager support.
8. **OCTAPAC culture:** Openness, Confrontation, Trust, Autonomy, Proactivity, Authenticity and Collaboration create a developmental culture.
9. **Positive climate:** Encourages learning, innovation, initiative, cooperation, motivation, communication and commitment.
10. **Negative climate:** Causes fear, resistance to change, low motivation, poor communication and turnover, so HRD efforts fail.

## Q3. Explain individual differences in the learning process, and learning strategies and styles.
### Individual differences in learning
1. **Meaning:** Not everyone learns in the same way or at the same speed, so HRD must consider each learner's starting level.
2. **Differences in background:** Employees differ in intelligence, prior knowledge, experience, motivation and personality.
3. **Other differences:** They also differ in attention, confidence, age, language ability, technical competence and cognitive ability.
4. **Example:** In the same Excel training, an employee who has used spreadsheets for three years and one who has never opened Excel cannot be taught at the same speed.
### Learning strategies
5. **Rehearsal:** Repeating information or skills, such as practising a presentation several times.
6. **Elaboration and organisation:** Connecting new information with existing knowledge, and arranging it with headings, diagrams and concept maps.
7. **Practice and self-regulation:** Repeatedly performing the skill, and monitoring one's own learning: set goal → study → monitor progress → find weakness → change strategy.
8. **Collaborative learning:** Learning through others by group discussion, peer learning, team projects and mentoring.
### Learning styles
9. **Meaning and types:** Learning styles classify learners by their preferred way of learning: visual, auditory, reading/writing and kinesthetic.
10. **Use in HRD:** Teaching should not be tied to one fixed style; trainers use explanation, demonstration, examples, practice, discussion, simulation, feedback and reflection.

# MODULE 3: DEVELOPING HUMAN CAPACITY – 2 MARKS

## Q1. What is meant by developing human capacity?
**Developing human capacity** means improving employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and overall potential so that they can perform their jobs effectively and contribute to organisational growth. In simple words, it means making employees more capable, skilled and productive.

## Q2. What is aptitude? (Q.P. 50374)
**Aptitude** is a person's natural ability or potential to learn and perform a particular kind of work, such as customer handling, teamwork or problem-solving. It shows how well a person can perform after training, not only what he already knows.

## Q3. What is Aptitude, Knowledge and Values? (Model Nov 2026)
**Aptitude** is a person's natural ability or potential to learn a particular skill or do a specific type of work. **Knowledge** is the information, facts and understanding gained through education, training, reading and experience. **Values** are the basic beliefs and principles that guide a person's behaviour and decisions about what is right and wrong.

## Q4. What are human relations skills?
**Human relations skills** are the abilities required to communicate, understand, cooperate and maintain good relationships with other people. They include communication, listening, teamwork, empathy, conflict management and emotional intelligence.

## Q5. What is responsiveness?
**Responsiveness** is the ability and willingness of an employee or organisation to respond promptly and appropriately to people's needs, requests, problems and feedback. Example: an employee who listens to a customer's complaint about an account error and resolves it in time.

## Q6. What is meant by loyalty and commitment?
1. **Loyalty:** The faithfulness and support an employee shows towards the organisation.
2. **Commitment:** The willingness and dedication to work sincerely towards organisational goals.

## Q7. What is transparency?
**Transparency** means openness, honesty and clarity in an organisation's decisions, policies, activities and communication. It means sharing relevant information clearly, for example explaining the criteria used for appraisal, rewards and promotion.

## Q8. Give the meaning of leadership development. (Q.P. 50374)
**Leadership development** is the planned process of building the leadership qualities of employees, such as decision-making, communication, team handling and strategic thinking, so that they are ready for managerial roles. It uses training, coaching, mentoring, job rotation and leadership projects.

## Q9. What is human resource accounting? (Model Nov 2026)
**Human resource accounting (HRA)** is the process of identifying, measuring and reporting the cost and value of an organisation's human resources. It treats spending on recruitment, selection, induction, training and development as an investment in people (for example Rs. 40,000 per employee).

## Q10. What is an HR audit? (Model Nov 2026)
An **HR audit** is a systematic examination and evaluation of an organisation's HR policies, procedures, practices and systems. It checks whether HR activities such as recruitment, training, pay, appraisal and records are managed properly and whether the applicable rules and laws are followed.

## Q11. What is an HRD audit? (Q.P. 50374)
**HRD audit** is a systematic review of an organisation's HRD strategies, systems, practices and competencies to find out how well they support business goals. It shows the strengths, gaps and areas where HRD must improve.

## Q12. What is benchmarking?
**Benchmarking** is the process of comparing an organisation's practices, processes or performance with those of other organisations or recognised standards to identify opportunities for improvement. Example: a company that takes 30 days to recruit studies another that does it in 15 days.

## Q13. What is meant by impact assessment and the bottom line?
1. **Impact assessment:** The process of measuring the results and benefits of HRD activities.
2. **Bottom line:** The organisation's final financial result, particularly its net profit.

# MODULE 3: DEVELOPING HUMAN CAPACITY – 5 MARKS

## Q1. Explain the types of aptitude.
1. **Numerical aptitude:** Ability to understand numbers and calculations; for example, an accountant calculating profit, loss or tax.
2. **Verbal aptitude:** Ability to understand language and communicate; for example, a customer-care executive explaining a product.
3. **Logical aptitude:** Ability to think logically and solve problems; for example, a manager finding out why sales have fallen.
4. **Mechanical aptitude:** Ability to understand machines and tools; for example, a technician identifying a machine fault.
5. **Creative aptitude:** Ability to generate new ideas; for example, a marketing employee developing an advertising idea.

## Q2. Explain the human relations skills required by employees.
1. **Communication and listening:** Expressing ideas clearly, and listening carefully without interrupting.
2. **Teamwork and cooperation:** Working with others and helping them towards a common goal.

## Q6. Explain human resource accounting and its methods.
1. **Meaning:** HRA identifies, measures and reports the costs and value of an organisation's human resources, showing the investment made in employees.
2. **Historical cost method:** Records the actual costs of recruiting, selecting, training and developing an employee. Example: Rs. 10,000 + Rs. 5,000 + Rs. 15,000 + Rs. 10,000 = Rs. 40,000.
3. **Replacement cost method:** Estimates the cost of replacing an employee with someone of similar skills and experience, including recruitment and training costs.
4. **Opportunity cost method:** Considers the value of an employee's services when choosing between alternative uses or departments.
5. **Limitations:** Human value is hard to measure in money, employees are not like physical assets, methods give different values and future contribution is uncertain.

## Q1. What is training?
**Training** is a planned process of teaching employees the knowledge, skills and work-related behaviour needed to perform their present jobs effectively. Example: a new cashier is trained to use billing software, handle cash and issue receipts.

## Q2. What is development?
**Development** is a broader and longer-term process that prepares employees for future responsibilities, higher positions and changing organisational needs. Example: leadership and strategic-planning programmes for an employee who may become a department manager.

## Q3. What is education?

## Q5. What is Training Need Analysis?

1. **Internal training:** Training organised within the organisation by its own managers, supervisors or trainers. Example: a senior accountant trains new accounts assistants.

**Induction training** introduces new employees to the organisation, its rules, its people and the workplace. It helps them settle into the job quickly.

5. **Use of competency mapping:** The gaps lead to an individual development plan of training, coaching, mentoring or work experience. Example: an employee with basic Excel in a job that needs advanced Excel gets advanced Excel training.

## Q8. Explain the four perspectives of the Balanced Scorecard.
1. **Financial:** How do our activities affect financial performance? Example: training cost, savings or return on training investment.
2. **Customer:** How do customers view the organisation? Example: customer satisfaction, complaints or service ratings.
3. **Internal process:** Which internal processes must work well? Example: error rate, service time and quality.
4. **Learning and growth:** How can the organisation keep improving? Example: employee skills, training completion, engagement and competency levels.
5. **Importance in HRD:** It links employee learning with strategy and shows how skills improve processes and customer results. Example: a bank trains staff in digital banking and tracks skills, transaction errors, satisfaction and efficiency.

## Q9. Discuss the 4-D cycle of Appreciative Inquiry.
1. **Discover:** Identify what works well and the organisation's strengths; "when have our teams performed at their best?"
2. **Dream:** Imagine what the organisation could achieve by building on its strengths.
3. **Design:** Plan the systems, practices and actions that support the desired future.
4. **Destiny (Deliver):** Implement the actions, learn and keep improving.
5. **Use in HRD:** It finds successful practices, encourages participation and positive communication, and builds shared ownership of change. Example: instead of asking why teamwork is poor, employees describe a time when it was excellent and plan to repeat it.

## Q10. Discuss the importance of research in HRD.
1. **Finds learning needs:** Research identifies employees' learning and development needs and emerging skill requirements.
2. **Explains performance:** It shows the factors that affect employee performance, motivation and job satisfaction.
3. **Evaluates training:** It checks the effectiveness and return on investment of training programmes. Example: comparing error rates before and after training.
4. **Studies the workplace:** It studies organisational climate, culture, employee behaviour, leadership and succession.
5. **Better decisions:** It improves HRD policies and lets the organisation decide on evidence rather than assumptions.

## Q1. Critically examine the recent trends in HRD. (Q.P. 50374)
### Recent trends
1. **Continuous learning and reskilling:** Employees keep learning; reskilling prepares them for a new role and upskilling improves skills for the present role.
2. **Technology-enabled learning:** Learning management systems, e-learning, mobile learning, simulations and blended learning.
3. **Coaching and mentoring:** Individual guidance for specific skills and for long-term career growth.
4. **Competency-based HRD:** Competency frameworks and competency mapping guide recruitment, training, promotion and career development.
5. **Research, data and measurement:** HRD research and analytics identify needs, and outcomes are measured through ROI and the balanced scorecard.
6. **Engagement, wellbeing and positive change:** More attention to engagement, inclusion, culture and strength-based approaches such as appreciative inquiry.
7. **Future direction:** Personalised learning, digital and problem-solving skills, and closer alignment of HRD strategy with organisational strategy.
### Critical view
8. **Technology problems:** Uneven digital literacy, the need for devices and internet, less direct interaction and the protection of employee data.
9. **Poor utilisation:** Training is wasted when employees lack tools, time, support or a chance to practise, or when the content is not relevant to the job.
10. **Careful adaptation:** A practice from another country or company may not suit local culture, language, laws and conditions; competency mapping must use real evidence and not be a one-time exercise.

## Q2. Discuss training for trainers and HRD professionals, promoting research in HRD, and creating awareness and commitment to HRD.
### Training for trainers and HRD professionals
1. **Meaning:** Developing the knowledge and skills of people who deliver training, and of HRD professionals who plan, coordinate and evaluate HRD.
2. **Need:** Trainers must learn how adults learn, use suitable methods, manage groups, evaluate learning and use digital tools.
3. **Competencies of a trainer:** Subject knowledge, communication, facilitation, instructional design, technology skills, assessment and adaptability.
### Promoting research in HRD
4. **Meaning:** The systematic collection and analysis of information on employee development and HRD practices, so that decisions rest on evidence.
5. **Areas:** Training needs, engagement and job satisfaction, leadership and succession, workplace learning, digital learning, training ROI and future competencies.
6. **Steps:** Identify the problem, review earlier studies, set objectives, select the method, collect data ethically, analyse it, conclude and share the findings.
### Creating awareness and commitment
7. **Meaning:** Helping employees and managers understand the purpose and benefits of HRD, and encouraging them to support and take part in it.
8. **Importance:** Employees do not take part if they do not see the purpose, and they apply learning only when supervisors give time and support.
9. **Communication methods:** Communicate HRD policies clearly, hold orientation and awareness sessions, and explain how learning supports careers and goals.
10. **Support methods:** Get visible support from senior management, discuss development in performance reviews, recognise employees who apply learning, and invite feedback.

## Q3. Define competency framework and explain the steps involved in competency mapping.
### Competency framework
1. **Competency:** A combination of knowledge, skills, abilities and behaviours that enables a person to perform a role effectively. Example: an HR executive needs recruitment knowledge, interviewing skill, communication and confidentiality.
2. **Competency framework:** An organised structure of the competencies required for different jobs, roles or levels, used for recruitment, training, appraisal, promotion and career development.
3. **Types of competencies:** Core (integrity, teamwork, communication), functional or technical (accounting knowledge for an accountant) and managerial (delegation, planning, decision-making).
4. **Importance:** It clarifies role requirements, finds strengths and gaps, guides training and succession, and aligns employee capabilities with organisational objectives.
### Steps in competency mapping
5. **Job study:** Identify the role and study its duties, responsibilities and expected results.
6. **Competency identification:** List the knowledge, skills, abilities and behaviours required, giving a competency profile.
7. **Proficiency definition:** Define each competency and describe the basic, intermediate and advanced levels.
8. **Assessment:** Assess the employee through interviews, tests, observation, work samples or performance evidence.
9. **Gap analysis:** Compare the current level with the required level and prioritise the gaps. Example: Excel required advanced, employee basic → advanced Excel training.
10. **Development planning and review:** Prepare an individual development plan of training, coaching, mentoring or job experience, and update the map when job needs change.
