# 2 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Define Human Resource Development (HRD). [PYQ] [College]
**Human Resource Development (HRD)** is a planned and continuous process of developing employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and values so that they contribute effectively to organisational objectives and achieve personal growth. In simple words, HRD means improving the abilities and potential of employees for better performance.

## Q2. What are the outcomes of HRD? [PYQ]
**Outcomes of HRD** are the results that the development of people brings. Nationally they are a skilled workforce, employment and economic development; in the organisation they are competent employees, better quality, leaders, satisfaction, lower turnover and effectiveness.

## Q3. Mention any two qualities of an HRD professional. [PYQ]
1. **Communication and interpersonal skills:** An HRD professional must explain, listen and build good relations with employees at every level.
2. **Coaching and training ability:** He must be able to train, coach and guide employees to learn new skills and grow.

## Q4. What is meant by HRD climate? [PYQ]
**HRD climate** is the employees' perception of whether their organisation provides an environment that supports learning, development, openness, trust and growth. It is shaped by the general climate, the OCTAPAC culture and the HRD mechanisms of the organisation.

## Q5. State any two external factors influencing employee behaviour. [PYQ]
1. **Leadership:** The style of the manager or leader strongly influences how employees think and act.
2. **Reward systems:** Employees behave according to the rewards and recognition the organisation gives.

## Q6. What is aptitude? [PYQ]
**Aptitude** is a person's natural ability or potential to learn and perform a particular kind of work, such as customer handling, teamwork or problem-solving. It shows how well a person can perform after training, not only what he already knows.

## Q7. Give the meaning of leadership development. [PYQ]
**Leadership development** is the planned process of building the leadership qualities of employees, such as decision-making, communication, team handling and strategic thinking, so that they are ready for managerial roles. It uses training, coaching, mentoring, job rotation and leadership projects.

## Q8. What is an HRD audit? [PYQ]
**HRD audit** is a systematic review of an organisation's HRD strategies, systems, practices and competencies to find out how well they support business goals. It shows the strengths, gaps and areas where HRD must improve.

## Q9. What is meant by outbound training? [PYQ]
**Outbound training** is learning through structured activities outside the usual workplace, often outdoors, such as team challenges and problem-solving tasks. It develops teamwork, leadership, communication, trust and problem-solving.

## Q10. Mention any two recent trends in HRD. [PYQ]
1. **Continuous learning and reskilling:** Employees keep learning new skills through digital learning platforms and technology-supported training.
2. **Competency-based HRD:** Organisations use competency frameworks and competency mapping to plan training, careers and promotions.

## Q11. What is mentoring? [PYQ]
**Mentoring** is a developmental relationship in which a more experienced person (the mentor) gives guidance, advice, support and knowledge to a less experienced person (the mentee) for wider professional and career development.

## Q12. What is competency mapping? [PYQ]
**Competency mapping** is the process of identifying the competencies required for a particular job and comparing them with the competencies an employee currently has. It shows the gap between required and existing capabilities, for example a job needing advanced Excel when the employee has only basic Excel.

## Q13. What is Human Resource Planning?  [College]
**Human Resource Planning (HRP)** is the process of estimating future human resource requirements and ensuring that the right people are available at the right place and time. In short, it means the right person, in the right job, at the right time and in the right number.

## Q14. What is potential appraisal?  [College]
**Potential appraisal** is a systematic process of evaluating an employee's future capability, leadership qualities and competencies to take up higher-level responsibilities. It asks "what can the employee achieve?" and supports promotion, succession planning and career development.

## Q15. What is an assessment centre?  [College]
An **assessment centre** is a systematic method of evaluating employees' competencies through simulated job situations such as group discussion, role play, case study, presentation, psychometric tests and in-basket exercises. It is used for selection, promotion, potential appraisal and leadership development.

## Q16. Mention any two HRD mechanisms.  [College]
1. **Performance appraisal:** The systematic assessment of an employee's performance against set standards, which also identifies training needs.
2. **Training and development:** Training improves skills for the present job; development prepares the employee for future roles.

## Q17. What is organisational climate?  [College]
**Organisational climate** is the employees' shared perception of the present work environment, such as openness, trust, support, fairness and freedom. It is "how the organisation feels" to its members, and it can change faster than culture.

## Q18. What is meant by a learning style?  [College]
A **learning style** is a person's preferred way of taking in and processing information while learning, such as visual, auditory, reading/writing or kinesthetic (learning by doing). Trainers should use a mix of methods rather than label employees by one style.

## Q19. What is Aptitude, Knowledge and Values?  [College]
**Aptitude** is a person's natural ability or potential to learn a particular skill or do a specific type of work. **Knowledge** is the information, facts and understanding gained through education, training, reading and experience. **Values** are the basic beliefs and principles that guide a person's behaviour and decisions about what is right and wrong.

## Q20. What is human resource accounting?  [College]
**Human resource accounting (HRA)** is the process of identifying, measuring and reporting the cost and value of an organisation's human resources. It treats spending on recruitment, selection, induction, training and development as an investment in people (for example Rs. 40,000 per employee).

## Q21. What is an HR audit?  [College]
An **HR audit** is a systematic examination and evaluation of an organisation's HR policies, procedures, practices and systems. It checks whether HR activities such as recruitment, training, pay, appraisal and records are managed properly and whether the applicable rules and laws are followed.

## Q22. State any two differences between training and development.  [College]
| Basis | Training | Development |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Present job | Future roles and overall growth |
| Time period | Usually short or medium term | Usually long term and continuous |

## Q23. What is a balanced scorecard?  [College]
A **balanced scorecard** is a strategic performance management framework that turns an organisation's strategy into objectives and measures from four perspectives: financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth. In HRD it links employee learning with processes, customers and financial results.

# 5 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Explain the developmental perspective of HRD. [PYQ]
1. **Employees as assets with potential:** The developmental perspective views employees as people whose knowledge, skills and potential can be continuously developed.
2. **Continuous learning:** Learning is a lifelong process; employees keep learning new skills to meet changes in work and technology.
3. **Skill, competency and career growth:** It builds the competencies needed for the present job and plans a career path for future growth.
4. **Leadership development:** It identifies and prepares employees for future managerial and leadership roles.
5. **Organisational learning and long-term growth:** Individual learning spreads across the organisation, so both the employee and the organisation grow in the long run.

## Q2. Briefly explain the importance of HRD in the present context. [PYQ]
1. **Improves employee performance:** Training raises the skills, productivity and quality of work.
2. **Adapts employees to technological change:** HRD helps employees learn new technology and digital tools.
3. **Develops leadership capabilities:** It builds a pipeline of future leaders from within the organisation.
4. **Increases motivation, engagement and retention:** Employees who see growth and career development feel valued and are less likely to leave.
5. **Encourages innovation and competitive advantage:** Skilled and learning employees bring new ideas, which keep the organisation ahead of competitors.

## Q3. Write a short note on HRD mechanisms. [PYQ]
1. **Performance appraisal:** The systematic assessment of an employee's performance against set standards; it identifies strengths, weaknesses and training needs.
2. **Potential appraisal:** It evaluates an employee's capacity to take up higher-level or future responsibilities, and supports promotion and succession.
3. **Training and development:** Training improves skills for the present job; development prepares the employee for future roles.
4. **Feedback and counselling:** Feedback tells employees how their performance compares with standards; counselling helps them solve performance and career problems.
5. **Career planning and job rotation:** Career planning maps the employee's career path, and job rotation moves him through different jobs to broaden his experience.

## Q4. Explain the learning principles in HRD. [PYQ]
1. **Motivation:** People learn better when they have a reason to learn, for example to get promoted.
2. **Readiness:** The learner must be mentally and physically ready; a tired or uninterested employee learns little.
3. **Practice and active participation:** Repeated practice and taking part through cases, discussions and role plays make learning stronger than a lecture alone.
4. **Feedback and reinforcement:** Learners need to know what they did right and wrong, and behaviour that is praised or rewarded is repeated.
5. **Meaningfulness and transfer:** Learning should be linked to real work, and what is learned must be applied on the job; only then does performance improve.

## Q5. What are the qualities of a good leader? [PYQ]
1. **Communication and vision:** A good leader explains ideas and instructions clearly and gives the team a clear direction.
2. **Decision-making and problem-solving:** Makes suitable decisions at the right time, identifies problems and finds solutions.
3. **Confidence and responsibility:** Is confident yet open to advice, and accepts accountability for results.
4. **Honesty:** Acts truthfully and ethically, so followers trust the leader.
5. **Motivation and emotional intelligence:** Encourages employees to perform well, and understands and manages emotions.

## Q6. Explain the concept of an HRD audit. [PYQ]
1. **Meaning:** An HR audit is a systematic examination and evaluation of an organisation's HR policies, procedures, practices and systems.
2. **Purpose:** It checks whether HR activities are managed properly and whether the applicable rules and laws are followed.
3. **Areas covered:** Recruitment and selection, training, compensation, performance appraisal, employee welfare, HR records and legal compliance.
4. **Steps:** Planning, collecting information, examination, identifying problems, preparing the report and corrective action.
5. **Importance:** It improves HR efficiency, finds weaknesses, supports compliance and reduces unnecessary costs.

## Q7. What are the techniques of training? [PYQ]
1. **On-the-job training and demonstration:** Workers learn while doing the actual job under guidance, and the trainer shows the correct way to operate equipment or do a task.
2. **Job instruction, apprenticeship and simulation:** Work is explained, shown, practised and reviewed step by step; apprenticeship combines practical work with structured instruction; simulation gives practice in a setting like the real job.
3. **Role play, case study and group discussion:** Supervisors practise giving feedback and resolving disputes, analyse employee and production problems, and learn to coordinate people.
4. **Coaching and job rotation:** Employees receive guidance from experienced people and gain experience by working in different areas.
5. **Management development techniques:** Managers learn through management development programmes, business games, case analysis, management by projects, executive coaching and mentoring, and conferences and seminars.

## Q8. Explain the role of coaching and mentoring in HRD. [PYQ]
1. **Coaching:** A structured process in which a coach helps an employee improve specific skills or reach defined performance goals through questions, guidance, practice and feedback.
2. **Role of coaching:** It encourages self-reflection and practical action, tracks progress, and improves confidence, performance and problem-solving. Example: a sales manager coaches a sales executive on customer objections.
3. **Mentoring:** A developmental relationship in which an experienced mentor gives guidance, advice and support to a less experienced mentee for long-term career development.
4. **Role of mentoring:** It supports confidence, career planning and understanding of the organisation, and shares knowledge between experienced and new employees.
5. **Role in HRD:** Both are methods of leadership development; they help employees grow faster, build commitment and reduce early turnover.

## Q9. Trace the evolution of HRD from personnel management.  [College]
1. **Personnel management (first stage):** The traditional, administrative stage; its focus was record keeping, wages, discipline and welfare, and its aim was to maintain employees.
2. **Human resource management (second stage):** The managerial stage; employees were seen as a resource to be managed and utilised for present performance.
3. **Human resource development (third stage):** The developmental stage; the focus is on developing the knowledge, skills, attitudes and potential of employees.
4. **Change in focus:** Administration (PM) → managing people (HRM) → developing people (HRD).
5. **Change in orientation:** Short-term maintenance (PM) → present performance (HRM) → long-term growth of the employee and the organisation (HRD).

## Q10. Explain the subsystems of HRD.  [College]
1. **Training and development:** Improves knowledge and skills for the present job and prepares employees for future roles.
2. **Performance and potential appraisal:** Performance appraisal evaluates present performance; potential appraisal identifies future capability for higher posts.
3. **Career planning and succession planning:** Maps the employee's career path and prepares employees to fill key positions in future.
4. **Employee counselling and feedback:** Helps employees with work-related and personal concerns and tells them how to improve.
5. **Rewards, recognition and organisation development:** Rewards encourage the desired performance, and OD builds a culture of openness, trust and teamwork.

## Q11. Explain the external and internal factors influencing employee behaviour.  [College]
1. **Internal – personality and perception:** Personality decides how a person reacts to situations, and perception decides how they interpret events.
2. **Internal – attitude, motivation and values:** A positive attitude, strong motivation and values such as honesty and fairness lead to better behaviour.
3. **Internal – ability and learning:** The capacity to do the task and past experience shape how an employee behaves.
4. **External – leadership, culture and climate:** The leader's style and the organisation's culture and climate strongly influence how employees act.
5. **External – rewards, job design, group, policies and technology:** Rewards, the nature of the job, co-workers, fair policies, technology and the economic environment also shape behaviour.

## Q12. Explain the principles of learning and how learning can be maximised.  [College]
1. **Motivation and readiness:** People learn better when they have a reason to learn and are mentally and physically ready.
2. **Practice and active participation:** Repeated practice and taking part through cases, discussions and role plays make learning stronger than a lecture alone.
3. **Feedback and reinforcement:** Learners need to know what they did right and wrong, and behaviour that is rewarded is repeated.
4. **Meaningfulness and transfer:** Learning must be linked to real work and applied on the job; only then does performance improve.
5. **Maximising learning:** Set clear objectives, keep content relevant and difficulty right, give practice and repetition, a supportive environment, follow-up after training and evaluation of results.

## Q13. Explain the process of training need analysis.  [College]
1. **Identify the problem:** Training need analysis is the systematic process of finding the gap between the skills employees have and the skills their jobs need; it starts with the organisational problem or performance objective.
2. **Collect information:** Through observation, interviews, questionnaires, performance appraisal reports, customer complaints and skill tests.
3. **Study three levels:** Organisational analysis (goals, strategy, resources), task or job analysis (duties and skills required) and individual analysis (each employee's present performance).
4. **Compare and find the cause:** Compare existing skills with required skills and confirm that training can solve the gap; lack of equipment or unrealistic targets need a different solution.
5. **Set priorities and recommend:** Set training priorities and objectives, and recommend the content, method, participants and schedule.

## Q14. Write short notes on (a) outbound training and (b) attitudinal training.  [College]
### (a) Outbound training
1. **Meaning:** Outbound training is learning through structured activities outside the usual workplace, often in an outdoor or unfamiliar setting.
2. **Activities:** Team-building games, group challenges, obstacle courses with proper safety, treasure hunts and outdoor projects, followed by reflection and discussion.
3. **Objectives:** It develops teamwork, leadership, trust, communication, planning, problem-solving and confidence; it is not just an outing, and needs planned objectives and supervision.
### (b) Attitudinal training
4. **Meaning:** Attitudinal training develops positive work-related attitudes, values and behaviour towards work, customers, colleagues and change. Example: employees who are rude to customers learn empathy, patience and service attitude.
5. **Methods and importance:** Role play, case studies, group discussion, behavioural modelling, self-assessment with feedback, and counselling; it improves discipline, customer service, morale and cooperation.

## Q15. Discuss the recent trends in HRD, with special reference to competency mapping.  [College]
1. **Continuous and digital learning:** Reskilling, upskilling and digital learning platforms keep employees up to date.
2. **Coaching, mentoring and data:** Individual guidance, research and analytics are used to plan HRD, and training is measured by business impact.
3. **Competency mapping:** The process of identifying the competencies a job requires and comparing them with the competencies the employee currently has.
4. **Steps in competency mapping:** Study the role, identify the knowledge, skills, abilities and behaviours needed, define proficiency levels, assess the employee and compare to find gaps.
5. **Use of competency mapping:** The gaps lead to an individual development plan of training, coaching, mentoring or work experience. Example: an employee with basic Excel in a job that needs advanced Excel gets advanced Excel training.

# 10 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Explain the subsystems of HRD with examples. [PYQ]
### Planning and appraisal subsystems
1. **Human resource planning:** Forecasting the number and kind of employees needed and making them available at the right time. Example: a bank plans the staff needed for 20 new branches.
2. **Performance appraisal:** Assessing an employee's present performance against standards. Example: a salesperson is rated against his target and his weak presentation skill is identified.
3. **Potential appraisal:** Assessing an employee's capacity for future, higher roles. Example: a good sales executive is assessed through an assessment centre for the sales manager post.
4. **Feedback and performance coaching:** Telling employees how they are doing and helping them improve. Example: a manager guides an employee to structure his reports better.
5. **Career planning:** Mapping a career path and the competencies needed for it. Example: executive → supervisor → branch manager, with training at each step.
### Development and support subsystems
6. **Training and development:** Building skills for the present job and the future. Example: training staff on new CRM software, and a leadership programme for future managers.
7. **Succession planning:** Preparing employees to fill key posts when they fall vacant. Example: a deputy manager is groomed to replace the retiring plant head.
8. **Organisation development:** Planned efforts to improve the culture, teamwork and openness of the whole organisation. Example: team-building workshops that build trust and collaboration (OCTAPAC).
9. **Rewards and recognition:** Rewarding desired behaviour and good performance. Example: an 'employee of the month' award, incentives and promotion.
10. **Employee welfare, counselling and HR information system:** Looking after the well-being of employees and keeping HR records that support decisions. Example: health check-ups, counselling services and a digital employee database.

## Q2. Discuss the influence of employee behaviour on organisational effectiveness. [PYQ]
### How employee behaviour affects effectiveness
1. **Work performance and productivity:** Committed and skilled behaviour gives more output and better quality, which directly raises organisational effectiveness.
2. **Attendance and punctuality:** Regular attendance keeps work on time; absenteeism and late coming reduce output and raise costs.
3. **Cooperation and teamwork:** When employees cooperate across departments, work flows smoothly and goals are reached faster.
4. **Communication:** Open and clear communication reduces mistakes and conflicts and speeds up decisions.
5. **Adaptability to change:** Employees who accept new technology and methods help the organisation grow; resistance slows it down.
6. **Organisational citizenship behaviour:** Employees who do more than their duty, help colleagues and protect the company's name build a strong organisation.
7. **Customer orientation and responsiveness:** Employees who take ownership solve customer complaints quickly, which raises customer satisfaction and sales.
### Factors and the role of HRD
8. **Negative behaviour harms effectiveness:** Poor attitude, conflict, fear of blame and frequent job-leaving raise costs and lower performance; high turnover means a constant loss of trained staff.
9. **Behaviour depends on internal and external factors:** Internal factors are personality, perception, attitude, motivation, ability and values; external factors are leadership, culture, climate, rewards, job design and policies.
10. **HRD improves behaviour and results:** Training, feedback, a good HRD climate and fair rewards change behaviour, which leads to better performance and organisational effectiveness. Example: after an HRD programme at a retail company, turnover fell from 24% to 17% and customer satisfaction rose from 76% to 84%.

## Q3. Explain the methods and practices for developing human capacity. [PYQ]
### Developing human capacity
1. **Aptitude-based recruitment:** Selecting people not only for qualifications but also for aptitude, such as customer handling, teamwork, adaptability and problem-solving.
2. **Knowledge development:** Product, process and system training (for example CRM training) so that employees know their work fully and can apply it.
3. **Values and ethics:** Workshops on integrity, honesty, fairness, accountability and customer orientation so that targets are not met by unethical means.
4. **Skills of human relations:** Training in communication, listening, empathy, teamwork, negotiation, conflict resolution and emotional intelligence.
5. **Responsiveness:** Giving employees authority and a no-blame climate so that they take ownership and solve problems quickly.
### Practices that support it
6. **Loyalty and commitment:** Recognition, career growth, fair pay and mentoring make employees stay and give their best.
7. **Transparency:** Clear appraisal scores, promotion criteria and reward rules build trust and a feeling of fairness.
8. **Leadership development:** Coaching, mentoring, job rotation and leadership projects prepare employees to be promoted from within.
9. **Coaching, mentoring and feedback:** Regular guidance from seniors and managers helps employees correct mistakes and grow faster.
10. **Evaluating HRD:** HR accounting, HR audit, benchmarking, impact assessment and ROI show whether HRD improves business results.

## Q4. Explain the types of training. [PYQ]
### Internal and external training
1. **Internal training:** Organised within the organisation by its own managers, supervisors or trainers. Example: a senior accountant trains new accounts assistants in company procedures.
2. **Merits and limits of internal training:** It fits the organisation's actual work, costs less and has flexible schedules; but internal trainers may lack specialist knowledge and work may be interrupted.
3. **External training:** Provided by an outside institution, professional trainer, consultancy, college or training centre. Example: HR executives attend a labour-law update programme.
4. **Merits and limits of external training:** It gives specialist trainers, updated knowledge and exposure to other ideas; but fees and travel are high and the content may not match the organisation's needs.
### Outbound and attitudinal training
5. **Outbound training:** Structured activities outside the workplace, such as team challenges, obstacle courses with safety and treasure hunts, followed by reflection and discussion.
6. **Objectives of outbound training:** It develops teamwork, leadership, trust, communication, planning and problem-solving; it is not just an outing.
7. **Attitudinal training:** Develops positive attitudes, values and behaviour towards work, customers, colleagues and change, through role play, case studies, behavioural modelling and counselling.
### Other types of training
8. **Induction training:** Introduces new employees to the organisation, its rules, people and workplace.
9. **Job, technical and safety training:** Teaches the skills for a particular job, and safe work practices and emergency procedures.
10. **Soft-skills, managerial, digital and compliance training:** Builds communication and teamwork, supervisory and leadership ability, the use of new software, and knowledge of rules and professional conduct.

## Q5. Critically examine the recent trends in HRD. [PYQ]
### Recent trends
1. **Continuous learning and reskilling:** Employees keep learning; reskilling prepares them for a new role and upskilling improves skills for the present role.
2. **Technology-enabled learning:** Learning management systems, e-learning, mobile learning, simulations and blended learning.
3. **Coaching and mentoring:** Individual guidance for specific skills and for long-term career growth.
4. **Competency-based HRD:** Competency frameworks and competency mapping guide recruitment, training, promotion and career development.
5. **Research, data and measurement:** HRD research and analytics identify needs, and outcomes are measured through ROI and the balanced scorecard.
6. **Engagement, wellbeing and positive change:** More attention to engagement, inclusion, culture and strength-based approaches such as appreciative inquiry.
7. **Future direction:** Personalised learning, digital and problem-solving skills, and closer alignment of HRD strategy with organisational strategy.
### Critical view
8. **Technology problems:** Uneven digital literacy, the need for devices and internet, less direct interaction and the protection of employee data.
9. **Poor utilisation:** Training is wasted when employees lack tools, time, support or a chance to practise, or when the content is not relevant to the job.
10. **Careful adaptation:** A practice from another country or company may not suit local culture, language, laws and conditions; competency mapping must use real evidence and not be a one-time exercise.

## Q6. Define HRD. Explain HRD at the macro and micro levels, and the qualities and competencies required of an HRD professional.  [College]
### Meaning and levels of HRD
1. **Definition:** HRD is a planned and continuous process of developing employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and values for organisational objectives and personal growth.
2. **Macro-level HRD:** The development of human resources at the national or societal level, through government and social initiatives.
3. **Macro-level activities:** Education and skill development, vocational training, employment generation and digital literacy, leading to national economic development.
4. **Micro-level HRD:** The development of employees within an organisation, through company initiatives.
5. **Micro-level activities:** Training and development, performance appraisal, career planning, leadership development and counselling, leading to organisational effectiveness.
### Qualities and competencies of an HRD professional
6. **Communication and interpersonal skills:** Explains clearly, listens and builds good relations at every level.
7. **Leadership, decision-making and problem-solving:** Guides people, takes sound decisions and handles problems calmly.
8. **Ethics, integrity and emotional intelligence:** Is fair and honest and understands people's feelings.
9. **Strategic thinking and change management:** Links HRD to business goals and helps the organisation accept change.
10. **Coaching, training and performance management:** Acts as trainer, coach, mentor, facilitator, counsellor, performance evaluator and change agent.

## Q7. Explain the HRD mechanisms. Discuss the role of organisational climate and culture in HRD.  [College]
### HRD mechanisms
1. **Performance appraisal:** Assesses performance against standards and identifies strengths, gaps and training needs.
2. **Potential appraisal:** Assesses the capacity for future, higher roles. Example: can a good sales executive become a sales manager?
3. **Training and development:** Training builds present competence; development builds future capability.
4. **Feedback and counselling:** Specific, timely and constructive feedback; counselling aimed at improvement, not punishment.
5. **Career planning, job rotation, coaching and rewards:** Career paths, movement across departments, guidance from seniors and recognition of good work.
### Role of organisational climate and culture
6. **Meaning:** Climate is how the organisation feels to employees now; culture is the deeper values, beliefs and norms that explain why it behaves that way.
7. **HRD climate:** The employees' perception that the organisation supports learning, openness, trust and growth; it is built by top management support, fair personnel policies and line manager support.
8. **OCTAPAC culture:** Openness, Confrontation, Trust, Autonomy, Proactivity, Authenticity and Collaboration create a developmental culture.
9. **Positive climate:** Encourages learning, innovation, initiative, cooperation, motivation, communication and commitment.
10. **Negative climate:** Causes fear, resistance to change, low motivation, poor communication and turnover, so HRD efforts fail.

## Q8. Explain the concept of developing human capacity with reference to aptitude, knowledge, values and skills of human relations. Discuss leadership development.  [College]
### Developing human capacity
1. **Meaning:** Developing human capacity means building the aptitude, knowledge, values and human relations skills of employees so that they can perform and grow.
2. **Aptitude:** A person's natural ability to learn and do a kind of work; recruitment should test customer handling, teamwork, adaptability and problem-solving, not only qualifications.
3. **Knowledge:** Product, process and system knowledge (for example CRM training) that employees must know and apply on the job.
4. **Values:** Integrity, honesty, fairness, accountability and customer orientation, built through ethics workshops so that targets are not met by unethical means.
5. **Skills of human relations:** Communication, listening, empathy, teamwork, negotiation, conflict resolution and emotional intelligence.
6. **Responsiveness, loyalty and transparency:** Ownership without fear of blame; recognition, career growth and mentoring for commitment; clear appraisal, promotion and reward rules for trust.
### Leadership development
7. **Meaning:** The planned process of building leadership qualities such as decision-making, communication, team handling and strategic thinking, to prepare employees for managerial roles.
8. **Need:** Without it, few employees are ready to be promoted and managers must be hired from outside.
9. **Methods:** Leadership training, mentoring, coaching, job rotation, delegation of responsibility with authority, action learning on real problems and the case study method.
10. **Benefits:** A pipeline of future leaders, more internal promotions, higher motivation and better retention. Example: internal promotions rose from 18% to 31% at a retail company after an HRD programme.

## Q9. Explain HR accounting, HR audit and benchmarking as tools for evaluating HRD, and discuss the impact of HRD initiatives on the bottom line of an organisation.  [College]
### Tools for evaluating HRD
1. **Human resource accounting (HRA):** Identifying, measuring and reporting the costs and value of an organisation's human resources. Example (historical cost): recruitment Rs. 10,000 + selection Rs. 5,000 + training Rs. 15,000 + development Rs. 10,000 = Rs. 40,000.
2. **Methods and use of HRA:** Historical cost, replacement cost and opportunity cost methods; HRA shows the investment in people and supports manpower decisions, though human value is hard to measure in money.
3. **HR audit:** A systematic examination of HR policies, procedures, practices and systems, covering recruitment, training, compensation, appraisal, welfare, HR records and legal compliance.
4. **Steps in an HR audit:** Planning, collecting information, examination, identifying problems, preparing the report and corrective action; it improves efficiency and finds weaknesses.
5. **Benchmarking:** Comparing practices and performance with other organisations or recognised standards. Example: if one company takes 30 days to recruit and another takes 15, the first studies the second's process.
6. **Types and steps of benchmarking:** Internal, competitive, functional and generic; identify the area, select a partner, collect data, compare, find gaps, implement and monitor.
### Impact of HRD on the bottom line
7. **Meaning:** Impact assessment measures the results of HRD activities; the bottom line is the organisation's final financial result, mainly net profit.
8. **Productivity, cost and quality:** Trained employees produce more, make fewer errors and less wastage, and give better products and service.
9. **Retention, customers and sales:** Career development lowers turnover and replacement cost; better service builds customer loyalty; product knowledge and selling skills raise revenue.
10. **Measuring the impact:** ROI = (Benefit − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100. Example: benefit Rs. 18 lakh on a cost of Rs. 12 lakh gives ROI = **50%**.

## Q10. Explain the principles involved in the selection of a training method and the techniques of training at different levels. How is training effectiveness evaluated?  [College]
### Principles of selecting a training method
1. **Objectives and nature of the job:** The method must achieve the learning outcome; practical jobs need demonstration and practice, conceptual subjects need discussion or case studies.
2. **Participants and group size:** Consider the trainees' age, education, experience and knowledge; a lecture suits a large group and coaching suits an individual.
3. **Cost, time and trainer:** Consider fees, materials and time away from work, and make sure the trainer can use the method well.
4. **Practice, transfer and evaluation:** Give practice with feedback, help employees apply learning at work, and choose a method whose results can be assessed.
### Techniques at different levels
5. **Workers:** On-the-job training, demonstration, job instruction training, apprenticeship and simulation. Example: learning machine operation from a skilled operator.
6. **Supervisors:** Role play, case study, group discussion, coaching skills training and job rotation. Example: a new supervisor learns to allocate duties and handle team conflict.
7. **Middle and senior managers:** Management development programmes, business games, case analysis, management by projects, executive coaching and mentoring, and seminars.
### Evaluating training effectiveness
8. **Four levels:** Reaction (did they find it useful?), learning (did knowledge and skill improve?), behaviour (is it applied at work?) and results (did complaints, errors or delays fall?).
9. **Methods:** Feedback forms, pre- and post-training tests, skill assessments, supervisor observation, performance and productivity records, and customer feedback.
10. **Steps:** Set objectives, decide the evidence, take a baseline before training, measure reaction and learning, observe application at work, review results and recommend improvements.
