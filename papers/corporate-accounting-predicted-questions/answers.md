# CORPORATE ACCOUNTING PREDICTED QUESTIONS — ANSWERS

# 2 MARKS

## Q1. Give the meaning of underwriting of shares.
**Underwriting of shares** is a contract in which underwriters agree, for a commission, to take up the shares that the public does not subscribe. It assures the company that the whole issue will be taken up.

## Q2. What is firm underwriting?
**Firm underwriting** is a definite commitment by an underwriter to take a fixed number of shares, whether or not the public subscribes for them. Those shares are his liability in addition to his share of any shortfall in the public issue.

## Q3. What is partial underwriting?
**Partial underwriting** is when only a part of the issue is underwritten. The company itself is treated as an underwriter for the part not underwritten, and bears that shortfall.

## Q4. What do you mean by profit prior to incorporation?
**Profit prior to incorporation** is the profit earned between the date a running business is purchased and the date the company is legally incorporated. A company cannot legally earn business profit before registration, so it is a **capital profit**.

## Q5. How do you treat pre and post incorporation profit?
1. **Pre-incorporation profit:** A capital profit.
2. **Post-incorporation profit:** A revenue profit.

## Q6. What do you mean by valuation of shares?
**Valuation of shares** is the process of finding the real worth of a company's share. It is done when shares are not quoted, or when the market price does not show the true value, for example on amalgamation, conversion of shares, or a purchase of a block of shares.

## Q7. What is goodwill?
**Goodwill** is the extra earning power of a business over and above the normal return on the capital employed. It is an intangible asset that arises from reputation, location, efficient management and customer connections.

## Q8. Write any four features of goodwill.
1. **Intangible:** Goodwill has no physical form.
2. **Not separable:** It can be sold only along with the business, not by itself.
3. **Fluctuating value:** Its value changes with the earning capacity of the business and does not fall merely by use.
4. **Helps to earn super profit:** It is the reason the business earns more than the normal return.

## Q9. What is super profit?
**Super profit** is the excess of the average profit over the normal profit. Normal profit is capital employed multiplied by the normal rate of return.

## Q10. Notes for: (i) bad debts, (ii) employer's PF contribution.
1. **Bad debts** are recorded under **Other expenses**.
2. **Employer's contribution to provident fund** is recorded under **Employee benefits expense**.

## Q11. Who is a liquidator?
A **liquidator** (Company Liquidator) is the person appointed to wind up a company. He realises the assets, pays the costs and the creditors in the legal order, and distributes any surplus to the shareholders.

## Q12. Mention any four preferential creditors.
1. **Government dues:** taxes, cesses and rates that became due and payable within 12 months before winding up.
2. **Wages and salary** of employees for not more than 4 months, within the prescribed limit.
3. **Accrued holiday remuneration** payable to employees.
4. **Provident fund, pension, gratuity and ESI** contributions payable by the company as employer.

## Q13. Who is an underwriter?
An **underwriter** is a person or institution (a merchant banker, stock broker, bank or financial institution) who guarantees the sale of a company's shares or debentures. If the public does not take them up, the underwriter buys the unsubscribed part himself, for a commission.

## Q14. What is complete underwriting?
**Complete underwriting** is when the whole issue of shares or debentures is underwritten, by one underwriter or by several together. The company is sure of full subscription.

## Q15. What is underwriting commission?
**Underwriting commission** is the payment made to underwriters for their guarantee. It is paid on the issue price of all the shares underwritten, whether or not they have to take up any shares.

## Q16. What are marked and unmarked applications?
1. **Marked applications:** Applications that carry the stamp of a particular underwriter.
2. **Unmarked applications:** Applications that carry no stamp.

## Q17. What is sub-underwriting?
**Sub-underwriting** is when an underwriter passes part of his own underwriting risk to other persons, called sub-underwriters. He pays them a part of his commission; the part he keeps for himself is called over-riding commission.

## Q18. What is joint underwriting?
**Joint underwriting** is when two or more underwriters together underwrite one issue in an agreed ratio. Each one is liable only for his own share of the shortfall.

## Q19. What is gross liability and net liability of an underwriter?
1. **Gross liability:** The number of shares an underwriter agreed to underwrite (total issue × his ratio).
2. **Net liability:** What he finally has to take up: gross liability minus his marked applications and his share of the unmarked applications (firm underwriting added as the agreement says).

## Q20. What is surplus in underwriting, and how is it treated?
**Surplus** arises when an underwriter's marked applications are more than his gross liability. The surplus is credited to the other underwriters in the ratio of their gross liability.

## Q21. What is time ratio?
**Time ratio** is the ratio of the length of the pre-incorporation period to the post-incorporation period. It is used for fixed or standing expenses such as rent, salaries, insurance, depreciation and telephone charges.

## Q22. What is sales ratio?
**Sales ratio** is the ratio of sales in the pre-incorporation period to sales in the post-incorporation period. It is used for gross profit and variable selling expenses such as commission, bad debts, advertisement, discount allowed and carriage outwards.

## Q23. What is revised (weighted) time ratio?
**Revised time ratio** is a time ratio worked out from the date of acquisition to the date of final payment to the vendors. It is used only to apportion **interest paid to vendors** on the purchase consideration.

## Q24. How is pre-incorporation loss treated?
**Pre-incorporation loss** is a capital loss. It is debited to **Goodwill Account** (or set off against capital reserve); it is not charged to the post-incorporation profit.

## Q25. Name any four expenses charged wholly to the post-incorporation period.
1. Directors' fees and remuneration.
2. Preliminary (formation) expenses written off.
3. Debenture interest and underwriting commission.
4. Managing Director's salary.

## Q26. Name two items charged wholly to the pre-incorporation period.
1. **Partner's or vendor's salary.**
2. **Interest on the vendor's capital.**

## Q27. What are the three key accounting periods in profit prior to incorporation?
1. **Pre-incorporation period:** From the date of purchase to the date of incorporation.
2. **Post-incorporation period:** From the date of incorporation to the closing date.
3. **Vendor settlement period:** From the date of acquisition to the date the purchase consideration is finally paid.

## Q28. What is intrinsic value of a share?
**Intrinsic value** is the value of a share based on the net assets of the company. Intrinsic value per equity share = Net assets available to equity shareholders ÷ Number of equity shares.

## Q29. What is yield value of a share?
**Yield value** is the value of a share based on its expected return. Yield value = (Expected rate of return ÷ Normal rate of return) × Paid-up value of one share.

## Q30. What is fair value of a share?
**Fair value** is the average of the intrinsic value and the yield value. Fair value = (Intrinsic value + Yield value) ÷ 2.

## Q31. What is normal rate of return?
**Normal rate of return** is the rate of profit that investors normally expect in a particular industry on the capital invested. It depends on the risk of the business and the bank rate.

## Q32. What is capital employed?
**Capital employed** is the total funds used in the business. Capital employed = Total assets (excluding goodwill and fictitious assets and non-trade investments) minus outside liabilities.

## Q33. What are fictitious assets?
**Fictitious assets** are debit balances that have no real value, such as preliminary expenses, discount on issue of shares and the debit balance of P&L. They are excluded when valuing shares.

## Q34. What is meant by number of years' purchase?
**Number of years' purchase** is the number of years for which the buyer is ready to pay for the business's average or super profit as goodwill. Goodwill = Average or super profit × Number of years' purchase.

## Q35. What is the annuity method of valuing goodwill?
In the **annuity method**, super profit is treated as an annuity for a fixed number of years and discounted at the normal rate. Goodwill = Super profit × Present value of an annuity of Re. 1.

## Q36. What are financial statements?
**Financial statements** are the formal records of a company's financial position and performance. Under the Companies Act, 2013 they include the Balance Sheet, the Statement of Profit and Loss, the Cash Flow Statement, the Statement of Changes in Equity and the notes, prepared as per **Schedule III**.

## Q37. What is Schedule III?
**Schedule III** of the Companies Act, 2013 prescribes the form and contents of the Balance Sheet and the Statement of Profit and Loss. Division I applies to companies following Accounting Standards, and Divisions II and III to companies following Ind AS.

## Q38. What is the operating cycle?
The **operating cycle** is the time between buying materials for processing and receiving cash from their sale. If it cannot be identified, it is taken as **12 months**; it decides whether an item is current or non-current.

## Q39. What is meant by reserves and surplus?
**Reserves and surplus** is a head under shareholders' funds. It includes capital reserve, securities premium, general reserve, debenture redemption reserve and the balance in the Statement of Profit and Loss (a debit balance is shown as a negative figure).

## Q40. What are trade receivables and trade payables?
1. **Trade receivables:** Amounts due from customers for goods sold or services given (debtors and bills receivable).
2. **Trade payables:** Amounts due to suppliers for goods bought or services received (creditors and bills payable).

## Q41. Heads for: (i) securities premium, (ii) debenture interest.
1. **Securities premium:** Shareholders' funds → Reserves and surplus.
2. **Debenture interest:** Statement of Profit and Loss → Finance costs.

## Q42. What is liquidation (winding up)?
**Liquidation** is the process of ending the life of a company. Its assets are realised, its liabilities are paid in the legal order, any surplus goes to the shareholders, and the company is then dissolved.

## Q43. What is the liquidator's final statement of account?
The **liquidator's final statement of account** is the statement prepared by the liquidator at the end of winding up. It shows the cash received from the assets and how it was paid out to costs, creditors and shareholders in the legal order.

## Q44. How is the liquidator's remuneration calculated?
The liquidator's remuneration is usually a percentage of the **assets realised** plus a percentage of the **amount paid to unsecured creditors** (including preferential creditors). When the cash is not enough, remuneration on unsecured creditors = Cash available × Rate ÷ (100 + Rate).

## Q45. What are overriding preferential payments?
**Overriding preferential payments** are workmen's dues and the dues of secured creditors, which rank equally (pari passu). They are paid before all other debts, including the preferential creditors.

## Q46. What is adjustment of shareholders' rights?
When shares of the same class are paid up by different amounts, the liquidator makes the loss per share equal. He makes a call on the partly paid shares, or refunds the fully paid ones, so that every shareholder bears the loss equally.

## Q47. What is a contributory?
A **contributory** is a person liable to contribute to the assets of a company on its winding up, mainly the holders of partly paid shares, up to the unpaid amount on their shares.

## Q48. Who is a partly secured creditor?
A **partly secured creditor** is one whose security is worth less than his debt. He is paid the value of the security, and the balance of his debt ranks with the unsecured creditors.

## Q49. Ratio for apportioning gross profit (pre and post incorporation).
Gross profit is apportioned between the pre- and post-incorporation periods in the **sales ratio** (the ratio of sales of the two periods). Time-based expenses are apportioned in the time ratio.

## Q50. Schedule III heads: (i) calls in arrears, (ii) proposed dividend.
Under Schedule III:
1. **Calls in arrears** are deducted from Share capital (shown under Equity and liabilities).
2. **Proposed dividend** is shown as a Short-term provision under Current liabilities.

## Q51. Value of shares: net asset method and yield method.
1. **Net assets:** Take all real assets at their realisable value.
2. **Deduct:** Subtract outside liabilities and preference share capital to get net assets for equity shareholders.
3. **Intrinsic value per share** = Net assets for equity ÷ Number of equity shares.
4. **Yield method, expected rate:** Expected rate = (Profit available for equity ÷ Equity paid-up capital) × 100.
5. **Yield value** = (Expected rate ÷ Normal rate) × Paid-up value of one share.

## Q52. What are the modes of liquidation of a company?
1. **Compulsory winding up by the Tribunal:** Under Sec. 271 of the Companies Act, 2013, the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) orders winding up on a petition.
2. **Grounds for compulsory winding up:** A special resolution of the company, acts against the sovereignty and integrity of India, affairs run fraudulently, or failure to file financial statements or annual returns for five years.
3. **Voluntary liquidation:** The company itself decides to close.
4. **Members' voluntary (solvent) liquidation:** The directors declare that the company can pay all its debts, and the members pass a resolution.
5. **Liquidation on insolvency:** An insolvent company is liquidated through the IBC process for the benefit of its creditors.

## Q57. Liquidator's remuneration: 5% on assets, 4% on unsecured.
1. **On assets realised:** 5% of Rs. 5,00,000 = **Rs. 25,000**.
2. Secured creditors are paid before the unsecured and preferential creditors. Cash left = 5,00,000 minus 1,00,000 minus 25,000 = **Rs. 3,75,000**.
3. Preferential creditors Rs. 2,00,000 plus unsecured creditors Rs. 10,00,000 = Rs. 12,00,000. Only Rs. 3,75,000 is available, so they cannot be paid in full.
4. The 4% is on the amount actually distributed. If that amount is D, then D + 4% of D = 3,75,000.
5. **Remuneration on distribution** = 3,75,000 x 4/104 = **Rs. 14,423.08**. Amount paid to those creditors = Rs. 3,60,576.92.
6. **Total liquidator's remuneration** = 25,000 + 14,423.08 = **Rs. 39,423.08**.

## Q59. Explain the functions of an underwriter.
1. **Guarantee of subscription:** He promises to take up the shares the public does not subscribe.
2. **Advice to the company:** He advises on the timing, size, type and price of the issue.
3. **Marketing the issue:** He uses his network of brokers and investors to sell the shares.
4. **Financial support:** He provides funds by taking up the unsubscribed shares.
5. **Building confidence:** His name in the prospectus gives investors confidence in the issue.

## Q60. What are the advantages of underwriting?
1. **Assured minimum subscription:** The company is sure of collecting the money it needs.
2. **Expert advice:** The company gets professional guidance on the issue.
3. **Public confidence:** An issue backed by reputed underwriters attracts more investors.
4. **Wide distribution:** Shares reach investors all over the country.
5. **Saves time and effort:** The company can concentrate on its business while underwriters handle the issue.

## Q61. Explain the types of underwriting.
1. **Complete underwriting:** The whole issue is underwritten.
2. **Partial underwriting:** Only a part of the issue is underwritten; the company bears the rest.
3. **Firm underwriting:** The underwriter agrees to take a fixed number of shares regardless of public applications.
4. **Sub-underwriting:** An underwriter passes part of his risk to sub-underwriters.
5. **Joint underwriting:** Two or more underwriters share one issue in an agreed ratio.

## Q62. State the SEBI guidelines regarding underwriting.
1. **Registration:** No one can act as an underwriter without a certificate of registration from SEBI (since 2021, underwriting is done by SEBI-registered merchant bankers and stock brokers).
2. **Capital adequacy:** An underwriter must have a net worth of at least **Rs. 20 lakh**.
3. **Limit on obligations:** Total underwriting obligations must not exceed **20 times** the underwriter's net worth.
4. **Written agreement:** The agreement must state the period, the amount underwritten, the commission and how the obligation will be met.
5. **Code of conduct:** The underwriter must honour the commitment when called, take no benefit other than the agreed commission, and keep books and records for SEBI inspection.

## Q63. Explain the provisions of the Companies Act regarding underwriting commission.
1. **Authorised by Articles:** Commission can be paid only if the Articles of Association allow it.
2. **Maximum rate:** Not more than 5% of the issue price on shares and 2.5% on debentures, or the rate in the Articles, whichever is lower (Sec. 40(6)).
3. **Disclosure in prospectus:** The prospectus must show the underwriters' names, the rate and amount of commission and the number of securities underwritten.
4. **Source of payment:** Commission may be paid out of the proceeds of the issue, out of profits, or both.
5. **Only on public offers:** No commission is paid on securities not offered to the public.

## Q64. Explain the procedure for determining the liability of underwriters.
1. **Gross liability:** Find each underwriter's gross liability = total issue × his agreed ratio.
2. **Deduct marked applications:** Subtract the applications bearing each underwriter's stamp.
3. **Share unmarked applications:** Divide the unmarked applications (and firm underwriting, if treated as unmarked) in the ratio of gross liability and subtract.
4. **Distribute surplus:** If any underwriter shows a negative balance (surplus), share it among the others in the ratio of gross liability.
5. **Net liability:** The balance is each underwriter's net liability.

## Q65. Distinguish between marked and unmarked applications.
| Basis | Marked applications | Unmarked applications |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Bear the stamp of an underwriter | Bear no stamp |
| Source | Procured by a particular underwriter | Come directly from the public |
| Credit | Given to that underwriter alone | Shared by all underwriters |
| Basis of credit | Actual number marked | Ratio of gross liability |
| Effect | Reduces only that underwriter's liability | Reduces every underwriter's liability |

## Q66. Distinguish between an underwriter and a broker.
| Basis | Underwriter | Broker |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Guarantees the sale of the issue | Only finds buyers for the shares |
| Risk | Must buy the unsubscribed shares | Bears no risk |
| Payment | Commission on the whole amount underwritten | Brokerage only on shares actually sold |
| Legal limit | Max. 5% on shares, 2.5% on debentures | No such limit in the Act |
| Liability | Liable to take up the shortfall | No liability for the shortfall |

## Q67. Distinguish between pre-incorporation and post-incorporation profit.
| Basis | Pre-incorporation profit | Post-incorporation profit |
|---|---|---|
| Period | Date of purchase to date of incorporation | Date of incorporation to year end |
| Nature | Capital profit | Revenue profit |
| Dividend | Cannot be distributed | Fully available for dividend |
| Transfer | Capital Reserve A/c | P&L Appropriation A/c |
| Use | Write off goodwill, preliminary expenses, issue discounts | Normal profit from operations |

## Q68. Explain the procedure for ascertaining profit prior to incorporation.
1. **Step 1, ratios:** Calculate the time ratio, sales ratio and revised time ratio.
2. **Step 2, gross profit:** Determine the overall gross profit for the full year.
3. **Step 3, apportion:** Split every item of the P&L into pre and post columns on the proper basis.
4. **Step 4, transfer:** Transfer the pre profit to Capital Reserve and the post profit to P&L A/c.
5. **Fluctuating items:** When sales change month to month, or rent or salary changes during the year, work out each period separately instead of using a simple ratio.

## Q69. Explain the basis of apportionment of expenses and incomes.
1. **Gross profit or loss:** Sales ratio.
2. **Fixed expenses** (rent, salaries, insurance, depreciation, office and bank charges): Time ratio.
3. **Selling expenses** (advertisement, commission, bad debts, discount allowed, carriage): Sales ratio.
4. **Pre-only charges** (vendor's salary, interest on vendor's capital): 100% pre-incorporation.
5. **Post-only charges** (directors' fees, preliminary expenses, debenture interest, MD's salary): 100% post-incorporation.

## Q70. What are the methods of ascertaining profit prior to incorporation?
1. **Closing the books on the date of incorporation:** Stock is taken and a trading and P&L account is prepared for the pre period.
2. **Apportioning the year's results:** At the year end one P&L is prepared and each item is divided between pre and post periods.
3. **Time ratio:** Used for items that accrue with time.
4. **Sales ratio:** Used for gross profit and items that vary with sales.
5. **Actual basis:** Items that belong wholly to one period are charged to it in full.

## Q71. Explain the need for valuation of shares.
1. **Amalgamation and absorption:** To fix the exchange ratio of shares.
2. **Purchase of a block of shares:** When a controlling interest is bought.
3. **Unquoted shares:** Shares of private companies have no market price.
4. **Conversion:** When preference shares are converted into equity shares.
5. **Taxation and loans:** For tax assessment, and when shares are given as security for a loan.

## Q72. Explain the factors affecting valuation of shares.
1. **Earning capacity:** Present and expected future profits.
2. **Dividend record:** The rate of dividend and how regularly it is paid.
3. **Net assets:** The value of assets backing each share.
4. **Market conditions:** Demand and supply of shares and the state of the economy.
5. **Management and goodwill:** Efficient management and a good name raise the value.

## Q73. Explain the factors to be considered in valuation of goodwill.
1. **Profitability:** Higher and steady profits mean more goodwill.
2. **Location:** A convenient site attracts customers.
3. **Efficient management:** Good management raises profits.
4. **Nature of business:** A monopoly or a business with little competition has more goodwill.
5. **Capital employed and normal rate:** The capital needed and the normal return in the industry.

## Q74. Explain the nature and types of goodwill.
1. **Nature:** Goodwill is an intangible asset that brings super profit.
2. **Purchased goodwill:** Paid for when a business is bought; only this is recorded in the books.
3. **Self-generated (inherent) goodwill:** Built up inside the business over time; it is not recorded.
4. **Cat and dog goodwill:** Cat goodwill comes from customers loyal to the place.
5. **Rat and rabbit goodwill:** Rat goodwill is from customers with no loyalty.

## Q75. Provisions for preparing financial statements (Schedule III).
1. **Vertical form:** The Balance Sheet and Statement of Profit and Loss are in vertical form.
2. **Two main heads:** The Balance Sheet shows Equity and Liabilities, and Assets.
3. **Current and non-current:** Items are classified using the operating cycle or 12 months.
4. **Notes to accounts:** Details of every item are given in notes, numbered against the main statement.
5. **Comparatives and rounding:** Previous year's figures are shown, and amounts are rounded off as prescribed.

## Q76. Give the main heads of a Balance Sheet as per Schedule III.
1. **Shareholders' funds:** Share capital; reserves and surplus; money received against share warrants.
2. **Non-current liabilities:** Long-term borrowings, deferred tax liabilities, other long-term liabilities, long-term provisions.
3. **Current liabilities:** Short-term borrowings, trade payables, other current liabilities, short-term provisions.
4. **Non-current assets:** Property, plant and equipment and intangible assets, non-current investments, deferred tax assets, long-term loans and advances, other non-current assets.
5. **Current assets:** Current investments, inventories, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents, short-term loans and advances, other current assets.

## Q77. Give the format of the Statement of Profit and Loss as per Schedule III.
1. **Income:** Revenue from operations + Other income = Total income.
2. **Expenses:** Cost of materials consumed; purchases of stock-in-trade; changes in inventories; employee benefits expense; finance costs; depreciation and amortisation expense; other expenses.
3. **Profit before tax:** Total income − Total expenses, after exceptional and extraordinary items.
4. **Tax expense:** Current tax and deferred tax.
5. **Profit for the period** and **earnings per share** (basic and diluted).

## Q78. State the head or note under which these items are shown as per Schedule III.
| Item | Head / note |
|---|---|
| Securities premium | Reserves and surplus |
| 12% debentures | Long-term borrowings |
| Public deposits | Long-term (or short-term) borrowings |
| Unclaimed dividend | Other current liabilities |
| Patents | Intangible assets |

## Q79. Explain the order of payment in the liquidator's final statement of account.
1. **Secured creditors:** Paid out of their own security; only the surplus comes into the statement.
2. **Legal and liquidation expenses,** then the **liquidator's remuneration.**.
3. **Preferential creditors:** Government dues, wages and salary, PF and similar dues.

## Q80. Explain the need for liquidation of a company.

2. **Inability to pay debts:** Creditors' claims cannot be met.

## Q81. Explain the legal provisions regarding liquidation.
1. **Order of payment:** Assets are applied in the order laid down by the Act.
2. **Overriding preferential payments:** Workmen's dues rank equally with secured creditors.
3. **Preferential payments (Sec. 327):** Government dues, wages and salary for 4 months, holiday pay and PF dues come before other unsecured debts.
4. **Liquidator's remuneration:** Fixed as a percentage of assets realised and of amounts paid to creditors.
5. **Adjustment of rights:** Losses are shared equally among shareholders of the same class through calls or refunds.

## Q88. Methods of valuation of shares and goodwill, with formulas.
### Valuation of shares
1. **Net assets method (intrinsic value):** The share is worth the net assets that belong to it.
2. **Yield method:** The share is worth what its earnings yield as compared with the normal rate.
3. **Fair value method:** The simple average of the other two, used when both asset backing and yield should count.
### Valuation of goodwill
4. **Average profits method:** Average profit = Total adjusted profits ÷ Number of years.
5. **Super profits method:** Normal profit = Capital employed × Normal rate of return.
6. **Annuity method:** Super profit is treated as an annuity and discounted.

## Q93. What is underwriting? Explain the functions and types of underwriting.
**Underwriting** is a contract in which underwriters agree, for a commission, to take up the shares that the public does not subscribe.
### Functions
1. **Guarantee of subscription:** The company is sure the issue will be fully taken up.
2. **Advice:** Underwriters advise on the timing, size and price of the issue.
3. **Marketing:** They sell the shares through their network of brokers and investors.
4. **Financial support:** They provide money by buying the unsubscribed shares.
5. **Confidence:** Their reputation attracts investors to the issue.
### Types
6. **Complete underwriting:** The whole issue is underwritten.
7. **Partial underwriting:** Only a part is underwritten; the company bears the rest.
8. **Firm underwriting:** A fixed number of shares is taken regardless of public applications.
9. **Sub-underwriting:** An underwriter passes part of his risk to sub-underwriters.
10. **Joint underwriting:** Several underwriters share one issue in an agreed ratio.

## Q94. SEBI guidelines and legal provisions on underwriting commission.
### SEBI guidelines
1. **Registration:** Only a SEBI-registered person may act as underwriter (now merchant bankers and stock brokers).
2. **Net worth:** At least Rs. 20 lakh.
3. **Limit:** Total obligations not more than 20 times net worth.
4. **Agreement:** A written agreement with the period, amount, commission and arrangements.
5. **Conduct:** Honour the commitment when called; no benefit beyond the commission; keep records.
### Underwriting commission (Companies Act, 2013)
6. **Articles:** Commission must be authorised by the Articles.
7. **Rate:** Max. 5% on shares and 2.5% on debentures, or the Articles' rate, whichever is lower (Sec. 40(6)).
8. **Prospectus:** Names of underwriters, rate and amount of commission and securities underwritten are disclosed.
9. **Source:** Paid out of the issue proceeds, profits, or both.
10. **Filing:** A copy of the commission contract is filed with the Registrar.

## Q95. Profit prior to incorporation: procedure and basis of allocation.
**Profit prior to incorporation** is the profit earned from the date of purchase of a running business to the date of incorporation.
### Procedure
1. **Ratios:** Calculate the time ratio, sales ratio and revised time ratio.
2. **Gross profit:** Find the gross profit for the full year.
3. **Apportion:** Divide each P&L item into pre and post columns.
4. **Transfer:** Pre profit to Capital Reserve; post profit to P&L A/c.
5. **Fluctuating items:** Monthly sales or changed rent or salary are worked out period by period.
### Basis of allocation
6. **Gross profit:** Sales ratio.
7. **Fixed expenses:** Time ratio (rent, salaries, insurance, depreciation, office and bank charges).
8. **Selling expenses:** Sales ratio (advertisement, commission, bad debts, discount, carriage).
9. **Wholly pre:** Vendor's salary and interest on vendor's capital.
10. **Wholly post:** Directors' fees, preliminary expenses, debenture interest, underwriting commission and MD's salary.

## Q96. Financial statements: Schedule III provisions and formats.
**Financial statements** are the Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit and Loss, Cash Flow Statement, Statement of Changes in Equity and notes, prepared as per Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013.
### Provisions and Balance Sheet
1. **Vertical form:** Both statements are prepared in vertical form.
2. **Classification:** Current and non-current items are decided by the operating cycle or 12 months.
3. **Shareholders' funds:** Share capital, reserves and surplus, money against share warrants.
4. **Non-current liabilities:** Long-term borrowings, deferred tax liabilities, other long-term liabilities, long-term provisions.
5. **Current liabilities:** Short-term borrowings, trade payables, other current liabilities, short-term provisions.
6. **Assets:** Non-current (PPE, intangibles, investments, loans and advances) and current (inventories, trade receivables, cash and cash equivalents, short-term loans and advances).
### Statement of Profit and Loss
7. **Income:** Revenue from operations and other income.
8. **Expenses:** Materials, purchases, change in inventories, employee benefits, finance costs, depreciation, other expenses.
9. **Profit:** Profit before tax, tax expense, profit for the period and earnings per share.
10. **Notes and comparatives:** Every item is supported by notes, and previous year's figures are shown.

## Q97. Liquidation: modes and order of payment.
**Liquidation** is the process of ending the life of a company, in which assets are sold, debts are paid and the company is dissolved.
### Modes of liquidation
1. **Compulsory winding up:** By order of the NCLT under Sec. 271 of the Companies Act, 2013.
2. **Grounds:** Special resolution, acts against India's sovereignty, fraud, or no filings for five years.
3. **Voluntary liquidation:** By the company's own decision, under Sec. 59 of the IBC, 2016.
4. **Members' voluntary:** A solvent company that can pay all its debts.
5. **Insolvency liquidation:** An insolvent company wound up for its creditors through the IBC.
### Order of payment
6. **Secured creditors and workmen's dues:** Paid from the security; workmen rank equally.
7. **Expenses:** Legal charges, liquidation expenses and the liquidator's remuneration.
8. **Preferential creditors:** Government dues, wages, salary and PF.
9. **Debenture holders and unsecured creditors:** Floating charge debentures, then unsecured creditors.
10. **Shareholders:** Preference shareholders first, then equity shareholders.
