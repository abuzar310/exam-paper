# SECTION A — 2 MARKS

## Q1.
**Underwriting of shares** is a contract in which underwriters agree, for a commission, to take up the shares or debentures that the public does not subscribe. It assures the company that the whole issue will be taken up.

## Q2.
**Firm underwriting** is a definite commitment by an underwriter to take a fixed number of shares, whether or not the public subscribes for them. These shares are his liability in addition to his share of any shortfall.

## Q3.
A **marked application** is an application form that bears the stamp or seal of a particular underwriter. It shows that the application was procured through that underwriter and is credited to him.

## Q4.
**Profit prior to incorporation** is the profit earned from the date a running business is taken over to the date the company is incorporated. It is a capital profit, transferred to capital reserve, and cannot be paid as dividend.

## Q5.
Gross profit is apportioned between the pre- and post-incorporation periods in the **sales ratio** (the ratio of sales of the two periods). Time-based expenses are apportioned in the time ratio.

## Q6.
**Goodwill** is the extra earning power of a business over and above the normal return on its capital employed. It is an intangible asset arising from reputation, location, management and customer connections.

## Q7.
**Super profit** is the excess of the average profit over the normal profit. Normal profit = Capital employed × Normal rate of return; Super profit = Average profit − Normal profit.

## Q8.
**Yield value of a share** = (Expected rate of return ÷ Normal rate of return) × Paid-up value of one share, where Expected rate = (Profit available to equity ÷ Equity paid-up capital) × 100.

## Q9.
Under Schedule III:
1. **Calls in arrears** are deducted from Share capital (shown under Equity and liabilities).
2. **Proposed dividend** is shown as a Short-term provision under Current liabilities.

## Q10.
**Liquidation** (winding up) is the process of closing a company permanently by realising its assets, paying the costs and the creditors in the legal order, and distributing any surplus to the shareholders. It ends in the dissolution of the company.

## Q11.
**Preferential creditors** are creditors paid before the unsecured creditors, under Sec. 327 of the Companies Act, 2013 — for example government taxes due within 12 months, employees' wages for up to 4 months, and the employer's provident fund and gratuity contributions.

## Q12.
**Liquidator's remuneration** is the commission paid to the liquidator for winding up the company. It is usually a percentage on the assets realised and a percentage on the amount distributed to creditors or shareholders.

## Q8.
1. **Full underwriting:** the whole issue is underwritten, so the company is assured of the entire amount.
2. **Partial underwriting:** only a part of the issue is underwritten; the company itself underwrites the rest.
3. **Firm underwriting:** the underwriter agrees to take a fixed number of shares outright, whether or not the public subscribes.
4. **Joint underwriting:** more than one underwriter shares a large issue, each taking an agreed portion.
5. **Sub-underwriting:** a main underwriter passes on a part of his liability to sub-underwriters to spread the risk.

## Q9.
1. **Compulsory winding up (by the Tribunal):** ordered by the National Company Law Tribunal, for example when the company passes a special resolution to be wound up or acts against the interest of the state.
2. **Voluntary winding up:** decided by the members themselves by passing a resolution, without the Tribunal.
3. **Members' voluntary winding up:** when the company is solvent and a declaration of solvency is made.
4. **Creditors' voluntary winding up:** when the company is insolvent and cannot pay its debts in full.
5. **Winding up under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016:** liquidation after the corporate insolvency resolution process fails.
