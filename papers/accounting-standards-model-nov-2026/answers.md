# SECTION A – 2 MARKS

## Q1.
An **accounting standard** is a written statement of rules for recognising, measuring, presenting and disclosing transactions in the financial statements. In India the Accounting Standards Board prepares them and the Council of the ICAI issues them. A standard cannot override the law.

## Q2.
Accounting standards are set so that enterprises follow one accepted practice. Two objectives are:
1. **Uniformity:** similar transactions are recorded in one way.
2. **Comparability:** results of different enterprises, and of different years, can be compared.

## Q3.
**Net realisable value**, as per AS 2, is the estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, less the estimated costs of completion and the estimated costs necessary to make the sale. Inventory is carried at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower.

## Q4.
The cost of an item of property, plant and equipment, as per AS 10, is:
1. the purchase price, including import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, after trade discounts and rebates,
2. costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition needed for its intended use, and
3. the initial estimate of dismantling, removal and site-restoration costs.

## Q5.
AS 13 classifies investments by the period for which they are held:
1. **Current investment:** readily realisable, and intended to be held for not more than one year from the date of purchase.
2. **Long-term investment:** any investment other than a current investment.

## Q6.
A **qualifying asset**, as per AS 16, is an asset that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale. Borrowing costs that are directly attributable to such an asset are capitalised as part of its cost.

## Q7.
A **finance lease** transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership from the lessor to the lessee. An **operating lease** does not. One test is that the present value of the minimum lease payments amounts to substantially all of the fair value of the asset.

## Q8.
An **intangible asset**, as per AS 26, is an identifiable non-monetary asset without physical substance, held for use in production or supply, for rental to others, or for administration. Three features are required: identifiability, control, and probable future economic benefits.

## Q9.
A **provision**, as per AS 29, is a liability that can be measured only by using a substantial degree of estimation. It is recognised only when a present obligation arises from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable, and a reliable estimate can be made.

## Q10.
Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when the seller has transferred the property in the goods for a price. That point usually coincides with the transfer of the significant risks and rewards of ownership. If the two do not coincide, revenue is recognised when the significant risks and rewards pass to the buyer.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS

## Q1.
The ICAI constituted the Accounting Standards Board in 1977 to prepare standards. The standard-setting process is:
1. The area that needs a standard is identified.
2. A study group is formed and it prepares a draft.
3. The draft is circulated and the views of different bodies are taken.
4. An exposure draft is finalised and issued for public comments.
5. The draft is modified in the light of those comments.
6. The Council of the ICAI issues the Accounting Standard. For companies, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs notifies it.

The National Financial Reporting Authority recommends accounting standards and auditing standards for companies, and it monitors and enforces compliance with them. It can also look into the professional misconduct of auditors. A standard does not override the law. Where the law requires a different treatment, the law is followed and the departure is disclosed.

## Q1.
AS 10 requires each significant part of an asset to be depreciated separately. The body and the engine are separate components. No residual value is given, so the whole cost is depreciated on the straight-line method.

| Component | Cost (Rs. crore) | Life (years) | Depreciation a year | For 5 years | Carrying amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body | 70 | 20 | 3.50 | 17.50 | 52.50 |
| Engine | 30 | 5 | 6.00 | 30.00 | Nil |

On 31 March 2025 the old engine is fully depreciated. Its carrying amount is nil, so derecognition gives no gain or loss. The new engine meets the recognition test and is capitalised at **Rs. 45 crore**. It is then depreciated over its own useful life, which begins on 1 April 2025. The body is not disturbed. It stays at a carrying amount of **Rs. 52.50 crore**, and depreciation of Rs. 3.50 crore a year continues for the remaining 15 years.

## Q3.
**Revenue** is the inflow of cash, receivables or other consideration arising in the course of the ordinary activities of an enterprise. AS 9 deals with when that revenue is recognised, and with the effect of uncertainty about the amount or its collection. It does not apply to construction contracts, hire-purchase and lease agreements, government grants, or the revenue of insurance companies.

Revenue covered by AS 9 arises from:
1. the sale of goods,
2. the rendering of services, and
3. the use by others of enterprise resources, which yields interest, royalties and dividends.

**Sale of goods.** Revenue is recognised when the seller has transferred the property in the goods to the buyer for a price. That normally coincides with the transfer of the significant risks and rewards of ownership. Where it does not, revenue waits until those risks and rewards have passed. A delay in delivery, whether the buyer's or the seller's, or an agreement fixing the time of transfer, can separate the two events.

**Rendering of services.** Revenue is recognised as the service is performed, by one of two methods:
1. **Completed-service method:** revenue is recognised only when the service is completed or substantially completed.
2. **Proportionate-completion method:** revenue is recognised in step with the degree of completion.

**Interest, royalties and dividends.**
1. **Interest** is recognised on a time basis, taking the amount outstanding and the rate.
2. **Royalties** are recognised on an accrual basis, as the agreement provides.
3. **Dividends** are recognised when the owner's right to receive payment is established.

Revenue is recognised only when it can be measured reliably and collection is reasonably certain. If collectability becomes uncertain after recognition, the amount already recorded is not reversed. A separate provision is made instead.

## Q5.
AS 7 says that where one contract covers a number of assets, the construction of each asset is a separate construction contract when three conditions are met:
1. separate proposals have been submitted for each asset,
2. each asset has been subject to separate negotiation, so that the contractor and the customer could accept or reject that part, and
3. the costs and revenues of each asset can be identified.

All three conditions are met for the units of the industrial complex. XYZ Construction Ltd. treats the construction of each unit as a **separate construction contract**.
