# SECTION A – 2 MARKS
## Q1. (a)
**Human resource planning** is the process of forecasting how many employees an organisation will need and what skills they must have. It compares future needs with the available workforce and plans how to fill gaps.
## Q1. (b)
**Learning strategies** are deliberate methods used to learn and retain knowledge or skills. They include rehearsal, elaboration, organisation, practice, self-regulation and collaborative learning.
## Q1. (c)
**Organisational climate** is employees' shared perception of what it feels like to work in an organisation. It reflects how people experience leadership, communication, trust and support in daily work.
## Q1. (d)
1. **Employee competence:** Training improves the knowledge and skills employees use in their jobs.
2. **Higher productivity:** Skilled employees produce better work with fewer errors and less waste.
## Q1. (e)
**Benchmarking** means comparing an organisation's practices or results with those of high-performing organisations or units. The comparison reveals gaps and guides improvement.
## Q1. (f)
**Values** are lasting beliefs about what is right, important or desirable. They guide decisions and conduct at work, as when honesty leads an employee to report an error.
## Q1. (g)
**Human resource accounting** identifies, measures and reports the cost and value of an organisation's human resources. It helps managers assess investment in recruitment, training and development.
## Q1. (h)
**An HRD mechanism** is a planned method an organisation uses to develop employee capabilities. Training, performance appraisal, counselling and job rotation are examples.
## Q1. (i)
**Human relations skills** help people work well with others. They include clear communication, active listening, empathy, teamwork and conflict resolution.
## Q1. (j)
**The balanced scorecard** measures performance from four perspectives: financial, customer, internal processes, and learning and growth. It connects daily performance measures with long-term goals.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS
## Q2.
**Potential appraisal** assesses an employee's capacity to handle future roles and greater responsibility.
1. **Identify talent:** It reveals abilities that the present job gives an employee little chance to use.
2. **Plan development:** It identifies the training needed for a more demanding role.
3. **Guide careers:** It helps employees choose a career path suited to their strengths.
4. **Support promotion:** It helps managers judge readiness for higher responsibilities.
5. **Plan succession:** It identifies employees who can be prepared for key vacancies.
## Q3.
1. **Technology:** Reskilling helps employees use new tools when work methods change.
2. **Competition:** Skilled employees improve quality, reduce waste and serve customers better.
3. **Adaptability:** Continuous learning prepares employees for changing roles and markets.
4. **Retention:** Career development encourages capable employees to stay and grow.
5. **Leadership:** Coaching prepares successors to take responsibility as the organisation expands.
## Q4.
1. **Shared values:** A culture that values quality encourages staff to report defects early.
2. **Leadership example:** Employees follow the standards of fairness and conduct that managers practise.
3. **Communication:** Openness encourages staff to ask questions and raise problems promptly.
4. **Rewards:** Recognising team results encourages employees to share knowledge and cooperate.
5. **Group norms:** Everyday expectations about punctuality and responsibility shape individual habits.
## Q5.
1. **Readiness and motivation:** Employees learn better when they see why a skill matters to their work.
2. **Meaningfulness:** Linking a new idea to familiar experience makes it easier to understand.
3. **Participation and practice:** Performing a task repeatedly builds confidence and accuracy.
4. **Feedback and reinforcement:** Timely correction improves performance and recognition encourages repetition.
5. **Transfer:** Applying a new skill on the job turns training into lasting improvement.
## Q6.
1. **Policy review:** An HR audit checks whether actual practices follow stated policies.
2. **Compliance:** It identifies gaps in employment records and compliance with labour requirements.
3. **Effectiveness:** It examines whether recruitment, appraisal and training achieve their objectives.
4. **Efficiency:** It reveals waste, delays and duplication in personnel procedures.
5. **Corrective action:** Its findings help managers assign responsibility and improve HR practices.
## Q7.
1. **Communication:** Clear expression and listening prevent misunderstandings about work.
2. **Teamwork:** Cooperation helps employees share tasks and combine their strengths.
3. **Trust:** Empathy and respect make employees more willing to seek or offer help.
4. **Conflict resolution:** Listening to both sides helps settle disagreements constructively.
5. **Performance:** Supportive working relationships improve morale and coordination.

# SECTION C – 10 MARKS
## Q10.
### Micro level – within the organisation
1. **Identify needs:** Appraisal reveals the gap between present skills and job requirements.
2. **Improve performance:** Training employees in better methods raises quality and productivity.
3. **Develop careers:** Career planning and rotation prepare employees for more demanding roles.
4. **Build leadership:** Coaching and delegation prepare successors for management positions.
5. **Retain employees:** Counselling and growth opportunities strengthen commitment and reduce turnover.
### Macro level – across society
6. **Build human capital:** Education and vocational training develop a skilled national workforce.
7. **Improve employability:** Training in skills needed by industry helps people find productive work.
8. **Support technological change:** Digital literacy and reskilling help workers enter emerging occupations.
9. **Raise national productivity:** A more capable workforce supports innovation and economic growth.
10. **Improve living standards:** Wider access to learning and employment increases earning opportunities.
## Q11.
### Learning strategies
1. **Rehearsal:** Repeating the steps of a safety procedure helps an employee recall them accurately.
2. **Elaboration:** Connecting a new rule to a familiar workplace case helps explain why it matters.
3. **Organisation:** A flowchart arranges a complex task into steps that are easier to remember.
4. **Practice:** Performing the task and correcting errors turns knowledge into a usable skill.
5. **Self-regulation:** Learners set a goal, check their progress and adjust their method.
6. **Collaborative learning:** Discussion, mentoring and team projects let employees learn from others.
### Learning preferences
7. **Visual:** Diagrams and demonstrations help learners see relationships or a sequence of actions.
8. **Auditory:** Spoken explanations and discussion help learners work through ideas aloud.
9. **Reading and writing:** Manuals, checklists and written summaries help learners organise information.
10. **Kinaesthetic:** Role play and hands-on trials build skill through experience; trainers can combine these methods to suit the task.
## Q12.
**Leadership development** is the planned process of building the knowledge, skills and judgement needed to guide others.
### How leadership is developed
1. **Training:** Workshops and role plays let employees practise communication and decision-making.
2. **Mentoring:** An experienced leader guides an employee through unfamiliar responsibilities.
3. **Coaching:** Specific feedback helps an employee improve a skill such as giving instructions.
4. **Job rotation:** Working across departments develops a wider understanding of the business.
5. **Delegation:** Leading a real assignment gives an employee practice in making accountable decisions.
### How it develops human capacity
6. **Judgement:** Weighing alternatives builds the ability to solve difficult workplace problems.
7. **Human relations:** Listening, empathy and conflict resolution help people lead cooperative teams.
8. **Confidence:** Handling responsibility develops initiative and readiness for greater duties.
9. **Developing others:** Trained leaders coach team members and help them apply new skills.
10. **Succession:** A pool of capable leaders supports continuity and prepares the organisation for change.
