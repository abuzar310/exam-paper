# SECTION A – 2 MARKS

## Q1. (a)
**Personality** is the set of behaviours, thoughts, emotions and habits that make a person unique. It reflects how a person reacts, communicates, thinks and presents himself in different situations, and it is shaped by heredity, environment and situation.

## Q1. (b)
**Attitude** is a mental state involving beliefs, feelings and behaviour towards a person, object or situation. In simple words, it is how we think and feel about something, which affects how we behave; it may be positive, negative or neutral.

## Q1. (c)
**Conflict management** is the process of handling disagreements or arguments between individuals or groups in a peaceful and constructive way. It uses techniques such as compromising and collaborating to find a solution instead of blaming.

## Q1. (d)
**Leadership** is the ability to influence, guide and motivate others to achieve a common goal. A leader shows the way, supports the team and helps it succeed.

## Q1. (e)
**Group discussion (GD)** is a structured conversation in which participants express their ideas and opinions on a given topic within a time limit. It is used in placements, entrance exams and job selection to test communication, thinking and leadership skills.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS

## Q2.
1. **Attitude and values of the perceiver:** A person sees things through his own beliefs and values, so two people may judge the same event differently.
2. **Past experience:** What a person has experienced before shapes how he interprets a new person or situation.
3. **Interests, needs and expectations:** People notice what interests them or what they expect to see, and ignore the rest.
4. **Situational factors:** The time, place and environment in which something is seen affect how it is understood; a loud voice at a party and in an office are perceived differently.
5. **Target factors:** The features of the person or object observed, such as appearance, behaviour, novelty and size, influence the perception formed.

## Q3.
**Self-awareness** is the ability to understand your own personality, emotions, behaviour, strengths and weaknesses. Its benefits are:
1. **Realistic goals:** Knowing your strengths and weaknesses helps you set goals you can actually achieve.
2. **Better decisions and emotional control:** You understand your feelings, so you think before reacting and decide wisely.
3. **Stronger relationships:** Understanding yourself helps you understand others and get along well with them.
4. **Knowing how others see you:** It shows how your behaviour affects people, so you can improve your image.
5. **Self-improvement:** It helps you work on weak areas; for example, a person who gets nervous in public speaking can practise in small groups to build confidence.

## Q4.
1. **Clear vision and strong communication:** An effective leader knows the goal and explains it clearly to the team.
2. **Confidence and decision-making ability:** He takes firm and timely decisions and faces problems with confidence.
3. **Empathy and emotional intelligence:** He understands the feelings of team members and controls his own emotions.
4. **Integrity and honesty:** He is fair and truthful and keeps his word, so people trust and follow him.
5. **Ability to motivate and support others:** He encourages the team, helps members grow and gets the best out of them.

## Q5.
1. **Personal interview:** A one-on-one discussion between the candidate and one interviewer.
2. **Panel interview:** Several interviewers question one candidate together, each judging a different area.
3. **Telephonic / video interview:** The interview is conducted remotely by phone or video call, often as a first round.
4. **Group interview:** Several candidates are interviewed together to see how they behave and communicate in a group.
5. **Behavioural interview:** It focuses on the candidate's past experiences and actions, and is best answered with the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

## Q6.
**Communication** is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts and feelings between people. Its process is: sender → message → medium → receiver → feedback.
1. **Sender:** The person who starts the communication and puts the idea into words, symbols or gestures (encoding).
2. **Message:** The information, idea or feeling that is to be shared.
3. **Medium or channel:** The method used to send the message, such as spoken, written or digital.
4. **Receiver:** The person who receives the message and understands its meaning (decoding).
5. **Feedback and noise:** Feedback is the receiver's response, which completes the process; noise is any physical or emotional disturbance that spoils the message.

## Q7.
1. **Specific:** The goal must be clear and well-defined, not vague.
2. **Measurable:** Progress must be trackable, so that you know how much has been achieved.
3. **Achievable:** The goal must be realistic and possible with the time and ability you have.
4. **Relevant:** It must match your personal or career aims, so that it is worth the effort.
5. **Time-bound:** It must have a fixed deadline. Example: "I will improve my spoken English by practising 30 minutes daily for 3 months."
