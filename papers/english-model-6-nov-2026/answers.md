# PART I - SHORT NOTES

## Q1. (1)
Nikolai Gogol uses a narrative style that combines realism, humour, satire, irony, and the supernatural.

The story is written in the third person. The narrator knows the characters and describes their thoughts and feelings. Realistic details present Akaki's life and the social conditions of Russia, so the story remains believable.

Satire criticises government offices, bureaucracy, and the pride of officials. Humour is used to expose serious social problems. Irony is important: Akaki finally owns a new overcoat, but he loses it on the same day. His greatest happiness becomes the cause of his greatest sorrow.

The story ends with a supernatural element. Akaki's ghost wanders the streets, which creates mystery and stands for justice. The overcoat itself is a symbol of hope, dignity, protection, and self-respect. The language is simple, and it mixes humour with sadness.

## Q1. (2)
The attack on Mudli is a turning point in Kirubunkal. It brings fear and sadness to the village, and it changes the lives of his family and neighbours.

Seena starts a secret romance with Vimli, the daughter of Chandranna. Chandranna catches them, beats Seena, and drags his daughter away. Seena collapses near Kirubunkal. Mudli finds him half-dead and takes him home. That same night, Chandranna and his men attack Mudli and beat him to death.

Officially, people say Mudli died of disease during an epidemic. He is buried quickly. The attack shows that even a village can be violent, and it reminds the reader of the value of peace and mutual respect.

## Q1. (3)
The cart driver reflects the caste discrimination of Ambedkar's childhood. At Masur railway station, Ambedkar and the other children wanted a bullock cart to Goregaon. The drivers refused because the children were Mahars. They were ready to pay double the fare, but the drivers believed that carrying untouchables would pollute them.

One driver agreed on a condition. The children had to drive the cart, and he would walk beside it. They accepted, because they had no other way to reach their father. In the darkness they became frightened and kept asking how far Goregaon was. He told them they would reach soon.

After several hours they reached a toll collector's hut. The driver told them to rest there. They could not get water and spent the night without eating. The next morning he returned, they started at eight o'clock, and they reached Goregaon. His actions show how untouchability made even ordinary travel difficult for children.

## Q1. (4)
Water is a sign of social inequality in On the Way to Goregaon. On the way from Masur, the children were hungry. They had brought food, but they needed water before they could eat it.

Near a stream, the water was dirty and not fit to drink. At the toll collector's hut they hoped for food and water. On the cartman's advice they pretended to be Muslims, but the toll collector still refused them water. Their caste stood in the way. They had food and still had to stay hungry.

Ambedkar saw that caste discrimination was not limited to social relationships. It reached a basic need such as drinking water. The incident shows the denial of a basic human right.

# PART I - LONG ANSWERS

## Q2. (1)
Nikolai Gogol mixes realism with satire and the grotesque. His characters often look unusual, but they stand for deeper truths about human life. Akaki Akakievich seems strange and humorous at first. Gogol then shows his innocence, loneliness, and humanity.

Akaki lives an unusual life. He has no interest in entertainment, wealth, or social status. His only happiness is copying official documents. He rarely speaks, except when it is necessary. He is shy and quiet, and he avoids arguments. This simple nature makes him look strange.

He is completely devoted to his work. Even after office hours he enjoys copying documents at home. His appearance is also odd. He wears old clothes, walks quietly, and pays little attention to how he looks. His colleagues laugh at him because he never answers with anger. He bears the insults in silence and goes on with his work.

The great change in his life is the dream of a new overcoat. His excitement over such a simple thing may seem unusual, but it shows the hardship of a poor man's life. After his death, his ghost wanders the streets and steals overcoats. That supernatural change adds another grotesque element.

Although he appears strange, he is kind, honest, hardworking, and innocent. He never harms anyone. Gogol uses this unusual personality to criticise a society that laughs at the weak instead of helping them.

## Q2. (2)
In Kirubunkal, Ravikumar Neha shows the oral tradition of Karnataka. Stories connect generations and preserve culture. Hirikateyajji, the elderly storyteller, is one of the most memorable characters.

Tales travel mainly through oral tradition. Grandparents tell stories to children. Parents share stories with their families. Storytellers narrate tales at village gatherings and festivals. Travellers carry stories from one place to another. As a story moves from one generation to the next, small changes are added, but the main message remains. In this way folktales survive for hundreds of years. They are not only entertainment. They preserve history, culture, values, and collective memory.

Hirikateyajji is an old woman who knows many ancient stories. She is the guardian of traditional knowledge. Her stories hold imagination, village history, beliefs, heroes, and moral lessons. She tells them naturally, as elders have done for centuries. Through her, children learn about their ancestors and their cultural traditions. She stands for the living memory of the village. Even while modern life changes quickly, her stories continue to inspire people. She reminds them that culture survives only when stories are remembered and shared.

She is the voice of tradition. She connects the past with the present, and her storytelling protects the heritage of the community. Oral literature is as valuable as written literature. Kirubunkal reminds the reader that stories are the true treasure of a society.

## Q2. (3)
Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's journey to Goregaon is one of the most important incidents in On the Way to Goregaon. It became a painful experience that exposed him to untouchability.

During his childhood, Ambedkar and his siblings were living away from their father. Their father, Ramji Sakpal, was working at Koregaon. The children were eager to meet him during their holidays. Their father had been informed about the visit and was expected to receive them at the railway station. They were excited because they had not seen him for some time.

They dressed in good clothes and started by train. They carried their luggage and provisions for the journey. They were happy and expected a pleasant reunion with their father. An unexpected problem occurred. Their father had not received the letter informing him of their arrival. His servant had hidden it. He was not at the railway station to receive them.

When they reached Masur railway station, they were alone in an unfamiliar place. They had to arrange transport to Goregaon. A journey begun in happiness became a turning point. The children belonged to the Mahar community, which was treated as untouchable. The journey exposed young Ambedkar to caste discrimination and to the injustice faced by oppressed people.

## Q3. (6)
1. **Blockchain:** A blockchain is used in cryptocurrency. It is a continuously growing list of records called blocks, linked and secured by cryptography. Each block contains a set of verified transactions. Once a transaction is recorded, it is virtually impossible to alter.
2. **CIBIL:** CIBIL (Credit Information Bureau (India) Limited) score is another name for a credit score. It is a three-digit number from 300 to 900 that reflects a person's creditworthiness. It summarises past loan repayments, credit card use, and other borrowing. A higher score means a lower risk for lenders.
3. **Demat account:** A dematerialised account. It is an electronic account that holds securities such as shares, bonds, mutual funds, government securities, and exchange traded funds in digital form. A bank account holds money. A demat account holds securities.
4. **Bullion:** Gold or silver in the form of mass or bars.
5. **Dividend:** The share of profits given to the shareholders.
