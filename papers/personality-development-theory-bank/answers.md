# MODULE 1: PERSONALITY, SELF-AWARENESS, SWOT & GOAL SETTING – 2 MARKS

## Q1. Define personality. (Q.P. 54324) (Model Nov 2026) (Question bank)
**Personality** is the set of behaviours, thoughts, emotions and habits that make a person unique. It reflects how a person reacts, communicates, thinks and presents themselves in different situations, and it is shaped by heredity, environment and situation.

## Q2. Mention the determinants of personality.
The **determinants of personality** are heredity, environment and situation. Heredity gives traits from parents such as body structure, intelligence and temperament; environment covers family, school, peer groups and media; and behaviour also changes with the situation.

## Q3. What are the Big Five personality traits?
The **Big Five personality traits** are openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism. They describe how curious, organised, outgoing, friendly and emotionally stable or anxious a person is.

## Q4. Name any two theories of personality.
1. **Type theory (Carl Jung):** Classifies people into personality types such as introverts and extroverts.
2. **Trait theory (Gordon Allport):** Focuses on specific traits that define a person's personality.

## Q5. Define self-awareness. (Question bank)
**Self-awareness** is the ability to understand your own personality, emotions, behaviour, strengths and weaknesses. It helps in setting realistic goals and in knowing how others see you.

## Q6. Mention any two ways to develop self-awareness.
1. **Self-reflection:** Thinking about your own actions and keeping a personal diary or journal.
2. **Feedback and tools:** Taking feedback from others and using personality assessment tools such as MBTI or the Johari Window.

## Q7. What is SWOT analysis? (Model Nov 2026) (Question bank)
**SWOT analysis** is a personal analysis tool that studies one's Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats. It helps a person understand themselves better and plan personal and career development.

## Q8. What is goal setting?
**Goal setting** is the process of identifying something you want to achieve and making a plan to reach it. It gives clarity and direction, and increases motivation and focus.

## Q9. What is a SMART goal?
A **SMART goal** is a goal that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. Example: "I will improve my spoken English by practising 30 minutes daily for 3 months."

## Q10. What is time management?
**Time management** means organising and planning how to divide your time between specific activities to work smarter, not harder. It reduces stress, increases productivity and ensures timely completion of tasks.

## Q11. Mention two time management techniques. (Model paper 2) (Question bank)
1. **Pomodoro technique:** Study or work for 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break.
2. **To-do list:** Prioritise tasks with a to-do list and follow a daily plan or schedule.

## Q12. What is the Pomodoro technique?
The **Pomodoro technique** is a time management technique in which a person studies or works for 25 minutes and then takes a 5-minute break. It is used along with a daily plan and a to-do list to work smarter.

## Q13. What is procrastination? (Q.P. 54324) (Question bank)
**Procrastination** is the habit of delaying important tasks, often by doing easier or less important activities. It is caused by fear of failure, lack of motivation, feeling overwhelmed and distractions.

## Q14. Mention any two ways to build self-discipline.
1. **Clear daily routine:** Set a fixed routine, such as a fixed time to study every day, even if it is only 30 minutes.
2. **Consistency:** Be consistent with habits, track your progress regularly and avoid excuses.

# MODULE 2: SELF-MONITORING, PERCEPTION, ATTITUDE & ASSERTIVENESS – 2 MARKS

## Q1. What is self-monitoring? (Model paper 2) (Question bank)
**Self-monitoring** is the ability to observe and control one's own behaviour, emotions and appearance in different situations to fit social expectations. It means paying attention to how you act and how others react, and adjusting your behaviour if needed.

## Q2. Who are high and low self-monitors?
1. **High self-monitors:** People who are very aware of how others see them and adjust their behaviour to suit the situation.
2. **Low self-monitors:** People who act the same in all situations and are not much concerned about others' opinions.

## Q3. Define perception. (Model paper 2) (Question bank)
**Perception** is the process of interpreting and understanding the information received through the senses. In simple words, it is how we see, understand and make sense of the world around us.

## Q4. Mention the stages of the perception process.
The **perception process** has three stages: selection, organisation and interpretation. Selection is choosing which information to focus on, organisation is arranging it in a meaningful way, and interpretation is giving meaning to it.

## Q5. What is the halo effect?
The **halo effect** is a perceptual error in which a whole opinion of a person is formed from one good quality. Example: thinking someone is intelligent just because they speak well.

## Q6. What is stereotyping?
**Stereotyping** is a perceptual error of judging someone on the basis of their group identity. Example: thinking all teenagers are lazy.

## Q7. What are cognitive biases?
**Cognitive biases** are errors in thinking that affect fair thinking and good decision-making. Examples are confirmation bias (only noticing information that supports your existing belief) and anchoring bias (relying too much on the first piece of information).

## Q8. Mention any two ways to avoid perceptual errors.
1. **Be aware of biases:** Know your own biases and avoid jumping to conclusions.
2. **Collect more information:** Gather more facts before judging and keep an open mind.

## Q9. Give the meaning of attitude. (Q.P. 54324)
**Attitude** is a mental state involving beliefs, feelings and behaviour towards a person, object or situation. In simple words, it is how we think and feel about something, which affects how we behave; it may be positive, negative or neutral.

## Q10. Mention two types of attitude. (Model Nov 2026) (Question bank)
1. **Positive attitude:** Optimistic, hopeful and open-minded.
2. **Negative attitude:** Pessimistic, doubtful and resistant to change.

## Q11. How are attitudes measured?
**Attitudes** are measured through surveys and questionnaires, observation of behaviour, and psychological tools such as Likert scales. These show how a person thinks and feels about something.

## Q12. Mention any two methods of attitude change.
1. **Education and information:** Learning new facts or perspectives.
2. **Positive reinforcement:** Rewards for showing better behaviour.

## Q13. What is assertive communication? (Model Nov 2026) (Question bank)
**Assertive communication** is expressing your thoughts, feelings and needs confidently and respectfully, without being aggressive or passive. It means standing up for yourself in a calm and honest way, using polite but firm language.

## Q14. Mention any two assertiveness techniques.
1. **"I" statements:** Focus on your own feelings instead of blaming others. Example: "I feel left out when I'm not included in group work."
2. **Broken record technique:** Calmly repeating your point until it is heard.

## Q3. What are the factors influencing perception? Explain. (Q.P. 54324)
1. **Attitude and values of the perceiver:** A person sees things through his own beliefs and values, so two people may judge the same event differently.
2. **Past experience:** What a person has experienced before shapes how he interprets a new person or situation.
3. **Interests, needs and expectations:** People notice what interests them or what they expect to see, and ignore the rest.
4. **Situational factors:** The time, place and environment in which something is seen affect how it is understood; a loud voice at a party and in an office are perceived differently.
5. **Target factors:** The features of the person or object observed, such as appearance, behaviour, novelty and size, influence the perception formed.

## Q4. Explain the perception process and cognitive biases in decision-making.
**Perception** is the process of interpreting and understanding the information received through the senses.
1. **Selection:** Choosing which information to focus on.
2. **Organisation:** Arranging the information in a meaningful way.
3. **Interpretation:** Giving meaning to the information.
4. **Confirmation bias:** Only noticing information that supports your existing belief.
5. **Anchoring bias:** Relying too much on the first piece of information. Such biases affect fair thinking and good decision-making.

## Q5. What are perceptual errors? Explain any three with examples. (Model Nov 2026) (Question bank)
**Perceptual errors** are mistakes we make while judging people or situations, because we interpret information wrongly.
1. **Stereotyping:** Judging someone based on their group identity. Example: thinking all teenagers are lazy.
2. **Halo effect:** Forming a whole opinion from one good quality. Example: thinking someone is intelligent just because they speak well.
3. **Horn effect:** Judging someone negatively because of one bad quality. Example: thinking a late-comer is careless in all work.
4. **Other errors:** First impression error (quick judgement without the full picture) and projection (assuming others think and feel as you do).
5. **Avoiding errors:** Be aware of your own biases, avoid jumping to conclusions, collect more information before judging and keep an open mind.

The **theories of leadership** are the trait theory, the behavioural theory and the situational theory. ## Q11. What is conflict management? (Q.P. 54324) (Question bank)

**Intrapersonal conflict** is conflict within oneself. Example: confusion about choosing a career.

## Q6. Explain the principles of negotiation.

## Q7. Define negotiation. Explain its process with an example. (Model Nov 2026)
**Negotiation** is the process where two or more people discuss and reach an agreement that benefits all parties. Example: a college fest committee hiring a sound system.
1. **Preparation:** Know your goals, the facts and the other party's needs. The committee fixes its budget at Rs. 15,000 and checks other vendors' rates.
2. **Opening:** Start the discussion politely and professionally. The committee meets the vendor and explains the event.
3. **Discussion:** Share ideas, offers and concerns, and listen actively. The vendor quotes Rs. 20,000 and explains the cost of the equipment.
