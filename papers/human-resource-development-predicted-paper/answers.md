# SECTION A — 2 MARKS

## Q1.
**Human Resource Development (HRD)** is a planned and continuous process of developing employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and values so that they contribute effectively to organisational objectives and achieve personal growth. In simple words, HRD means improving the abilities and potential of employees for better performance.

## Q2.
**Human Resource Planning (HRP)** is the process of estimating future human resource requirements and ensuring that the right people are available at the right place and time. In short, it means the right person, in the right job, at the right time and in the right number.

## Q3.
1. **Performance appraisal:** The systematic assessment of an employee's performance against set standards, which also identifies training needs.
2. **Training and development:** Training improves skills for the present job; development prepares the employee for future roles.

## Q4.
**HRD climate** is the employees' perception of whether their organisation provides an environment that supports learning, development, openness, trust and growth. It is shaped by the general climate, the OCTAPAC culture and the HRD mechanisms of the organisation.

## Q5.
A **learning style** is a person's preferred way of taking in and processing information while learning, such as visual, auditory, reading/writing or kinesthetic (learning by doing). Trainers should use a mix of methods rather than label employees by one style.

## Q6.
**Aptitude** is a person's natural ability or potential to learn and perform a particular kind of work, such as customer handling, teamwork or problem-solving. It shows how well a person can perform after training, not only what he already knows.

## Q7.
**Human resource accounting (HRA)** is the process of identifying, measuring and reporting the cost and value of an organisation's human resources. It treats spending on recruitment, selection, induction, training and development as an investment in people (for example Rs. 40,000 per employee).

## Q8.
**HRD audit** is a systematic review of an organisation's HRD strategies, systems, practices and competencies to find out how well they support business goals. It shows the strengths, gaps and areas where HRD must improve.

## Q9.
**Outbound training** is learning through structured activities outside the usual workplace, often outdoors, such as team challenges and problem-solving tasks. It develops teamwork, leadership, communication, trust and problem-solving.

## Q10.
| Basis | Training | Development |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Present job | Future roles and overall growth |
| Time period | Usually short or medium term | Usually long term and continuous |

## Q11.
**Competency mapping** is the process of identifying the competencies required for a particular job and comparing them with the competencies an employee currently has. It shows the gap between required and existing capabilities, for example a job needing advanced Excel when the employee has only basic Excel.

## Q12.
A **balanced scorecard** is a strategic performance management framework that turns an organisation's strategy into objectives and measures from four perspectives: financial, customer, internal process, and learning and growth. In HRD it links employee learning with processes, customers and financial results.

# SECTION B — 5 MARKS

## Q2.
1. **Personnel management (first stage):** The traditional, administrative stage; its focus was record keeping, wages, discipline and welfare, and its aim was to maintain employees.
2. **Human resource management (second stage):** The managerial stage; employees were seen as a resource to be managed and utilised for present performance.
3. **Human resource development (third stage):** The developmental stage; the focus is on developing the knowledge, skills, attitudes and potential of employees.
4. **Change in focus:** Administration (PM) → managing people (HRM) → developing people (HRD).
5. **Change in orientation:** Short-term maintenance (PM) → present performance (HRM) → long-term growth of the employee and the organisation (HRD).

## Q3.
1. **Internal – personality and perception:** Personality decides how a person reacts to situations, and perception decides how they interpret events.
2. **Internal – attitude, motivation and values:** A positive attitude, strong motivation and values such as honesty and fairness lead to better behaviour.
3. **Internal – ability and learning:** The capacity to do the task and past experience shape how an employee behaves.
4. **External – leadership, culture and climate:** The leader's style and the organisation's culture and climate strongly influence how employees act.
5. **External – rewards, job design, group, policies and technology:** Rewards, the nature of the job, co-workers, fair policies, technology and the economic environment also shape behaviour.

## Q4.
1. **Motivation:** People learn better when they have a reason to learn, for example to get promoted.
2. **Readiness:** The learner must be mentally and physically ready; a tired or uninterested employee learns little.
3. **Practice and active participation:** Repeated practice and taking part through cases, discussions and role plays make learning stronger than a lecture alone.
4. **Feedback and reinforcement:** Learners need to know what they did right and wrong, and behaviour that is praised or rewarded is repeated.
5. **Meaningfulness and transfer:** Learning should be linked to real work, and what is learned must be applied on the job; only then does performance improve.

## Q5.
1. **Communication and vision:** A good leader explains ideas and instructions clearly and gives the team a clear direction.
2. **Decision-making and problem-solving:** Makes suitable decisions at the right time, identifies problems and finds solutions.
3. **Confidence and responsibility:** Is confident yet open to advice, and accepts accountability for results.
4. **Honesty:** Acts truthfully and ethically, so followers trust the leader.
5. **Motivation and emotional intelligence:** Encourages employees to perform well, and understands and manages emotions.

## Q6.
1. **Meaning:** HRA identifies, measures and reports the costs and value of an organisation's human resources, showing the investment made in employees.
2. **Historical cost method:** Records the actual costs of recruiting, selecting, training and developing an employee. Example: Rs. 10,000 + Rs. 5,000 + Rs. 15,000 + Rs. 10,000 = Rs. 40,000.
3. **Replacement cost method:** Estimates the cost of replacing an employee with someone of similar skills and experience, including recruitment and training costs.
4. **Opportunity cost method:** Considers the value of an employee's services when choosing between alternative uses or departments.
5. **Limitations:** Human value is hard to measure in money, employees are not like physical assets, methods give different values and future contribution is uncertain.

## Q7.
1. **Identify the problem:** Training need analysis is the systematic process of finding the gap between the skills employees have and the skills their jobs need; it starts with the organisational problem or performance objective.
2. **Collect information:** Through observation, interviews, questionnaires, performance appraisal reports, customer complaints and skill tests.
3. **Study three levels:** Organisational analysis (goals, strategy, resources), task or job analysis (duties and skills required) and individual analysis (each employee's present performance).
4. **Compare and find the cause:** Compare existing skills with required skills and confirm that training can solve the gap; lack of equipment or unrealistic targets need a different solution.
5. **Set priorities and recommend:** Set training priorities and objectives, and recommend the content, method, participants and schedule.

## Q8.
### (a) Outbound training
1. **Meaning:** Outbound training is learning through structured activities outside the usual workplace, often in an outdoor or unfamiliar setting.
2. **Activities:** Team-building games, group challenges, obstacle courses with proper safety, treasure hunts and outdoor projects, followed by reflection and discussion.
3. **Objectives:** It develops teamwork, leadership, trust, communication, planning, problem-solving and confidence; it is not just an outing, and needs planned objectives and supervision.
### (b) Attitudinal training
4. **Meaning:** Attitudinal training develops positive work-related attitudes, values and behaviour towards work, customers, colleagues and change. Example: employees who are rude to customers learn empathy, patience and service attitude.
5. **Methods and importance:** Role play, case studies, group discussion, behavioural modelling, self-assessment with feedback, and counselling; it improves discipline, customer service, morale and cooperation.

## Q9.
1. **Coaching:** A structured process in which a coach helps an employee improve specific skills or reach defined performance goals through questions, guidance, practice and feedback.
2. **Role of coaching:** It encourages self-reflection and practical action, tracks progress, and improves confidence, performance and problem-solving. Example: a sales manager coaches a sales executive on customer objections.
3. **Mentoring:** A developmental relationship in which an experienced mentor gives guidance, advice and support to a less experienced mentee for long-term career development.
4. **Role of mentoring:** It supports confidence, career planning and understanding of the organisation, and shares knowledge between experienced and new employees.
5. **Role in HRD:** Both are methods of leadership development; they help employees grow faster, build commitment and reduce early turnover.

# SECTION C — 10 MARKS

## Q10.
### Planning and appraisal subsystems
1. **Human resource planning:** Forecasting the number and kind of employees needed and making them available at the right time. Example: a bank plans the staff needed for 20 new branches.
2. **Performance appraisal:** Assessing an employee's present performance against standards. Example: a salesperson is rated against his target and his weak presentation skill is identified.
3. **Potential appraisal:** Assessing an employee's capacity for future, higher roles. Example: a good sales executive is assessed through an assessment centre for the sales manager post.
4. **Feedback and performance coaching:** Telling employees how they are doing and helping them improve. Example: a manager guides an employee to structure his reports better.
5. **Career planning:** Mapping a career path and the competencies needed for it. Example: executive → supervisor → branch manager, with training at each step.
### Development and support subsystems
6. **Training and development:** Building skills for the present job and the future. Example: training staff on new CRM software, and a leadership programme for future managers.
7. **Succession planning:** Preparing employees to fill key posts when they fall vacant. Example: a deputy manager is groomed to replace the retiring plant head.
8. **Organisation development:** Planned efforts to improve the culture, teamwork and openness of the whole organisation. Example: team-building workshops that build trust and collaboration (OCTAPAC).
9. **Rewards and recognition:** Rewarding desired behaviour and good performance. Example: an 'employee of the month' award, incentives and promotion.
10. **Employee welfare, counselling and HR information system:** Looking after the well-being of employees and keeping HR records that support decisions. Example: health check-ups, counselling services and a digital employee database.

## Q11.
### HRD mechanisms
1. **Performance appraisal:** Assesses performance against standards and identifies strengths, gaps and training needs.
2. **Potential appraisal:** Assesses the capacity for future, higher roles. Example: can a good sales executive become a sales manager?
3. **Training and development:** Training builds present competence; development builds future capability.
4. **Feedback and counselling:** Specific, timely and constructive feedback; counselling aimed at improvement, not punishment.
5. **Career planning, job rotation, coaching and rewards:** Career paths, movement across departments, guidance from seniors and recognition of good work.
### Role of organisational climate and culture
6. **Meaning:** Climate is how the organisation feels to employees now; culture is the deeper values, beliefs and norms that explain why it behaves that way.
7. **HRD climate:** The employees' perception that the organisation supports learning, openness, trust and growth; it is built by top management support, fair personnel policies and line manager support.
8. **OCTAPAC culture:** Openness, Confrontation, Trust, Autonomy, Proactivity, Authenticity and Collaboration create a developmental culture.
9. **Positive climate:** Encourages learning, innovation, initiative, cooperation, motivation, communication and commitment.
10. **Negative climate:** Causes fear, resistance to change, low motivation, poor communication and turnover, so HRD efforts fail.

## Q12.
### Tools for evaluating HRD
1. **Human resource accounting (HRA):** Identifying, measuring and reporting the costs and value of an organisation's human resources. Example (historical cost): recruitment Rs. 10,000 + selection Rs. 5,000 + training Rs. 15,000 + development Rs. 10,000 = Rs. 40,000.
2. **Methods and use of HRA:** Historical cost, replacement cost and opportunity cost methods; HRA shows the investment in people and supports manpower decisions, though human value is hard to measure in money.
3. **HR audit:** A systematic examination of HR policies, procedures, practices and systems, covering recruitment, training, compensation, appraisal, welfare, HR records and legal compliance.
4. **Steps in an HR audit:** Planning, collecting information, examination, identifying problems, preparing the report and corrective action; it improves efficiency and finds weaknesses.
5. **Benchmarking:** Comparing practices and performance with other organisations or recognised standards. Example: if one company takes 30 days to recruit and another takes 15, the first studies the second's process.
6. **Types and steps of benchmarking:** Internal, competitive, functional and generic; identify the area, select a partner, collect data, compare, find gaps, implement and monitor.
### Impact of HRD on the bottom line
7. **Meaning:** Impact assessment measures the results of HRD activities; the bottom line is the organisation's final financial result, mainly net profit.
8. **Productivity, cost and quality:** Trained employees produce more, make fewer errors and less wastage, and give better products and service.
9. **Retention, customers and sales:** Career development lowers turnover and replacement cost; better service builds customer loyalty; product knowledge and selling skills raise revenue.
10. **Measuring the impact:** ROI = (Benefit − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100. Example: benefit Rs. 18 lakh on a cost of Rs. 12 lakh gives ROI = **50%**.

## Q13.
### Principles of selecting a training method
1. **Objectives and nature of the job:** The method must achieve the learning outcome; practical jobs need demonstration and practice, conceptual subjects need discussion or case studies.
2. **Participants and group size:** Consider the trainees' age, education, experience and knowledge; a lecture suits a large group and coaching suits an individual.
3. **Cost, time and trainer:** Consider fees, materials and time away from work, and make sure the trainer can use the method well.
4. **Practice, transfer and evaluation:** Give practice with feedback, help employees apply learning at work, and choose a method whose results can be assessed.
### Techniques at different levels
5. **Workers:** On-the-job training, demonstration, job instruction training, apprenticeship and simulation. Example: learning machine operation from a skilled operator.
6. **Supervisors:** Role play, case study, group discussion, coaching skills training and job rotation. Example: a new supervisor learns to allocate duties and handle team conflict.
7. **Middle and senior managers:** Management development programmes, business games, case analysis, management by projects, executive coaching and mentoring, and seminars.
### Evaluating training effectiveness
8. **Four levels:** Reaction (did they find it useful?), learning (did knowledge and skill improve?), behaviour (is it applied at work?) and results (did complaints, errors or delays fall?).
9. **Methods:** Feedback forms, pre- and post-training tests, skill assessments, supervisor observation, performance and productivity records, and customer feedback.
10. **Steps:** Set objectives, decide the evidence, take a baseline before training, measure reaction and learning, observe application at work, review results and recommend improvements.

## Q14.
### Recent trends
1. **Continuous learning and reskilling:** Employees keep learning; reskilling prepares them for a new role and upskilling improves skills for the present role.
2. **Technology-enabled learning:** Learning management systems, e-learning, mobile learning, simulations and blended learning.
3. **Coaching and mentoring:** Individual guidance for specific skills and for long-term career growth.
4. **Competency-based HRD:** Competency frameworks and competency mapping guide recruitment, training, promotion and career development.
5. **Research, data and measurement:** HRD research and analytics identify needs, and outcomes are measured through ROI and the balanced scorecard.
6. **Engagement, wellbeing and positive change:** More attention to engagement, inclusion, culture and strength-based approaches such as appreciative inquiry.
7. **Future direction:** Personalised learning, digital and problem-solving skills, and closer alignment of HRD strategy with organisational strategy.
### Critical view
8. **Technology problems:** Uneven digital literacy, the need for devices and internet, less direct interaction and the protection of employee data.
9. **Poor utilisation:** Training is wasted when employees lack tools, time, support or a chance to practise, or when the content is not relevant to the job.
10. **Careful adaptation:** A practice from another country or company may not suit local culture, language, laws and conditions; competency mapping must use real evidence and not be a one-time exercise.
