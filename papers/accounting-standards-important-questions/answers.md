# 2 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Define an accounting standard. [PYQ] [College]
An **accounting standard** is a written statement of rules for recognising, measuring, presenting and disclosing transactions in the financial statements. In India the Accounting Standards Board prepares them and the Council of the ICAI issues them. A standard cannot override the law.

## Q2. State any benefits of accounting standards. [PYQ]
Accounting standards are issued so that enterprises follow one accepted practice. Their benefits are:
1. **Standardisation:** similar transactions are recorded in one way.
2. **Disclosure:** the statements give the extra information users need.
3. **Comparability:** results of different enterprises, and of different years, can be compared.

## Q3. What is a construction contract? [PYQ]
A **construction contract**, as per AS 7, is a contract specifically negotiated for the construction of an asset or a combination of assets that are closely interrelated or interdependent in terms of their design, technology and function or their ultimate purpose or use. Examples are contracts for a bridge, building, dam, road, ship or refinery.

## Q4. Define revenue. [PYQ]
**Revenue** is the gross inflow of cash, receivables or other consideration arising in the course of the ordinary activities of an enterprise, from the sale of goods, the rendering of services, and the use by others of enterprise resources yielding interest, royalties and dividends. Amounts collected on behalf of third parties, such as GST, are not revenue.

## Q5. Define property, plant and equipment as per AS 10. [PYQ]
**Property, plant and equipment**, as per AS 10 (Revised), are tangible items held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes. They are expected to be used during more than one period, that is for more than 12 months.

## Q6. State the types of employee benefits. [PYQ]
AS 15 (Revised) divides employee benefits into four types:
1. **Short-term benefits**, such as wages, paid leave and bonuses payable within twelve months.
2. **Post-employment benefits**, such as gratuity and pension.
3. **Other long-term benefits**, such as long-service leave.
4. **Termination benefits**, payable when employment comes to an end.

## Q7. Give the meaning of borrowing cost. [PYQ]
**Borrowing cost**, as per AS 16, means interest and other costs incurred in connection with the borrowing of funds. It includes interest on loans, amortisation of discount or premium, finance charges under leases, and exchange differences to the extent they are an adjustment to interest.

## Q8. What is a lease? [PYQ]
A **lease**, as per AS 19, is an agreement by which the lessor gives the lessee the right to use an asset for an agreed period, in return for a payment or a series of payments. A finance lease transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership. An operating lease does not.

## Q9. State any four intangible assets. [PYQ]
Intangible assets have no physical substance. Four examples are:
1. Patents
2. Copyrights
3. Trademarks
4. Computer software

## Q10. Write a note on AS 29. [PYQ]
**AS 29** lays down when a provision, a contingent liability and a contingent asset are recognised. A provision is recorded only when a present obligation arises from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable, and the amount can be estimated reliably. A contingent liability is disclosed, not recognised. A contingent asset is not recognised.

## Q11. What are inventories as per AS 2 (revised)? [PYQ]
**Inventories**, as per AS 2 (Revised), are assets held:
1. for sale in the ordinary course of business,
2. in the process of production for such sale, or
3. as materials or supplies to be consumed in production or in rendering services.

## Q12. Mention the types of investments. Differentiate between Current Investments and Long-term Investments according to AS-13. [PYQ] [College]
AS 13 classifies investments by the period for which they are held:
1. **Current investment:** readily realisable, and intended to be held for not more than one year from the date of purchase.
2. **Long-term investment:** any investment other than a current investment.

## Q13. State any two objectives of setting accounting standards.  [College]
Accounting standards are set so that enterprises follow one accepted practice. Two objectives are:
1. **Uniformity:** similar transactions are recorded in one way.
2. **Comparability:** results of different enterprises, and of different years, can be compared.

## Q14. State the timing of revenue recognition from the sale of goods under AS-9.  [College]
Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when the seller has transferred the property in the goods for a price. That point usually coincides with the transfer of the significant risks and rewards of ownership. If the two do not coincide, revenue is recognised when the significant risks and rewards pass to the buyer.

## Q15. What constitutes the cost of an item of Property, Plant, and Equipment under AS-10?  [College]
The cost of an item of property, plant and equipment, as per AS 10, is:
1. the purchase price, including import duties and non-refundable purchase taxes, after trade discounts and rebates,
2. costs directly attributable to bringing the asset to the location and condition needed for its intended use, and
3. the initial estimate of dismantling, removal and site-restoration costs.

## Q16. Define "Qualifying Asset" as per AS-16 (Borrowing Costs).  [College]
A **qualifying asset**, as per AS 16, is an asset that necessarily takes a substantial period of time to get ready for its intended use or sale. Borrowing costs that are directly attributable to such an asset are capitalised as part of its cost.

## Q17. Distinguish between a Finance Lease and an Operating Lease under AS-19.  [College]
A **finance lease** transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership from the lessor to the lessee. An **operating lease** does not. One test is that the present value of the minimum lease payments amounts to substantially all of the fair value of the asset.

## Q18. What is an Intangible Asset according to AS-26?  [College]
An **intangible asset**, as per AS 26, is an identifiable non-monetary asset without physical substance, held for use in production or supply, for rental to others, or for administration. Three features are required: identifiability, control, and probable future economic benefits.

## Q19. Define a Provision as per AS-29.  [College]
A **provision**, as per AS 29, is a liability that can be measured only by using a substantial degree of estimation. It is recognised only when a present obligation arises from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable, and a reliable estimate can be made.

## Q20. What is Net Realisable Value (NRV) in inventory valuation as per AS-2?  [College]
**Net realisable value**, as per AS 2, is the estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, less the estimated costs of completion and the estimated costs necessary to make the sale. Inventory is carried at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower.

# 5 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Explain the standard-setting process. [PYQ]
The ICAI constituted the Accounting Standards Board in 1977 to prepare standards. The standard-setting process is:
1. The area that needs a standard is identified.
2. A study group is formed and it prepares a draft.
3. The draft is circulated and the views of different bodies are taken.
4. An exposure draft is finalised and issued for public comments.
5. The draft is modified in the light of those comments.
6. The Council of the ICAI issues the Accounting Standard. For companies, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs notifies it.

A standard does not override the law. Where the law requires a different treatment, the law is followed and the departure is disclosed.

## Q2. What is meant by amortisation of an intangible asset? What are the different methods of amortisation as per AS 26? [PYQ]
**Amortisation** is the systematic writing off of the depreciable amount of an intangible asset over its useful life. Depreciable amount is cost, or the amount substituted for cost, less any residual value. The charge begins when the asset is available for use and is recognised as an expense.

AS 26 requires the method to follow the pattern in which the economic benefits are consumed. If that pattern cannot be determined reliably, the straight-line method is used. The methods are:
1. **Straight-line method:** an equal amount each year.
2. **Diminishing-balance method:** a fixed percentage on the written-down value.
3. **Unit-of-production method:** based on the units expected from the asset.

The period and the method are reviewed at each financial year end. A change is treated as a change in accounting estimate.

## Q3. When should a provision be recognised as per AS 29? Explain in brief. [PYQ]
AS 29 says a provision is recognised only when all three conditions are met:
1. The enterprise has a present obligation, legal or constructive, as a result of a past event.
2. An outflow of resources embodying economic benefits is probable, that is more likely than not.
3. A reliable estimate can be made of the amount. The amount recognised is the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the balance-sheet date.

If any one condition fails, no provision is made. The item is then a contingent liability and is disclosed, unless the possibility of outflow is remote. A contingent asset is not recognised.

## Q4. Explain the standard-setting process in India by the Accounting Standards Board (ASB) and the National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA).  [College]
The ICAI constituted the Accounting Standards Board in 1977 to prepare standards. The standard-setting process is:
1. The area that needs a standard is identified.
2. A study group is formed and it prepares a draft.
3. The draft is circulated and the views of different bodies are taken.
4. An exposure draft is finalised and issued for public comments.
5. The draft is modified in the light of those comments.
6. The Council of the ICAI issues the Accounting Standard. For companies, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs notifies it.

The National Financial Reporting Authority recommends accounting standards and auditing standards for companies, and it monitors and enforces compliance with them. It can also look into the professional misconduct of auditors. A standard does not override the law. Where the law requires a different treatment, the law is followed and the departure is disclosed.

## Q1. Explain the criteria for classification of non-company entities as decided by the ICAI. [PYQ]
The Council of the ICAI, for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2020, classifies non-company entities into four levels. Level I entities are large and comply with every Accounting Standard in full. Level II, Level III and Level IV are medium, small and micro entities. Together they are called MSMEs, and they are allowed certain relaxations.

### Level I
An entity is Level I if it falls in any one of these:
1. Its securities are listed, or are in the process of listing, in India or outside India.
2. It is a bank, including a co-operative bank, a financial institution, or it carries on insurance business.
3. Its turnover, excluding other income, exceeds Rs. 250 crore in the immediately preceding accounting year.
4. Its borrowings, including public deposits, exceed Rs. 50 crore at any time during that year.
5. It is a holding or subsidiary entity of any of the above.

### Level II
An entity that is not Level I is Level II if:
1. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds Rs. 50 crore but does not exceed Rs. 250 crore, or
2. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed Rs. 10 crore but do not exceed Rs. 50 crore, or
3. It is a holding or subsidiary entity of any such entity.

### Level III
An entity that is not Level I or Level II is Level III if:
1. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds Rs. 10 crore but does not exceed Rs. 50 crore, or
2. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed Rs. 2 crore but do not exceed Rs. 10 crore, or
3. It is a holding or subsidiary entity of any such entity.

### Level IV
Every non-company entity not covered above is a Level IV entity. An MSME that uses the relaxations discloses, in a note, that it is an MSME and states its level. This classification does not apply to a non-company entity that is required to follow Ind AS.

## Q2. XYZ Construction Ltd. undertakes an industrial complex. Separate proposals were raised for each unit. Each unit was subject to separate negotiation, and the costs and revenues of each unit can be identified. Should each unit be treated as a separate construction contract under AS 7?  [College]
AS 7 says that where one contract covers a number of assets, the construction of each asset is a separate construction contract when three conditions are met:
1. separate proposals have been submitted for each asset,
2. each asset has been subject to separate negotiation, so that the contractor and the customer could accept or reject that part, and
3. the costs and revenues of each asset can be identified.

All three conditions are met for the units of the industrial complex. XYZ Construction Ltd. treats the construction of each unit as a **separate construction contract**.

## Q3. Explain Accounting Standard 9 in detail.  [College]
**Revenue** is the inflow of cash, receivables or other consideration arising in the course of the ordinary activities of an enterprise. AS 9 deals with when that revenue is recognised, and with the effect of uncertainty about the amount or its collection. It does not apply to construction contracts, hire-purchase and lease agreements, government grants, or the revenue of insurance companies.

Revenue covered by AS 9 arises from:
1. the sale of goods,
2. the rendering of services, and
3. the use by others of enterprise resources, which yields interest, royalties and dividends.

**Sale of goods.** Revenue is recognised when the seller has transferred the property in the goods to the buyer for a price. That normally coincides with the transfer of the significant risks and rewards of ownership. Where it does not, revenue waits until those risks and rewards have passed. A delay in delivery, whether the buyer's or the seller's, or an agreement fixing the time of transfer, can separate the two events.

**Rendering of services.** Revenue is recognised as the service is performed, by one of two methods:
1. **Completed-service method:** revenue is recognised only when the service is completed or substantially completed.
2. **Proportionate-completion method:** revenue is recognised in step with the degree of completion.

**Interest, royalties and dividends.**
1. **Interest** is recognised on a time basis, taking the amount outstanding and the rate.
2. **Royalties** are recognised on an accrual basis, as the agreement provides.
3. **Dividends** are recognised when the owner's right to receive payment is established.

Revenue is recognised only when it can be measured reliably and collection is reasonably certain. If collectability becomes uncertain after recognition, the amount already recorded is not reversed. A separate provision is made instead.
