# 2 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Give the meaning of underwriting of shares. [PYQ]
**Underwriting of shares** is a contract in which underwriters agree, for a commission, to take up the shares that the public does not subscribe. It assures the company that the whole issue will be taken up.

## Q2. What is firm underwriting? [PYQ]
**Firm underwriting** is a definite commitment by an underwriter to take a fixed number of shares, whether or not the public subscribes for them. Those shares are his liability in addition to his share of any shortfall in the public issue.

## Q3. What is partial underwriting? [PYQ]
**Partial underwriting** is when only a part of the issue is underwritten. The company itself is treated as an underwriter for the part not underwritten, and bears that shortfall.

## Q4. What do you mean by profit prior to incorporation? [PYQ]
**Profit prior to incorporation** is the profit earned between the date a running business is purchased and the date the company is legally incorporated. A company cannot legally earn business profit before registration, so it is a **capital profit**.

## Q5. How do you treat pre and post incorporation profit? [PYQ]
1. **Pre-incorporation profit:** A capital profit. It is transferred to **Capital Reserve**, cannot be paid as dividend, and is used to write off goodwill, preliminary expenses or issue discounts.
2. **Post-incorporation profit:** A revenue profit. It goes to the **P&L Appropriation Account** and is fully available for dividend.

## Q6. What do you mean by valuation of shares? [PYQ]
**Valuation of shares** is the process of finding the real worth of a company's share. It is done when shares are not quoted, or when the market price does not show the true value, for example on amalgamation, conversion of shares, or a purchase of a block of shares.

## Q7. What is goodwill? [PYQ]
**Goodwill** is the extra earning power of a business over and above the normal return on the capital employed. It is an intangible asset that arises from reputation, location, efficient management and customer connections.

## Q8. Write any four features of goodwill. [PYQ]
1. **Intangible:** Goodwill has no physical form.
2. **Not separable:** It can be sold only along with the business, not by itself.
3. **Fluctuating value:** Its value changes with the earning capacity of the business and does not fall merely by use.
4. **Helps to earn super profit:** It is the reason the business earns more than the normal return.

## Q9. What is super profit? [PYQ]
**Super profit** is the excess of the average profit over the normal profit. Normal profit is capital employed multiplied by the normal rate of return. Super profit = Average profit minus Normal profit.

## Q10. Under which note are the following recorded? (i) Bad debts (ii) Employer contribution to PF. [PYQ]
1. **Bad debts** are recorded under **Other expenses**.
2. **Employer's contribution to provident fund** is recorded under **Employee benefits expense**.

## Q11. Who is a liquidator? [PYQ]
A **liquidator** (Company Liquidator) is the person appointed to wind up a company. He realises the assets, pays the costs and the creditors in the legal order, and distributes any surplus to the shareholders.

## Q12. Mention any four preferential creditors. [PYQ]
Preferential creditors are paid before the unsecured creditors. Under Sec. 327 of the Companies Act, 2013, four of them are:
1. **Government dues:** taxes, cesses and rates that became due and payable within 12 months before winding up.
2. **Wages and salary** of employees for not more than 4 months, within the prescribed limit.
3. **Accrued holiday remuneration** payable to employees.
4. **Provident fund, pension, gratuity and ESI** contributions payable by the company as employer.

# 5 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. How do you calculate the value of shares under the net asset method and the yield method? [PYQ]
1. **Net assets:** Take all real assets at their realisable value; leave out fictitious assets such as preliminary expenses.
2. **Deduct:** Subtract outside liabilities and preference share capital to get net assets for equity shareholders.
3. **Intrinsic value per share** = Net assets for equity ÷ Number of equity shares.
4. **Yield method, expected rate:** Expected rate = (Profit available for equity ÷ Equity paid-up capital) × 100.
5. **Yield value** = (Expected rate ÷ Normal rate) × Paid-up value of one share.

## Q2. What are the modes of liquidation of a company? [PYQ]
1. **Compulsory winding up by the Tribunal:** Under Sec. 271 of the Companies Act, 2013, the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) orders winding up on a petition.
2. **Grounds for compulsory winding up:** A special resolution of the company, acts against the sovereignty and integrity of India, affairs run fraudulently, or failure to file financial statements or annual returns for five years.
3. **Voluntary liquidation:** The company itself decides to close; this now falls under Sec. 59 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
4. **Members' voluntary (solvent) liquidation:** The directors declare that the company can pay all its debts, and the members pass a resolution.
5. **Liquidation on insolvency:** An insolvent company is liquidated through the IBC process for the benefit of its creditors.

# 10 MARKS — IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

## Q1. Explain the different methods of valuation of shares and valuation of goodwill, with formulas. [PYQ]
### Valuation of shares
1. **Net assets method (intrinsic value):** The share is worth the net assets that belong to it. Fictitious assets are excluded, and outside liabilities and preference capital are deducted. Intrinsic value per equity share = Net assets available to equity holders ÷ Number of equity shares.
2. **Yield method:** The share is worth what its earnings yield as compared with the normal rate. Expected rate = (Profit available for equity ÷ Equity paid-up capital) × 100. Yield value = (Expected rate ÷ Normal rate) × Paid-up value of one share.
3. **Fair value method:** The simple average of the other two, used when both asset backing and yield should count. Fair value = (Intrinsic value + Yield value) ÷ 2.
### Valuation of goodwill
4. **Average profits method:** Average profit = Total adjusted profits ÷ Number of years. Goodwill = Average profit × Number of years' purchase. Abnormal items are removed first.
5. **Super profits method:** Normal profit = Capital employed × Normal rate of return. Super profit = Average profit − Normal profit. Goodwill = Super profit × Number of years' purchase.
6. **Annuity method:** Super profit is treated as an annuity and discounted. Goodwill = Super profit × Present value of an annuity of Re. 1 for the given years at the normal rate. Goodwill is lower than under plain years' purchase because later profits are discounted.
