# SECTION A – 2 MARKS
## Q1. (a)
**Perception** is the process of selecting, organising and interpreting information received through the senses. It shapes how we understand people and situations.
## Q1. (b)
**Attitude** is a person's beliefs, feelings and tendency to act towards a person, object or situation. It can be positive, negative or neutral.
## Q1. (c)
**Conflict management** is the process of handling disagreements constructively. Methods such as negotiation and collaboration help people resolve differences and work together.
## Q1. (d)
**Bureaucratic leadership** is a style in which leaders follow established rules, procedures and a formal chain of command. Employees carry out clearly defined duties under the authority of their designated supervisor.
## Q1. (e)
**Procrastination** is the habit of putting off an important task even when the delay may cause problems. A student who keeps choosing easier tasks instead of starting an assignment is procrastinating.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS
## Q2.
1. **Meaning:** Personality theories explain why people differ in their usual patterns of thought, feeling and behaviour.
2. **Type theory:** Carl Jung grouped people by broad tendencies such as introversion and extraversion.
3. **Trait theory:** Gordon Allport explained personality through lasting qualities, such as sociability, that vary from person to person.
4. **Psychoanalytic theory:** Sigmund Freud emphasised how unconscious motives and childhood experiences shape behaviour.
5. **Humanistic theory:** Abraham Maslow explained personal growth through the desire to fulfil one's potential.
## Q3.
1. **Recognise bias:** Notice when a stereotype or personal preference is influencing your judgement.
2. **Delay judgement:** Give yourself time to look beyond a first impression.
3. **Check the facts:** Use several observations before judging someone from a single incident.
4. **Seek another perspective:** Ask the person for their explanation to understand the circumstances.
5. **Keep an open mind:** Revise your first impression when reliable evidence challenges it.
## Q4.
1. **Strong beliefs and habits:** Long-held views become familiar and difficult to question.
2. **Peer pressure:** People may keep an attitude to retain the approval of their group.
3. **Fear of change:** Uncertainty about the outcome makes a familiar attitude feel safer.
4. **Lack of awareness:** People may overlook new facts that give them a reason to reconsider.
5. **Low motivation:** People rarely change a view when they see little benefit in doing so.
## Q5.
1. **Agility:** A leader weighs new information quickly and makes timely decisions.
2. **Empathy:** Understanding team members' feelings helps a leader respond to their needs.
3. **Adaptability:** A leader revises plans when technology, staffing or customer needs change.
4. **Technological awareness:** A leader chooses digital tools that help the team coordinate its work.
5. **Communication:** A leader explains goals clearly and listens to feedback before acting.
## Q6.
1. **Direction:** A clear goal identifies the result a person wants to achieve.
2. **Focus:** Goals help a person give priority to tasks that contribute to that result.
3. **Motivation:** A worthwhile target encourages effort even when progress is slow.
4. **Planning and discipline:** Breaking a goal into small tasks makes regular action easier.
5. **Measuring progress:** Comparing actual results with the target shows where to adjust effort.
## Q7.
1. **Language differences:** Unfamiliar terms or jargon can hide the intended meaning.
2. **Cultural differences:** The same word or gesture may carry different meanings across cultures.
3. **Emotional barriers:** Anger, fear or stress can distort how a message is expressed or understood.
4. **Poor listening:** Interrupting or planning a reply can cause the listener to miss the main point.
5. **Physical distractions:** Noise, distance or a weak internet connection can interrupt a message.
