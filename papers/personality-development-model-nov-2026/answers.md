# SECTION A – 2 MARKS

## Q1. (a)
**Personality** is the set of behaviours, thoughts, emotions and habits that make a person unique. It reflects how a person reacts, communicates, thinks and presents themselves in different situations, and it is shaped by heredity, environment and situation.

## Q1. (b)
**SWOT analysis** is a personal analysis tool that studies one's Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats. It helps a person understand themselves better and plan personal and career development.

## Q1. (c)
1. **Positive attitude:** Optimistic, hopeful and open-minded.
2. **Negative attitude:** Pessimistic, doubtful and resistant to change.

## Q1. (d)
**Assertive communication** is expressing your thoughts, feelings and needs confidently and respectfully, without being aggressive or passive. It means standing up for yourself in a calm and honest way, using polite but firm language.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS

## Q2.
1. **Meaning:** Personality is the set of behaviours, thoughts, emotions and habits that make a person unique; it is shaped by heredity, environment and situation.
2. **Heredity:** Traits inherited from parents, such as body structure, intelligence and temperament. Example: a child may have the calm temperament of a parent.
3. **Family environment:** Upbringing, values and habits learnt at home shape how a person thinks and behaves.
4. **Social and cultural environment:** School, peer groups, media and culture influence a person's attitudes, language and behaviour. Example: a student in an active college club grows more confident.
5. **Situation:** Behaviour may change with the situation. Example: someone may be quiet at home but confident in a classroom or office.

## Q3.
**Self-awareness** is the ability to understand your own personality, emotions, behaviour, strengths and weaknesses.
1. **Realistic goals and better decisions:** Knowing your strengths and weaknesses helps you set achievable goals and improves decision-making and emotional control.
2. **Stronger relationships:** It builds better relationships and helps you understand how others perceive you.
3. **Self-reflection and a journal:** Think about your own actions and keep a personal diary or journal.
4. **Feedback from others:** Ask teachers, friends and colleagues for honest feedback and act on it.
5. **Personality assessment tools:** Use tools such as MBTI or the Johari Window. Example: if you get nervous during public speaking, practise in small groups to build confidence.

## Q4.
**Perceptual errors** are mistakes we make while judging people or situations, because we interpret information wrongly.
1. **Stereotyping:** Judging someone based on their group identity. Example: thinking all teenagers are lazy.
2. **Halo effect:** Forming a whole opinion from one good quality. Example: thinking someone is intelligent just because they speak well.
3. **Horn effect:** Judging someone negatively because of one bad quality. Example: thinking a late-comer is careless in all work.
4. **Other errors:** First impression error (quick judgement without the full picture) and projection (assuming others think and feel as you do).
5. **Avoiding errors:** Be aware of your own biases, avoid jumping to conclusions, collect more information before judging and keep an open mind.

## Q6.
**Team building** is the process of creating a group of individuals who work together effectively to achieve common goals.
1. **Importance:** It encourages cooperation and trust, improves communication, increases productivity and creativity, reduces conflicts and builds a sense of belonging.
2. **Formal teams:** Created by an organisation with a clear structure and goals. Example: a project team in a company.
3. **Informal teams:** Formed naturally by people with common interests. Example: friends working together on a college event.
4. **Cross-functional teams:** Members from different departments. Example: marketing, finance and HR working together.
5. **Virtual teams:** Teams that work online or remotely. Example: a remote customer support team.

## Q7.
**Negotiation** is the process where two or more people discuss and reach an agreement that benefits all parties. Example: a college fest committee hiring a sound system.
1. **Preparation:** Know your goals, the facts and the other party's needs. The committee fixes its budget at Rs. 15,000 and checks other vendors' rates.
2. **Opening:** Start the discussion politely and professionally. The committee meets the vendor and explains the event.
3. **Discussion:** Share ideas, offers and concerns, and listen actively. The vendor quotes Rs. 20,000 and explains the cost of the equipment.
4. **Bargaining:** Make offers and find compromises for a win-win result. The committee offers Rs. 16,000 plus the vendor's name on the fest banner.
5. **Closing and implementation:** Agree on the final terms and follow through. Both agree on Rs. 16,500 with the banner, put it in writing and keep their word on the day.
