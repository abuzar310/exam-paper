# SECTION A – 2 MARKS

## Q1. (a)
An **accounting standard** is a written statement of rules for recognising, measuring, presenting and disclosing transactions in the financial statements. In India the Accounting Standards Board prepares them and the Council of the ICAI issues them. A standard cannot override the law.

## Q1. (b)
Accounting standards are issued so that enterprises follow one accepted practice. Their benefits are:
1. **Standardisation:** similar transactions are recorded in one way.
2. **Disclosure:** the statements give the extra information users need.
3. **Comparability:** results of different enterprises, and of different years, can be compared.

## Q1. (c)
**Inventories**, as per AS 2 (Revised), are assets held:
1. for sale in the ordinary course of business,
2. in the process of production for such sale, or
3. as materials or supplies to be consumed in production or in rendering services.

## Q1. (d)
**Property, plant and equipment**, as per AS 10 (Revised), are tangible items held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes. They are expected to be used during more than one period, that is for more than 12 months.

## Q1. (e)
AS 13 classifies investments by the period for which they are held:
1. **Current investment:** readily realisable, and intended to be held for not more than one year from the date of purchase.
2. **Long-term investment:** any investment other than a current investment.

## Q1. (f)
**Borrowing cost**, as per AS 16, means interest and other costs incurred in connection with the borrowing of funds. It includes interest on loans, amortisation of discount or premium, finance charges under leases, and exchange differences to the extent they are an adjustment to interest.

## Q1. (g)
Intangible assets have no physical substance. Four examples are:
1. Patents
2. Copyrights
3. Trademarks
4. Computer software

## Q1. (h)
A **lease**, as per AS 19, is an agreement by which the lessor gives the lessee the right to use an asset for an agreed period, in return for a payment or a series of payments. A finance lease transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership. An operating lease does not.

## Q1. (i)
AS 15 (Revised) divides employee benefits into four types:
1. **Short-term benefits**, such as wages, paid leave and bonuses payable within twelve months.
2. **Post-employment benefits**, such as gratuity and pension.
3. **Other long-term benefits**, such as long-service leave.
4. **Termination benefits**, payable when employment comes to an end.

## Q1. (j)
**AS 29** lays down when a provision, a contingent liability and a contingent asset are recognised. A provision is recorded only when a present obligation arises from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable, and the amount can be estimated reliably. A contingent liability is disclosed, not recognised. A contingent asset is not recognised.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS

## Q2.
The ICAI constituted the Accounting Standards Board in 1977 to prepare standards. The standard-setting process is:
1. The area that needs a standard is identified.
2. A study group is formed and it prepares a draft.
3. The draft is circulated and the views of different bodies are taken.
4. An exposure draft is finalised and issued for public comments.
5. The draft is modified in the light of those comments.
6. The Council of the ICAI issues the Accounting Standard. For companies, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs notifies it.

A standard does not override the law. Where the law requires a different treatment, the law is followed and the departure is disclosed.

## Q3.
**Amortisation** is the systematic writing off of the depreciable amount of an intangible asset over its useful life. Depreciable amount is cost, or the amount substituted for cost, less any residual value. The charge begins when the asset is available for use and is recognised as an expense.

AS 26 requires the method to follow the pattern in which the economic benefits are consumed. If that pattern cannot be determined reliably, the straight-line method is used. The methods are:
1. **Straight-line method:** an equal amount each year.
2. **Diminishing-balance method:** a fixed percentage on the written-down value.
3. **Unit-of-production method:** based on the units expected from the asset.

The period and the method are reviewed at each financial year end. A change is treated as a change in accounting estimate.

## Q4.
AS 29 says a provision is recognised only when all three conditions are met:
1. The enterprise has a present obligation, legal or constructive, as a result of a past event.
2. An outflow of resources embodying economic benefits is probable, that is more likely than not.
3. A reliable estimate can be made of the amount. The amount recognised is the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the balance-sheet date.

If any one condition fails, no provision is made. The item is then a contingent liability and is disclosed, unless the possibility of outflow is remote. A contingent asset is not recognised.

# SECTION C – 10 MARKS

## Q10.
The Council of the ICAI, for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2020, classifies non-company entities into four levels. Level I entities are large and comply with every Accounting Standard in full. Level II, Level III and Level IV are medium, small and micro entities. Together they are called MSMEs, and they are allowed certain relaxations.

### Level I
An entity is Level I if it falls in any one of these:
1. Its securities are listed, or are in the process of listing, in India or outside India.
2. It is a bank, including a co-operative bank, a financial institution, or it carries on insurance business.
3. Its turnover, excluding other income, exceeds Rs. 250 crore in the immediately preceding accounting year.
4. Its borrowings, including public deposits, exceed Rs. 50 crore at any time during that year.
5. It is a holding or subsidiary entity of any of the above.

### Level II
An entity that is not Level I is Level II if:
1. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds Rs. 50 crore but does not exceed Rs. 250 crore, or
2. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed Rs. 10 crore but do not exceed Rs. 50 crore, or
3. It is a holding or subsidiary entity of any such entity.

### Level III
An entity that is not Level I or Level II is Level III if:
1. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds Rs. 10 crore but does not exceed Rs. 50 crore, or
2. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed Rs. 2 crore but do not exceed Rs. 10 crore, or
3. It is a holding or subsidiary entity of any such entity.

### Level IV
Every non-company entity not covered above is a Level IV entity. An MSME that uses the relaxations discloses, in a note, that it is an MSME and states its level. This classification does not apply to a non-company entity that is required to follow Ind AS.
