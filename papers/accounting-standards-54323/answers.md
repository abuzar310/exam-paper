# SECTION A – 2 MARKS

## Q1. (a)
An **accounting standard** is a written statement of accounting rules and guidelines, issued by an expert body, covering how transactions and events are recognised, measured, presented and disclosed in the financial statements. In India the Accounting Standards Board of the ICAI prepares them, and the Council of the ICAI issues them. They cannot override the law.

## Q1. (b)
1. **Standardisation:** They cut down the choice of alternative accounting treatments, so similar transactions are recorded in a similar way.
2. **Disclosure:** They require extra information in the financial statements, so users can understand the figures.
3. **Comparability:** Statements of different enterprises, and of the same enterprise over years, can be compared.

## Q1. (c)
As per **AS 2 (Revised)**, inventories are assets held:
1. for sale in the ordinary course of business,
2. in the process of production for such sale, or
3. in the form of materials or supplies to be consumed in production or in rendering services.

## Q1. (d)
As per **AS 10 (Revised)**, property, plant and equipment are tangible items that are held for use in the production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes, and are expected to be used during more than one period (more than 12 months).

## Q1. (e)
As per **AS 13**, investments are classified as:
1. **Current investments:** readily realisable and intended to be held for not more than one year from the date of purchase.
2. **Long-term investments:** investments other than current investments.

They are further shown, where applicable, as government or trust securities; shares, debentures or bonds; investment properties; and others.

## Q1. (f)
**Borrowing cost**, as per **AS 16**, means interest and other costs incurred by an enterprise in connection with the borrowing of funds. Examples are interest on loans and debentures, amortisation of discounts or premiums, finance charges on leases, and exchange differences on foreign-currency borrowings to the extent they are regarded as an adjustment to interest.

## Q1. (g)
Four intangible assets are:
1. Patents
2. Copyrights
3. Trademarks
4. Computer software

Licences and franchises are also intangible assets. Internally generated goodwill is not recognised as an asset under AS 26.

## Q1. (h)
A **lease**, as per **AS 19**, is an agreement by which the lessor gives the lessee the right to use an asset for an agreed period of time, in return for a payment or a series of payments. A finance lease transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership. An operating lease does not.

## Q1. (i)
**AS 15 (Revised)** classifies employee benefits into four types:
1. **Short-term benefits**, such as wages, salaries, paid leave and bonuses payable within twelve months.
2. **Post-employment benefits**, such as gratuity, pension and retirement benefits.
3. **Other long-term benefits**, such as long-service leave and deferred compensation.
4. **Termination benefits**, payable on ending an employee's service.

## Q1. (j)
**AS 29** deals with provisions, contingent liabilities and contingent assets. A provision is recognised only when there is a present obligation from a past event, an outflow of resources is probable, and the amount can be estimated reliably. Contingent liabilities are not recognised; they are disclosed, unless the chance of outflow is remote. Contingent assets are not recognised.

# SECTION B – 5 MARKS

## Q2.
The ICAI set up the **Accounting Standards Board (ASB)** in 1977. The standard-setting process is:
1. **Identification of the area** for which a standard is needed.
2. **Constitution of a study group** to prepare the draft.
3. **Preparation of the draft** and its circulation among the members of the ASB and others.
4. **Views of different bodies** on the draft are obtained.
5. **Finalisation of the exposure draft.**
6. **Comments on the exposure draft** are received from the public and interested bodies.
7. **Modification of the draft** in the light of those comments.
8. **Issue of the Accounting Standard** under the authority of the Council of the ICAI. For companies, the standard is notified by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs.

An Accounting Standard does not override the law. If a law requires a different treatment, the law is followed and the departure is disclosed.

## Q3.
**Amortisation** is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an intangible asset over its useful life. Depreciable amount is cost (or the amount substituted for cost) less residual value. Amortisation begins when the asset is available for use, and the charge for each period is recognised as an expense, unless another standard requires it to be included in the carrying amount of another asset.

As per **AS 26**, the method should follow the pattern in which the economic benefits are consumed. If that pattern cannot be determined reliably, the straight-line method is used. The methods are:
1. **Straight-line method:** an equal amount each year over the useful life.
2. **Diminishing-balance method:** a fixed percentage on the written-down value.
3. **Unit-of-production method:** based on the units expected from the asset.

The method is applied consistently. The period and the method are reviewed at least at each financial year end, and a change is treated as a change in estimate under AS 5.

## Q4.
As per **AS 29**, a provision is recognised when all three conditions are met:
1. **Present obligation:** the enterprise has a present obligation (legal or constructive) as a result of a past event.
2. **Probable outflow:** it is probable that an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation. Probable means more likely than not.
3. **Reliable estimate:** a reliable estimate can be made of the amount. The amount is the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the balance-sheet date.

If any one condition fails, no provision is made. Where the outflow is not probable, or the amount cannot be estimated, the item is a contingent liability and is disclosed, unless the possibility of outflow is remote. An enterprise should not recognise a contingent asset.

## Q6.

Useful life is unchanged, so 6 years remain. Under AS 10 the revalued amount is depreciated over the remaining useful life.

Depreciation from year 5 = 2,700 / 6 = **Rs. 450 lakhs per year**.

| At the end of | Rs. lakhs |

**Carrying amount at the end of year 5 is Rs. 2,250 lakhs, and at the end of year 6 is Rs. 1,800 lakhs.**

# SECTION C – 10 MARKS

## Q10.
The Council of the ICAI, at its meeting of 18–19 March 2021, classified **non-company entities** for Accounting Standards applicable to periods beginning on or after 1 April 2020. There are four levels. Level I entities are large. Level II are medium, Level III are small and Level IV are micro. Levels II, III and IV are together called MSMEs.

**Level I** (any one of the following, at the end of the relevant accounting period):
1. Securities are listed, or are in the process of listing, on any stock exchange in India or outside India.
2. Banks (including co-operative banks), financial institutions, or an insurance business.
3. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds **Rs. 250 crore** in the immediately preceding accounting year.
4. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed **Rs. 50 crore** at any time during the immediately preceding accounting year.
5. Holding and subsidiary entities of any of the above.

Level I entities comply in full with all Accounting Standards.

**Level II** (not Level I, but any one of the following):
1. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds **Rs. 50 crore** but does not exceed **Rs. 250 crore**.
2. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed **Rs. 10 crore** but do not exceed **Rs. 50 crore**.
3. Holding and subsidiary entities of any of the above.

**Level III** (not Level I or Level II, but any one of the following):
1. Turnover, excluding other income, exceeds **Rs. 10 crore** but does not exceed **Rs. 50 crore**.
2. Borrowings, including public deposits, exceed **Rs. 2 crore** but do not exceed **Rs. 10 crore**.
3. Holding and subsidiary entities of any of the above.

**Level IV:** every non-company entity not covered in Level I, II or III.

Levels II, III and IV are given exemptions and relaxations. An entity which uses them must disclose, in a note, that it is an MSME, its level, and that it has complied with the Accounting Standards so far as they apply to that level. This classification does not apply to a non-company entity which is required to follow Ind AS.
