1.1 Personality 
Definition: 
Personality is the set of behaviours, thoughts, emotions, and habits that 
make a person unique. It reflects how a person reacts, communicates, 
thinks, and presents themselves in different situations. 
Example: 
A person who is friendly, confident, and calm under pressure has a 
different personality compared to someone who is shy, nervous, and avoids 
communication. 
Determinants of Personality: 
1. Heredity – Traits inherited from parents such as body structure, 
intelligence, and temperament. 
2. Environment – Social and cultural surroundings including family, 
school, peer groups, and media. 
3. Situations – A person’s behaviour may change depending on the 
situation. For example, someone may be quiet at home but confident 
in a classroom or office. 
Personality Traits (Big Five): 
1. Openness – Creative, curious, and open to new experiences 
2. Conscientiousness – Organized, responsible, and goal-oriented 
3. Extraversion – Outgoing, talkative, and energetic 
4. Agreeableness – Friendly, helpful, and cooperative 
5. Neuroticism – Emotionally unstable, anxious, or easily upset

Theories of Personality: 
1. Type Theory – Classifies people into personality types like introverts 
and extroverts (Carl Jung). 
2. Trait Theory – Focuses on specific traits that define a person’s 
personality (Gordon Allport). 
3. Psychoanalytic Theory – Emphasizes the unconscious mind and 
childhood experiences (Sigmund Freud). 
4. Humanistic Theory – Focuses on personal growth and self-fulfilment 
(Abraham Maslow).

1. Improves communication and social interaction 
2. Builds self-confidence and motivation 
3. Helps in job interviews, leadership roles, and teamwork 
4. Supports emotional control and stress management

2.1 Self-Monitoring 
Definition: 
Self-monitoring is the ability to observe and control one’s own behavior, 
emotions, and appearance in different situations to fit social expectations. 
In simple words: 
It means paying attention to how you act, how others react to you, and 
adjusting your behavior if needed. 
Types of Self-Monitors: 
1. High Self-Monitors: 
o They are very aware of how they are seen by others. 
o They adjust their behaviour to suit the situation. 
o Example: A person who speaks politely in a formal meeting 
but is casual with friends. 
2. Low Self-Monitors: 
o They act the same in all situations. 
o They are not much concerned about others’ opinions. 
o Example: A person who behaves casually even during 
interviews. 
Advantages of High Self-Monitoring: 
 
Better at communication and teamwork 
 
Easily adapt to new environments 
 
Good at building relationships 
Disadvantages of High Self-Monitoring: 
 
May appear fake or less genuine 
 
Can be mentally exhausting

2.2 Perception 
Definition: 
Perception is the process of interpreting and understanding the 
information received through the senses. 
In simple words: 
It is how we see, understand, and make sense of the world around us. 
Factors Influencing Perception: 
1. Personal factors – Attitude, experience, values, interests 
2. Situational factors – Time, place, and environment 
3. Target factors – Characteristics of the person or object being 
observed 
Perception Process: 
1. Selection – Choosing which information to focus on 
2. Organization – Arranging information in a meaningful way 
3. Interpretation – Giving meaning to the information 
Errors in Perception: 
1. Stereotyping – Judging someone based on group identity 
Example: Thinking all teenagers are lazy 
2. Halo Effect – Forming an opinion based on one good quality 
Example: Thinking someone is intelligent just because they speak 
well 
3. Horn Effect – Judging someone negatively due to one bad quality 
4. First Impression Error – Making quick judgments without knowing 
the full picture 
5. Projection – Assuming others think and feel like you do

## Page 14

14 
 
 
Cognitive Biases in Decision-Making: 
 
Confirmation Bias: Only noticing information that supports your 
existing belief 
 
Anchoring Bias: Relying too much on the first piece of information 
These biases can affect fair thinking and good decision-making. 
How to Avoid Perceptual Errors: 
a) Be aware of your own biases 
b) Avoid jumping to conclusions 
c) Collect more information before judging 
d) Keep an open mind 
2.3 Attitude 
Definition: 
Attitude is a mental state involving beliefs, feelings, and behavior toward 
a person, object, or situation. 
In simple words: 
It is how we think and feel about something, which affects how we 
behave. 
Formation of Attitude: 
a) Family and upbringing 
b) Personal experiences 
c) Peer influence 
d) Media and culture 
e) Education 
Types of Attitude: 
1. Positive Attitude – Optimistic, hopeful, and open-minded 
2. Negative Attitude – Pessimistic, doubtful, and resistant to change

3. Neutral Attitude – Neither strong positive nor negative feelings 
Measurement of Attitudes:

Barriers to Attitude Change:

3.3 Negotiation Skills 
Definition: 
Negotiation is the process where two or more people discuss and reach an 
agreement that benefits all parties. 
In simple words: 
It is a way of solving problems or making deals by talking and

3. Create a win-win situation

2. Opening – Start the discussion politely and professionally 
3. Discussion – Share ideas, offers, and concerns 
4. Bargaining – Make offers and find compromises 
5. Closing – Agree on final terms 
6. Implementation – Follow through on the agreement

3.4 Conflict Management

opinion that are easily resolved and do not cause lasting tension. 
b) Medium – Involves more frequent or persistent issues, such as 
regular disagreements, ongoing communication breakdowns, or 
unresolved issues that start to affect relationships or productivity
