                  PROFIT PRIOR TO INCORPORATION
1. Meaning of Profit Prior to Incorporation

Profit prior to incorporation means the profit earned by a business during the period before a
company is legally incorporated or registered.

When an existing business is taken over by a newly formed company, the company may
purchase the business from an earlier date. The profit earned between the date of purchase and
the date of incorporation is called profit prior to incorporation.

Such profit is treated as a capital profit, not a revenue profit.

Example:

A company was incorporated on 1st July 2026 but purchased an existing business from 1st
April 2026.

The profit earned from 1st April 2026 to 30th June 2026 is called profit prior to incorporation.

The profit earned from 1st July 2026 onwards is called profit after incorporation.

2. Meaning of Incorporation

Incorporation means the legal registration of a company under the Companies Act.

After incorporation, the company becomes a separate legal entity.

3. Meaning of Profit After Incorporation

Profit after incorporation means the profit earned by a company from the date of incorporation
up to the end of the accounting year.

It is treated as revenue profit and is available for distribution as dividends, subject to the
provisions of law.

4. Difference Between Profit Prior to Incorporation and Profit After
Incorporation

 Basis     Profit prior to incorporation    Profit after incorporation
 Meaning Profit earned before incorporation Profit earned after incorporation
 Nature    Capital profit                   Revenue profit
 Period    Before incorporation date        From incorporation date
 Treatment Transferred to Capital Reserve   Transferred to Statement of Profit and Loss
 Dividend Not available for dividend        May be available for dividend subject to law
 Purpose Capital benefit                    Normal business profit

5. Features of Profit Prior to Incorporation
   1. It arises when a company takes over an existing business.
   2. It relates to the period before incorporation.
   3. It is treated as capital profit.
   4. It is not available for distribution as dividend.
   5. It is transferred to Capital Reserve.
   6. It is calculated by dividing the total profit into pre-incorporation and post-incorporation
      periods.
   7. Expenses and incomes are allocated according to suitable bases.

6. Importance of Calculating Profit Prior to Incorporation
   1.   To identify the actual profit earned before incorporation.
   2.   To calculate the profit earned after incorporation.
   3.   To distinguish capital profit from revenue profit.
   4.   To determine the correct accounting treatment.
   5.   To prepare accurate financial statements.
   6.   To avoid incorrect distribution of dividends.



7. Procedure for Ascertaining Profit or Loss Prior to Incorporation

The profit or loss prior to incorporation is calculated by dividing the total profit or loss of the
business into two periods:

   1. Pre-incorporation period.
   2. Post-incorporation period.

Steps involved

Step 1: Find the date of purchase.

Identify the date from which the company has taken over the business.

Step 2: Find the date of incorporation.

Identify the date on which the company was legally registered.

Step 3: Calculate the time ratio.

Calculate the number of months in the pre-incorporation and post-incorporation periods.

Step 4: Calculate the sales ratio.

Find the sales made during both periods.

Step 5: Allocate expenses and incomes.

8. Time Ratio

Meaning

Time ratio means the ratio of the period before incorporation to the period after incorporation.

It is calculated based on the number of months in each period.

Formula

\Time Ratio= {Pre-incorporation months}: {Post-incorporation months}

Uses of Time Ratio

Time ratio is used to allocate:

   1.   Rent and rates.
   2.   Salaries and wages (when related to time).
   3.   Insurance.
   4.   Depreciation.
   5.   Office expenses.
   6.   Audit fees (where appropriate).
   7.   General expenses.

9. Sales Ratio

Meaning

Sales ratio means the ratio of sales made during the pre-incorporation period to sales made
during the post-incorporation period.

It is calculated based on the sales of each period.

Formula

Sales Ratio= {Pre-incorporation Sales}: {Post-incorporation Sales}

Uses of Sales Ratio

Sales ratio is used to allocate:

    4.   Carriage outward.
    5.   Discount allowed.
    6.   Bad debts (where sales are the appropriate basis).
    7.   Sales promotion expenses.

11. Procedure and Basis of Allocation of Expenses and Incomes
The expenses and incomes are divided between pre-incorporation and post-incorporation
periods using different methods.

There are three important bases:

    1. Time ratio.
    2. Sales ratio.
    3. Actual basis.

A. Items allocated on the basis of Time Ratio

These expenses are related to the passage of time.

Expenses                 Basis
Rent                     Time ratio
Rates and taxes          Time ratio
Insurance                Time ratio
Salaries                 Time ratio, if staff and salary levels are uniform
Depreciation             Time ratio, if asset use is uniform
Office expenses          Time ratio
General expenses         Time ratio
