VALUATION OF SHARES

1. Meaning of Valuation of Shares

Valuation of shares means determining the fair or actual value of a company's shares.

The face value of a share may be ₹10, ₹100 or any other amount, but its actual value may be
different depending on the financial position, profitability and future prospects of the company.

Example:

The face value of a share is ₹10, but its calculated value is ₹50.

Therefore, the value of the share is ₹50, even though its face value is ₹10.

2. Meaning of Share

A share is a unit of ownership in the share capital of a company.

A person who purchases shares in a company is called a shareholder.

Important terms

   1. Face Value: The nominal value printed on a share.
   2. Market Value: The price at which shares are traded in the stock market.
   3. Book Value: The value of a share according to the company's balance sheet.
   4. Intrinsic Value: The estimated actual value of a share based on the company's assets,
      liabilities and earning capacity.
   5. Fair Value: A reasonable value determined by considering relevant financial
      information and valuation methods.

3. Need for Valuation of Shares

Valuation of shares is required for the following purposes:

   1. Purchase and sale of shares: To determine a fair price when shares are purchased or
      sold.
   2. Amalgamation: To determine the value of shares when two or more companies
      combine.
   3. Absorption: To calculate the value of shares when one company takes over another
      company.
   4. Reconstruction: To determine the value of shares during company reconstruction.
   5. Taxation: To determine the value of shares for applicable tax purposes.
   6. Settlement of disputes: To settle disputes between shareholders.
   7. Conversion of shares: To determine the value when one type of share is converted into
      another.
   8. Investment decisions: To help investors decide whether shares are worth purchasing.

4. Factors Affecting Valuation of Shares

The following factors influence the value of shares:

1. Financial Position

A company with a strong financial position generally has higher share value.

2. Profitability

Companies earning higher profits may have higher share values.

3. Dividend Rate

Companies paying regular and attractive dividends may attract investors.

4. Nature of Business

The nature and stability of the company's business affect its share value.

5. Goodwill

A company with a good reputation and strong goodwill may have higher share value.

6. Market Conditions

Demand and supply in the market influence share prices.

7. Management Efficiency

Efficient management improves the performance and value of the company.

8. Future Prospects

Companies with good growth opportunities may have higher share values.

9. Capital Structure

The proportion of equity and debt capital affects the risk and value of shares.

10. Economic Conditions

Inflation, interest rates, government policies and economic conditions influence share values.

12. Industry Competition

5. Methods of Valuation of Shares

There are three important traditional methods of valuation of shares:

   1. Net Asset Method.
   2. Yield Method.
   3. Fair Value Method.

A. Net Asset Method (Intrinsic Value Method)

Meaning

Under the net asset method, the value of shares is calculated based on the net assets available
to equity shareholders.

It is also called the intrinsic value method or asset backing method.

Step 1: Calculate net assets available to equity shareholders.

Step 2: Calculate value per share.

Advantages

   1.   It is simple to understand.
   2.   It considers the actual value of assets.
   3.   It is useful for companies having substantial assets.
   4.   It is suitable for liquidation-related valuations, with appropriate adjustments.

Disadvantages

   1.   It ignores future earning capacity.
   2.   It may not reflect the goodwill of the company.
   3.   It does not directly consider dividend-paying capacity.
   4.   Asset valuation may be difficult.

B. Yield Method (Earning Capacity Method)

Meaning

Under the yield method, the value of shares is calculated based on the company's expected
earning capacity.

This method considers the return that shareholders receive from their investment.

Formula

Step 1: Calculate expected maintainable profit.

Step 2: Calculate profit available to equity shareholders.

Step 3: Calculate expected rate of return.

Advantages

   1.   It considers earning capacity.
   2.   It is useful for profitable companies.
   3.   It considers the expected return on investment.
   4.   It is useful for comparing companies.

Disadvantages

   1.   Future profits may be uncertain.
   2.   The normal rate of return may be difficult to determine.
   3.   It may ignore the actual value of assets.
   4.   It depends on estimates of maintainable profits.

C. Fair Value Method

Meaning

The fair value method is a combination of the net asset method and yield method.

It considers both the asset backing and earning capacity of the company.

Advantages

   1.   It considers both assets and profits.
   2.   It gives a balanced valuation.
   3.   It is useful for purchase and sale of shares.
   4.   It reduces the limitations of using only one method.

Disadvantages

   1. It involves calculations under two methods.
   2. It may be time-consuming.
   3. It depends on the accuracy of asset and profit estimates.

PART VALUATION OF GOODWILL

1. Meaning of Goodwill

Goodwill is the reputation and good name earned by a business because of its quality products,
customer satisfaction, efficient management, location and other advantages.

It is an intangible asset that helps a business earn higher profits compared with similar
businesses.

Example:

Two shops sell the same products. One shop has a good reputation and more customers.
Customers prefer that shop because of its quality and service.

The additional earning capacity created by its reputation is called goodwill.

2. Meaning of Valuation of Goodwill

Valuation of goodwill means calculating the monetary value of the reputation and other
advantages enjoyed by a business.

It is necessary when a business is sold, purchased, merged or reorganised.

3. Nature of Goodwill

The nature of goodwill can be explained as follows:

   1. Intangible Asset: Goodwill cannot be seen or touched.
   2. Non-physical Asset: It has no physical existence.
   3. Valuable Asset: It helps the business earn higher profits.
   4. Fictitious or Deferred Expense: Goodwill is not a fictitious asset; it is an intangible
      asset.
   5. Difficult to Measure: Its value cannot be determined exactly without valuation.
   6. Business Reputation: It represents the reputation and image of the business.

   7. Created Over Time: Goodwill generally develops through continuous business
       activities.
   8. Business-specific: Goodwill is connected with a particular business.
   9. Fluctuating Value: Its value may increase or decrease over time.
   10. Transferable with Business: Purchased goodwill may be transferred when the
       business is sold, subject to the terms of sale.

4. Features of Goodwill

   1. Goodwill is an intangible asset.
   2. It has economic value.
   3. It increases the earning capacity of a business.
   4. It is created through reputation and customer loyalty.
   5. It is difficult to calculate accurately.
   6. It depends on the profitability of the business.
   7. It is affected by market competition.
   8. It cannot usually be sold separately from the business.
   9. It is valuable during the sale or purchase of a business.
   10. Internally generated goodwill is generally not recognised as an asset in financial
       statements under applicable accounting standards.

5. Types of Goodwill

Goodwill is broadly classified into two types:

A. Purchased Goodwill

Purchased goodwill is the goodwill acquired when one business purchases another business for
an amount greater than the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired.

B. Non-purchased or Internally Generated Goodwill

Internally generated goodwill is the goodwill developed by a business through its own efforts,
reputation, customer relationships and quality services.

Example:

A business earns customer loyalty through good service and quality products over many years.

It develops internally generated goodwill.

Important accounting point: Internally generated goodwill is generally not recognised as an
asset in the balance sheet under Ind AS 38.

   1. Average Profit Method.
   2. Super Profit Method.
   3. Capitalisation Method.

A. Average Profit Method

Meaning

Under the average profit method, goodwill is calculated based on the average profits earned by
the business during the past few years.

Advantages

   1.   It is simple to calculate.
   2.   It is easy to understand.
   3.   It is based on past profits.
   4.   It is useful for businesses with stable profits.

Disadvantages

   1.   It ignores the capital employed.
   2.   It may not reflect future earning capacity.
   3.   Abnormal profits and losses require adjustments.
   4.   It may not be suitable for businesses with fluctuating profits.

B. Weighted Average Profit Method

Meaning

C. Super Profit Method

Meaning

Super profit means the profit earned by a business over and above the normal profit expected
from the capital employed.

Under this method, goodwill is calculated by multiplying super profit by the agreed number of
years' purchase.

Advantages

   1. It considers capital employed.
   2. It considers normal profit.
   3. It gives importance to extra earning capacity.

D. Capitalisation Method

Meaning

Under the capitalisation method, goodwill is calculated by comparing the capitalised value of
maintainable profits with the actual capital employed.

There are two approaches.
