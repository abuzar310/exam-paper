                      LIQUIDATION OF COMPANIES
1. Meaning of Liquidation

Liquidation of a company means the process of closing down a company permanently by
selling its assets, collecting amounts due from debtors, paying liabilities to creditors and
distributing the remaining amount among shareholders.

It is also known as Winding Up of a Company.

During liquidation, the business activities of the company are brought to an end and its legal
existence is terminated after completing the prescribed legal formalities.

Example: If a company is unable to pay its debts and decides to close its business, its assets
may be sold to pay creditors. This process is called liquidation.

2. Need for Liquidation of a Company

The main reasons for liquidation are:

   1. Continuous Losses: When a company suffers continuous losses and cannot continue
      its business.
   2. Insolvency: When a company is unable to pay its debts and liabilities.
   3. Voluntary Decision: When shareholders decide to close the company.
   4. Fraudulent Activities: When the company is involved in fraudulent or illegal
      activities.
   5. Failure of Business: When the company fails to achieve its business objectives.
   6. Legal Orders: When a competent authority or tribunal orders the winding up of a
      company.
   7. Lack of Finance: When a company does not have sufficient funds to carry on its
      business.
   8. Deadlock in Management: When disputes among directors or shareholders make it
      impossible to manage the company.

3. Objectives of Liquidation

   1.   To close the business permanently.
   2.   To sell the assets of the company.
   3.   To collect outstanding amounts from debtors.
   4.   To pay the creditors and other liabilities.
   5.   To settle the claims of employees.
   6.   To distribute the remaining amount among shareholders.
   7.   To dissolve the company according to law.

4. Types of Liquidation

Liquidation of a company is mainly classified into the following types:

A. Compulsory Liquidation

Compulsory liquidation means closing a company under the order of the National Company
Law Tribunal (NCLT) or through the applicable insolvency liquidation process.

Reasons:

   1.   The company has committed fraudulent activities.
   2.   The company has violated legal provisions.
   3.   The company has failed to comply with statutory requirements.
   4.   The Tribunal considers it just and equitable to wind up the company.

Procedure:

   1.   Filing a winding-up petition.
   2.   Examination of the petition by the Tribunal.
   3.   Appointment of a liquidator.
   4.   Taking control of company assets.
   5.   Preparation of a statement of affairs.
   6.   Sale of assets.
   7.   Payment of liabilities.
   8.   Distribution of surplus, if any.
   9.   Dissolution of the company.

B. Voluntary Liquidation

Voluntary liquidation means closing a company voluntarily by following the legal procedure
when the company is solvent and its members decide to discontinue its business.

Reasons:

   1.   The company has completed its objectives.
   2.   The company does not want to continue its business.
   3.   The shareholders decide to close the company.
   4.   The company has sufficient funds to pay its liabilities.

Procedure:

   1.   Declaration of solvency by directors.
   2.   Approval by shareholders through the required resolution.
   3.   Appointment of an insolvency professional as liquidator.
   4.   Public announcement and invitation of claims.
   5.   Collection and sale of assets.
   6.   Payment of creditors.
   7.   Distribution of surplus among shareholders.
   8.   Preparation of final report.

5. Company Liquidator

Meaning:

A company liquidator is a person appointed to take control of the company's affairs, realise its
assets, settle its liabilities and distribute the remaining funds among the persons entitled to
receive them.

The liquidator plays an important role in completing the liquidation process.

Qualifications of a Liquidator

      Must satisfy the legal requirements for appointment.
      In applicable IBC processes, must be a registered insolvency professional.
      Must be independent and free from conflicts of interest.
      Must possess the required professional knowledge and experience.

Duties and Responsibilities of a Liquidator

   1. To take possession of the company's assets.
   2. To prepare a statement of affairs.
   3. To examine the books of accounts.
   4. To collect amounts due from debtors.
   5. To sell the company's assets.
   6. To verify claims of creditors.
   7. To pay liquidation expenses.
   8. To settle secured and unsecured creditors' claims according to law.
   9. To distribute the surplus among shareholders.
   10. To prepare the final statement of account and report.
   11. To apply for dissolution of the company.

6. Preparation of Liquidator's Final Statement of Account

Meaning

Liquidator's Final Statement of Account is a statement prepared by the liquidator showing the
total cash received from the sale of assets and other sources and the payments made to various
parties during the liquidation process.

It is prepared to show how the money realised from the company's assets has been utilised.

It is also commonly called the Liquidator's Final Statement of Account or Liquidator's
Cash Account in company accounts examination problems.

Objectives

   1.   To record all cash receipts.
   2.   To record all payments.
   3.   To show the amount realised from assets.
   4.   To show the payment of creditors.
   5.   To calculate the liquidator's remuneration.
   6.   To determine the surplus available to shareholders.
   7.   To show the final settlement of accounts.

Steps Involved in Preparing the Final Statement

Step 1: Opening Balance

Record cash and bank balances available at the beginning of liquidation.

Step 2: Realisation of Assets

Record the amount received by selling the company's assets, such as:

       Land and buildings.
       Plant and machinery.
       Furniture.
       Stock.
       Investments.
       Debtors.

Step 3: Other Receipts

Record other amounts received, such as calls on contributories and outstanding amounts
collected.

Step 4: Payment of Liquidation Expenses

Pay expenses incurred during liquidation, including legal charges, valuation charges and other
necessary expenses.

Step 5: Payment to Creditors

Pay creditors according to the applicable statutory priority and security arrangements.

Step 6: Liquidator's Remuneration

Calculate and pay the liquidator's remuneration as prescribed or agreed under the applicable
rules.

Step 7: Payment to Shareholders

After satisfying the prior claims, distribute the available surplus among preference and equity
shareholders according to their rights.

Step 8: Closing Balance

The final statement should show how all available funds have been utilised.

Important Points to Remember in Final Statement

      Assets are shown at the actual amount realised, not necessarily their book value.
      Fictitious assets are not realised.
      Liquidation expenses are paid from the available funds.
      Creditors are paid according to their legal priority.
      Shareholders receive payment only after prior claims are settled.
      Preference shareholders are generally paid before equity shareholders, subject to
       applicable law and their rights.

B. Important Grounds for Winding Up under Section 271

A company may be wound up by the Tribunal on the following grounds:

   1. The company has passed a special resolution for winding up by the Tribunal.
   2. The company has acted against the sovereignty and integrity of India or other specified
      national interests.
   3. The company has conducted its affairs fraudulently.
   4. The company has failed to file financial statements or annual returns for five
      consecutive financial years.
   5. The Tribunal considers it just and equitable to wind up the company.

D. Order of Payment under Section 53 of IBC

The general order of distribution is:

   1. Insolvency resolution process costs and liquidation costs.
   2. Secured creditors who relinquish their security and workmen's dues for 24 months.
   3. Employees' wages and unpaid dues for 12 months.

E. Section 59 – Voluntary Liquidation

This section deals with voluntary liquidation of a corporate person that has not committed a

      Realisation of assets.
      Settlement of liabilities.
      Distribution of proceeds.
      Final report and application for dissolution.
