                       UNDERWRITING OF SHARES
1. Meaning of Underwriting

Underwriting is an agreement under which an individual or financial institution (called an
underwriter) agrees to purchase the shares or debentures that are not subscribed to by the
public.

In simple words, underwriting gives a guarantee to a company that its issue of shares or
debentures will be subscribed to, either by the public or by the underwriters.

Example:

A company issues 1,00,000 shares to the public. The public subscribes to only 80,000 shares.
The underwriter agrees to purchase the remaining 20,000 shares.

Therefore, the underwriter purchases 20,000 shares and helps the company raise the required
capital.

2. Meaning of Underwriter

An underwriter is a person, bank, financial institution or registered intermediary who agrees to
purchase the unsubscribed portion of shares or debentures issued by a company.

Example: Banks, financial institutions and registered underwriting firms may act as
underwriters, subject to applicable regulations.

3. Features of Underwriting

   1.   It is an agreement between a company and an underwriter.
   2.   It provides a guarantee for subscription of securities.
   3.   It helps companies raise the required capital.
   4.   The underwriter receives commission for the service.
   5.   The underwriter takes the risk of purchasing unsubscribed securities.
   6.   It creates confidence among companies issuing shares and debentures.
   7.   Underwriters must follow applicable SEBI rules and regulations.

4. Functions of an Underwriter

   1. Guarantee of subscription: Guarantees subscription of the agreed number of shares
      or debentures.
   2. Assurance of capital: Helps the company raise the required capital.
   3. Public confidence: Creates confidence among investors in the issue.
   4. Marketing of securities: Helps promote and distribute shares and debentures.
   5. Purchase of unsubscribed shares: Purchases securities that remain unsubscribed,
      according to the agreement.
   6. Expert advice: Provides advice regarding the issue and market conditions.
   7. Risk bearing: Takes the risk of securities not being subscribed by the public.
   8. Support to the company: Helps companies, especially new companies, raise funds.

5. Advantages of Underwriting

A. Advantages to the company

   1.   Assured capital: The company gets assurance that the agreed issue will be subscribed.
   2.   Reduces risk: Reduces the risk of an unsuccessful issue.
   3.   Expert guidance: The company can obtain expert advice from underwriters.
   4.   Quick issue: Helps in completing the issue process smoothly.
   5.   Investor confidence: Creates confidence among investors.
   6.   Business expansion: Helps companies raise funds for expansion and development.

B. Advantages to investors

   1. Underwriting may increase confidence in the issue.
   2. It can help improve the credibility of the issue.
   3. Investors may receive better information and guidance through the issue process.

C. Advantages to underwriters

   1. They earn commission for providing underwriting services.
   2. They develop business relationships with companies.
   3. They gain experience in the securities market.

6. Types of Underwriting

Underwriting can be classified into the following types:

A. Complete or Full Underwriting

When the entire issue of shares or debentures is underwritten by one or more underwriters, it
is called complete underwriting.

Example: A company issues 50,000 shares and the entire issue is guaranteed by underwriters.

B. Partial Underwriting

When only a part of the issue is underwritten, it is called partial underwriting.

Example: A company issues 1,00,000 shares, but underwriters guarantee only 60,000 shares.

C. Firm Underwriting

When an underwriter agrees to purchase a fixed number of shares or debentures in addition to
the shares he may have to take up under the underwriting agreement, it is called firm
underwriting.

Example: An underwriter agrees to purchase 5,000 shares on a firm basis, irrespective of
public subscription.

D. Joint Underwriting

When two or more underwriters jointly guarantee an issue of shares or debentures, it is called
joint underwriting.

Example: A company issues 1,00,000 shares. Underwriter A guarantees 40,000 shares and
Underwriter B guarantees 60,000 shares.

E. Syndicate Underwriting

When a group or syndicate of underwriters jointly undertakes the responsibility of underwriting
a large issue, it is called syndicate underwriting.

F. Sub-underwriting

When the main underwriter transfers a part of the underwriting risk to another underwriter, it
is called sub-underwriting.

Example: Underwriter A accepts responsibility for 50,000 shares and gives responsibility for
10,000 shares to Underwriter B.

7. Marked and Unmarked Applications

Applications received from the public are classified into two categories for calculating the
liability of underwriters.

A. Marked Applications

Marked applications are applications that bear the stamp, seal or identification mark of a
particular underwriter.

      They help identify the underwriter who introduced the applicant.
      They are credited to the account of that particular underwriter.
      They reduce the liability of that underwriter.

Example: An application for 1,000 shares carries the stamp of Underwriter A. It is treated as
a marked application of A.

B. Unmarked Applications

Unmarked applications are applications that do not carry the stamp or identification mark of
any underwriter.

      It is not possible to identify which underwriter introduced the applicant.
      They are distributed among underwriters according to the terms of the underwriting
       agreement.
      They reduce the liability of the underwriters in the agreed proportion.

Example: An application for 500 shares is received without the stamp of any underwriter. It is
treated as an unmarked application.

8. Calculation of Underwriter's Liability
The liability of an underwriter is calculated after considering marked and unmarked
applications.

Steps:

   1. Calculate the gross liability of each underwriter.
   2. Deduct the marked applications received by each underwriter.
   3. Distribute the unmarked applications according to the agreement or the applicable
      proportional basis.
   4. Deduct the share of unmarked applications.
   5. The remaining balance is the net liability of each underwriter.

9. Underwriting Commission

Meaning

Underwriting commission is the amount paid by a company to an underwriter for guaranteeing
the subscription of shares or debentures.

It is the remuneration paid for taking the risk of purchasing unsubscribed securities.

Features of Underwriting Commission

   1.    It is paid by the issuing company.
   2.    It is paid to the underwriter for underwriting services.
   3.    It is calculated according to the agreed rate and applicable law.
   4.    It is generally based on the value of securities underwritten.
   5.    It must comply with the Companies Act and applicable SEBI regulations.
   6.    The commission arrangement should be properly disclosed and documented.

10. SEBI Guidelines Regarding Underwriting

SEBI stands for Securities and Exchange Board of India. It regulates the securities market
and protects the interests of investors.

The important rules relating to underwriting are as follows:

   1. Registration: Underwriters must be registered with SEBI under the applicable SEBI
       (Underwriters) Regulations, 1993.
   2. Underwriting agreement: The company and underwriter must enter into an agreement
       specifying the underwriting obligations.
   3. Disclosure: Details of underwriting arrangements must be disclosed in the offer
       document wherever required.
   4. Commission details: The agreement must mention the amount of commission or
       brokerage payable to the underwriter.
   5. Minimum obligations: Lead managers must fulfil the minimum underwriting
       obligations prescribed under the applicable regulations.
   6. Responsibility for unsubscribed shares: Underwriters must subscribe to the agreed
       number of securities when called upon to do so.
   7. Code of conduct: Underwriters must follow the code of conduct prescribed by SEBI.
   8. No additional benefits: An underwriter should not obtain direct or indirect benefits
       from underwriting other than the agreed commission or brokerage.
   9. Financial capacity: Underwriters must maintain the required financial capacity to
       fulfil their underwriting commitments.
   10. Performance of obligations: If an underwriter fails to fulfil the agreement, the
       responsible lead manager must take action according to the applicable regulations.

11. Legal Provisions Regarding Underwriting Commission

Under the Companies Act, 2013:

       Section 40(6): A company may pay commission to a person in connection with
        subscribing or agreeing to subscribe, whether absolutely or conditionally, to its
        securities, subject to prescribed conditions.
       Rule 13 of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014:
        Provides conditions relating to payment of commission.
       The company's Articles of Association must authorise the payment.
       The commission rate and other required particulars must be disclosed in the prospectus.
       The commission must comply with the applicable legal requirements
