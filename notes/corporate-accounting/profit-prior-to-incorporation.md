## Slide 3

What is Pre-Incorporation Profit?
A newly formed company comes into existence legally only after receiving its Certificate of Incorporation.
Often, a company acquires an existing running business from a prior date (usually matching the vendor's last balance sheet date).
The profit earned or loss incurred between the date of purchase and the date of legal incorporation is called Profit or Loss Prior to Incorporation.
Legally, a company cannot earn business profit before its formal legal registration.
Meaning & Legal Context

## Slide 4

Pre-Incorporation Profit
Nature: Capital Profit
• Belongs to the company as capital surplus.
• Cannot be distributed as dividend to shareholders.
• Transferred to Capital Reserve Account.
• Used to write off preliminary expenses, goodwill, or issue discounts.
Post-Incorporation Profit
Nature: Revenue Profit
• Earned after legal existence is established.
• Fully available for dividend distribution.
• Transferred to P&L Appropriation Account.
• Treated as normal revenue profits from operations.
Accounting Nature & Treatment

## Slide 5

Pre-Incorporation Period
Period starting from the Date of Purchase / Acquisition up to the Date of Incorporation.
Post-Incorporation Period
Period starting from the Date of Incorporation up to the Closing Date of the financial year.
Vendor Settlement Period
Period from the acquisition date to the actual date of final discharge of purchase consideration to vendors.
Key Accounting Periods

## Slide 7

Time Ratio
Used for fixed or standing expenses like rent, salaries, rates, insurance, depreciation, and telephone charges.
Sales Ratio
Used for variable selling expenses like commission, bad debts, advertisement, discount allowed, and carriage outwards.
Revised Time Ratio
Used exclusively for apportioning interest paid to vendors from acquisition date until final payment settlement.
