1. Meaning and Definition of HRD
Human Resource Development (HRD) is a planned and continuous process of developing employees'
knowledge, skills, abilities, attitudes and values so that they can contribute effectively to organisational
objectives and achieve personal growth.
In simple words: HRD means improving the abilities and potential of employees for better organisational
performance.
Key points:
• HRD is a continuous process.
• It focuses on employee development.
• It improves organisational effectiveness.
• It builds future capabilities.
2. Objectives of HRD
• Improve employee skills and competencies.
• Increase productivity and performance.
• Develop future leaders.
• Improve employee motivation and satisfaction.

4. Developmental Perspective of HRD
The developmental perspective views employees as people whose knowledge, skills, competencies and
potential can be continuously developed.
• Continuous learning
• Skill and competency development
• Career growth
• Leadership development
• Organisational learning
• Long-term employee and organisational development
5. HRD at Macro and Micro Levels
Macro Level HRD
HRD at the macro level refers to development of human resources at the national or broader societal level.
• Education and skill development
• Vocational training
• Employment generation
• Digital literacy
• National economic development
Micro Level HRD
HRD at the micro level refers to employee development within an organisation.
• Training and development
• Performance appraisal
• Career planning
• Leadership development
• Employee counselling

9. HRD Concepts and Subsystems
HRD includes several interconnected activities designed to improve employee and organisational capabilities.
• Training and Development – improves knowledge and skills.
• Performance Appraisal – evaluates employee performance.
• Career Planning – supports career growth.
• Potential Appraisal – identifies future capabilities.
• Employee Counselling – supports employees with work-related and personal concerns.
• Rewards and Recognition – encourages desired performance.
• Succession Planning – prepares employees for future key positions.
10. Human Resource Planning (HRP)
Human Resource Planning is the process of estimating future human resource requirements and ensuring that
the right people are available at the right place and time.
Objectives
• Forecast manpower needs
• Avoid shortage and surplus
• Improve employee utilisation
• Support recruitment
• Support training and career planning
Steps
1. Analyse organisational objectives.
2. Forecast HR demand.
3. Forecast HR supply.
4. Identify HR gaps.
5. Recruit and select employees.
6. Provide training and development.
7. Evaluate the plan.

11. Potential and Potential Appraisal
Potential means an employee's future capability to perform higher or more complex responsibilities.
Potential appraisal is a systematic process of evaluating an employee's future abilities, leadership qualities,
competencies and growth potential.
Objectives
• Identify future leaders
• Support promotion decisions
• Succession planning
• Career development
• Identify training needs

• Performance review

• 360-degree feedback

12. Assessment Centre
An Assessment Centre is a systematic method of evaluating employees' competencies through simulated job
situations. It is commonly used for recruitment, promotion, leadership development and potential appraisal.
Techniques
• Group Discussion – communication and teamwork.
• Role Play – leadership and interpersonal skills.
• Case Study – problem-solving and analytical ability.
• Presentation – communication and confidence.
• Psychometric Tests – personality and aptitude.
• In-Basket Exercise – decision-making and prioritisation.
Advantages
• Systematic evaluation
• Helps identify future leaders
• Supports better promotion decisions
• Assesses multiple competencies
