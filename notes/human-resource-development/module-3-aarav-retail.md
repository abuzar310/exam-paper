# HRD Module 3 — Aarav Retail Solutions

Summary of Module 3 – HRD Case Study: Aarav Retail Solutions [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Overview is a retail company with 18 stores and about 650 employees. Despite rapid growth, it experienced rising employee turnover, more customer complaints, and declining sales productivity. The case examines how Human Resource Development (HRD) can solve these people-related issues and how its impact can be measured. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Key Problems Identified
Recruitment Issues
Hiring focused mainly on qualifications and experience.
Little attention was given to aptitude, customer-handling ability, teamwork, adaptability, and problem-solving potential.
Result: Employees had knowledge but lacked practical capability. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Knowledge and Skill Gaps
Employees struggled with: 
Customer complaint handling
Product explanation
Cross-selling and upselling
CRM usage
Professional communication
The company realized that knowledge must be effectively applied through skills and behavior. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Values Problem
Some employees prioritized sales targets over ethics.
Important organizational values such as integrity, honesty, fairness, accountability, teamwork, and customer orientation were not strongly communicated. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Human Relations Issues
Poor teamwork and communication between employees and departments.
Lack of listening, cooperation, appreciation, and conflict resolution.
Needed skills included communication, empathy, teamwork, negotiation, and emotional intelligence. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Responsiveness Problem
Complaint resolution time increased from 24 to 72 hours.
Employees avoided responsibility due to fear of blame.
Greater ownership and responsiveness were required. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Declining Loyalty and Commitment
High turnover, especially within the first 18 months.
Causes: 
Lack of recognition
Poor career growth opportunities
Weak communication
No mentoring system
Unclear promotion criteria [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Lack of Transparency
Employees did not understand promotions, appraisal scores, and rewards.
This reduced trust and perceptions of fairness. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Leadership Shortage
Few employees were ready for managerial roles.
The company had to recruit managers externally instead of promoting internally. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]

HRD Solutions Implemented
The company launched the“Aarav People Growth Initiative” with an investment of ₹12 lakh. Key initiatives included: [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Better aptitude-based recruitment
Product, process, and CRM training
Ethics and integrity workshops
Communication and teamwork training
Customer-service training
Career development and recognition programs
Transparent appraisal and promotion systems
Coaching, mentoring, and leadership projects [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]

HR Evaluation Methods
Human Resource Accounting (HRA)
Measures investment in employees.
Example employee investment: 
Recruitment: ₹8,000
Selection: ₹4,000
Induction: ₹5,000
Training: ₹15,000
Development: ₹8,000
Total: ₹40,000 [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
HR Audit
Examines HR systems such as:
Recruitment and selection
Training and development
Performance appraisal
Compensation
Employee relations
Succession planning
HR analytics and records [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Audit findings showed weaknesses in recruitment, training evaluation, promotion criteria, grievance tracking, and performance discussions. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Benchmarking Results (After 12 Months)
Employee turnover: 24% → 17%
Complaint resolution time: 72 hours → 38 hours
Training hours per employee: 6 → 18
Internal promotions: 18% → 31%
Customer satisfaction: 76% → 84% [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]

HRD Impact Assessment
The program was evaluated at four levels:
Learning
Knowledge scores improved from 58% to 86%. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Behavior
Better communication, teamwork, listening, and complaint handling. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Performance
Improvements tracked through productivity, turnover, absenteeism, and complaints. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Business Results 
Revenue, profitability, customer retention, and satisfaction were monitored. [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]

ROI of HRD Program
HRD Investment = ₹12 lakh
Estimated Benefit = ₹18 lakh
ROI Formula = (Benefits − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100
ROI = 50% [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Non-financial benefits included:
Better teamwork
Stronger leadership pipeline
Improved communication
Higher employee confidence
Better organizational culture [Module_3_H...utions.pdf]
Main Takeaway
The case shows that organizational success depends not only on employee knowledge and skills but also on aptitude, values, human relations, responsiveness, commitment, transparency, and leadership development. Effective HRD should be continuously measured through Human Resource Accounting, HR Audits, Benchmarking, Impact Assessment, and ROI analysis to connect people development with business result
