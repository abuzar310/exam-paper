## Summary of the lesson

3. ON THE WAY TO GOREGAON
SUMMARY OF THE LESSON 
Family Background
1. The narrator’s family was originally from Dapoli, Ratnagiri (Bombay Presidency).
2. His forefathers had left their traditional occupation and served in the East India Company’s Army.
3. His father also served in the army and retired as a Subhedar.
4. After retirement, the family moved to Satara around 1901.
Situation at Satara
5. The narrator’s mother had passed away.
6. His father worked as a cashier at Koregaon (Satara District) on a famine relief project.
7. The children (narrator, his brother, and nephews) lived with their handicapped aunt, who could not cook or manage the house.
8. The children cooked for themselves—mostly pulav (rice with meat), as they could not make bread.
The Journey Begins
9. During summer vacation, the father invited them to Koregaon.
10. The children were excited to travel by train for the first time.
11. They bought new clothes, caps, shoes, and dhoties for the trip.
12. They hired a tonga (horse-cart) to go 10 miles to the railway station.
13. They were given pocket money and enjoyed lemonade at the station.
14. They boarded the train and got down at Masur station in the evening.
At Masur Station:
15. Nobody came to receive them, though their father had promised to send someone.
16. The station-master was kind at first and assumed they were Brahmin children.
17. When the narrator revealed they were Mahars (untouchables), his attitude changed suddenly.
18. No bullock-cart driver agreed to take them because they were untouchables, even when offered double fare.
19. Finally, one cartman agreed on the condition that the children themselves would drive the cart, while he walked beside it.
On the Way to Koregaon
20. They stopped at a dry river to eat food brought from home but could not drink the dirty water.
21. The cartman delayed returning from his meal, making them anxious.
22. After some time, the cartman himself sat in the cart, forgetting his earlier fear of “pollution.”
23. Darkness fell, and the children became frightened.
24. They suspected the cartman of tricking them for their gold ornaments.
25. They cried and begged him to take them safely.
26. At midnight, they reached a toll-collector’s hut at the foot of a hill.
The Night Struggle
27. Hungry and thirsty, they asked for water.
28. On the cartman’s advice, they pretended to be Muslims, but the toll-collector still refused to give water.
29. They had food but could not eat without water.
30. They slept hungry in the cart, keeping watch in turns out of fear.
Reaching Koregaon
31. Next morning, they continued and reached Koregaon by 11 a.m.
32. Their father was surprised, saying he had never received their letter.
33. It was later found that his servant had hidden the letter.
Realization about Untouchability
34. At school, the narrator already faced discrimination:
Could not sit with other children.
Had to bring his own gunny cloth to sit on.
Could not touch the school tap; needed the peon to open it. (“No peon, no water”).
35. At home:
Sisters washed clothes because washermen refused.
Sisters cut hair because barbers refused.
36. The journey incident, however, gave the narrator his first big shock about untouchability.
37. It made him think deeply about the cruelty and injustice of caste discrimination.
👉 This event left a lifelong impression on him at the age of nine.

### 3. Trace the circumstances leading to Ambedkar's train journey to Goregaon.

Introduction: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's journey to Goregaon is one of the most important incidents in his autobiographical essay On the Way to Goregaon. This journey became a painful experience that exposed him to the cruel reality of untouchability.
Circumstances leading to the journey: During his childhood, Ambedkar and his siblings were living away from their father. Their father, Ramji Sakpal, was working at Koregaon. The children were eager to meet him during their holidays.
Their father had been informed about their proposed visit and was expected to receive them at the railway station. The children were excited about the journey because they had not seen him for some time.
Ambedkar and his siblings dressed themselves in good clothes and started their journey by train. They carried their luggage and provisions for the journey. They were happy and expected a pleasant reunion with their father.
However, an unexpected problem occurred. Their father had not received the letter informing him about their arrival. His servant had hidden it. Therefore, he was not present at the railway station to receive them.
When they reached Masur railway station, they found themselves alone in an unfamiliar place. They had to arrange transportation to reach Goregaon.
This simple journey soon turned into a painful experience because the children belonged to the Mahar community, which was treated as untouchable by the caste-ridden society.
Conclusion: Thus, a journey undertaken with happiness and excitement became a turning point in young Ambedkar's life. It exposed him to caste discrimination and made him understand the injustice faced by people belonging to oppressed communities.

### 4. Briefly narrate young Ambedkar's experiences at Masur railway station.

Introduction: The experience of young Ambedkar at Masur railway station is a significant incident in On the Way to Goregaon. It reveals the inhuman treatment given to people belonging to the so-called lower castes.
Experience at the station: When Ambedkar and his siblings arrived at Masur railway station, they expected their father to receive them. Unfortunately, their father was not there because he had not received their letter. His servant had hidden it.
The children were confused and worried. They did not know how to travel from Masur to Goregaon. They were dressed neatly and carried their luggage.
The station master noticed the children and asked them about their destination and family background. At first, he was kind and helpful. When he came to know that they belonged to the Mahar community, his attitude changed.
The children faced difficulty in finding a cart driver who was willing to take them to Goregaon. The cart drivers refused to transport them because they considered them untouchables.
The children were innocent and could not understand why people treated them differently. They had money to pay for the journey, but their caste became an obstacle.
Finally, after considerable difficulty, a cartman agreed to take them under certain conditions.
Conclusion: The Masur railway station incident shows how deeply caste discrimination was rooted in society. Ambedkar's experience reveals that even innocent children were denied basic human dignity because of their birth.

### 5. Briefly narrate young Ambedkar's journey to Goregaon by cart from Masur.

Introduction: Ambedkar's journey from Masur railway station to Goregaon was filled with difficulties and humiliation. This incident exposed the cruelty of untouchability and the inhuman behaviour of caste-conscious people.
The journey by cart: After reaching Masur railway station, Ambedkar and the other children waited for their father or his servant. Nobody arrived to receive them. They decided to hire a bullock cart to reach Goregaon.
However, the cart drivers refused to carry them because they belonged to the Mahar community. Even though the children were ready to pay double the fare, nobody agreed to take them.
Finally, one cart driver agreed on a condition. The children had to drive the cart themselves, while he would walk alongside it. The children accepted the condition because they desperately wanted to reach their father.
Initially, they were happy that their journey had started. But as darkness surrounded them, they became frightened. There were no streetlights, houses or people on the road. They began to worry that the cart driver might take them somewhere dangerous.
After travelling for several hours, they reached a toll collector's hut. They hoped to get some food and water. However, they faced further humiliation because of their caste. They could not get water, even though they had food with them.
They were forced to spend the night near a hill without eating. The children were frightened and exhausted. Early the next morning, the cart driver suggested continuing the journey. The children refused to start immediately and waited until eight o'clock. Finally, they reached Goregaon at about eleven in the morning.
Conclusion: The cart journey was a painful experience for young Ambedkar. It taught him how untouchability deprived people of basic necessities and human dignity. This incident remained an unforgettable memory throughout his life.

### 6. How does young Ambedkar experience caste discrimination at Masur railway station?

Introduction: In the essay On the Way to Goregaon, Ambedkar describes the discrimination he experienced at Masur railway station. This incident clearly shows the deep-rooted caste prejudice in Indian society.
Discrimination at the railway station: When Ambedkar and his siblings arrived at Masur railway station, they were waiting for their father or his servant. Nobody came to receive them. The station master noticed the children and approached them to find out why they were waiting.
The children were well dressed and spoke politely. Therefore, the station master initially believed that they belonged to a high-caste family. He felt sympathy for them and wanted to help them.
However, when he asked about their caste, young Ambedkar innocently replied that they belonged to the Mahar community. Immediately, the station master's attitude changed. He was shocked and moved away from them.
This sudden change in behaviour surprised the children. They realised that their caste identity was more important to people than their appearance, education or behaviour.
The station master eventually returned and tried to arrange a bullock cart for them. But the cart drivers refused to carry them because they considered it a violation of their ritual purity to transport untouchables.
Even when the children offered double the fare, nobody agreed. Thus, they experienced humiliation and rejection at a very young age.
Conclusion: The incident at Masur railway station reveals the cruelty of caste discrimination. Ambedkar understood that society judged people not by their character but by their birth. This experience strengthened his determination to fight against untouchability and social inequality.

### 7. Explain briefly how Ambedkar experienced ritual purity and caste hierarchy in his journey to Goregaon.

### 8. What lessons did Ambedkar learn from his experiences at Goregaon? Explain.

Introduction: Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's journey to Goregaon was one of the most unforgettable experiences of his childhood. Though he was only a young boy, the incidents taught him important lessons about caste discrimination, social injustice and human dignity.
Lessons learnt from the experience
1. Caste discrimination is inhuman: Ambedkar understood that people were treated differently based on their caste. Even innocent children were humiliated because they belonged to the Mahar community.
2. Money cannot overcome caste prejudice: The children were willing to pay double the fare, but the cart drivers refused to carry them. This showed that caste prejudice was stronger than financial considerations.
3. Denial of basic human rights: The children could not obtain drinking water because of their caste. Ambedkar realised that untouchability denied people even their basic necessities.
4. Lack of human sympathy: The people they met failed to show kindness and compassion. Their behaviour revealed how social customs had destroyed human values.
5. Importance of equality: Ambedkar realised that all human beings deserve equal treatment, irrespective of their caste, religion or social position.
6. Need to fight social injustice: These experiences made him understand the importance of challenging the caste system and fighting for the rights of oppressed communities.
7. Importance of education: Ambedkar strongly believed that education was necessary for the progress and empowerment of oppressed people. His later life reflected this belief.
8. Human dignity is essential: Every individual deserves respect and freedom. No person should be considered inferior because of birth.
Conclusion: The journey to Goregaon left a permanent impression on Ambedkar's mind. It opened his eyes to the cruel realities of Indian society. These childhood experiences inspired him to become a great social reformer and fight throughout his life for equality, justice, liberty and human dignity.
