What is a folktale? How far is Kirubunkal a folktale?
Introduction to the Author and the Novella
Ravikumar Neha is a well-known contemporary Kannada writer. His writings beautifully combine folklore, myths, history, and modern life. He presents ordinary village life in an interesting way and gives importance to the oral storytelling tradition of Karnataka.
Kirubunkal is a modern Kannada novella based on the life and beliefs of rural people. The story combines traditional folktales with modern narration. Through the characters and stories, the author shows how folklore continues to influence people’s lives and culture.
What is a Folktale?
A folktale is a traditional story passed from one generation to another by word of mouth. These stories belong to the common people and reflect their customs, beliefs, values, and imagination. Folktales usually include kings, queens, animals, spirits, magical events, heroes, and wise old people. They entertain people and also teach moral lessons.
Folktales do not usually have one known author because they are created and preserved by the community. Every generation may add or change some parts of the story.
Kirubunkal as a Folktale
Kirubunkal has many qualities of a folktale.
First, the story is based on oral storytelling. Characters narrate stories that they have heard from their elders. This shows how stories travel from one generation to another.
Secondly, the novella contains myths, legends, and village beliefs. These stories are connected with local traditions and cultural practices. The people believe in old customs and supernatural powers.
Another important feature is the presence of Hirikateyajji, who represents the traditional storyteller. She preserves the old tales and shares them with the younger generation. Through her, the ancient culture remains alive.
The language of the novella is also simple and close to everyday speech. The storytelling style resembles the way elders narrate stories in villages.
The characters respect nature, traditions, and community life, which are common themes in folktales.
Modern Elements
Although Kirubunkal has many features of a folktale, it is also a modern novella. Ravikumar Neha uses modern narration, realistic characters, and psychological depth. The story discusses modern issues such as changing lifestyles, identity, and the influence of urban culture.
The author does not simply repeat old stories but interprets them in a modern way.
Conclusion
Thus, Kirubunkal is largely a folk tale in spirit because it preserves oral traditions, village culture, myths, and legends. At the same time, it is a modern literary work that connects traditional folklore with present-day life. Ravikumar Neha successfully keeps the rich heritage of folklore alive through this novella.

Show how Ravikumar Neha combines the elements of two traditions in his story Kirubunkal.
Introduction to the Author and the Novella
Ravikumar Neha is an important Kannada writer who skillfully blends traditional folklore with modern literature. His novella Kirubunkal reflects both the rich cultural heritage of villages and the changing world of modern society.
Two Traditions in Kirubunkal
The two traditions in the novella are:
The traditional folk culture
The modern way of thinking
The author combines these two traditions throughout the story.
Folk Tradition
The traditional side is seen through oral storytelling, myths, village customs, beliefs, and legends. Characters like Hirikateyajji preserve ancient stories and pass them to younger generations. These stories show the wisdom and values of village life.
The people respect elders, nature, and cultural traditions. Festivals, rituals, and local beliefs are important parts of their lives.
Modern Tradition
The modern side is represented by educated people who question old beliefs. Characters like Seena want to appear modern and fashionable. They are influenced by city life, modern education, and new ideas.
Modern thinking brings curiosity, scientific ideas, and changing lifestyles.
Combination of Both Traditions
Ravikumar Neha never presents one tradition as completely right or wrong. Instead, he brings both together.
The folk stories become meaningful even in modern society because they preserve cultural identity. Modern thinking helps people understand life differently without rejecting the past completely.
The author uses modern literary techniques while telling traditional stories. This makes the novella unique and attractive to modern readers.
The storytelling itself becomes a bridge between the past and the present.
Importance
This combination teaches readers that society grows by respecting both tradition and progress. Old stories help people understand their roots, while modern ideas help them face the future.
Conclusion
Kirubunkal successfully combines folk tradition with modern literature. Ravikumar Neha shows that both traditions are necessary for a balanced society. The novella celebrates cultural heritage while accepting social change.

How do tales travel? What is the tale of Hirikateyajji?
Introduction to the Author and the Novella
Ravikumar Neha’s Kirubunkal celebrates the rich oral tradition of Karnataka. The novella shows how stories connect generations and preserve culture. One of the most memorable characters is Hirikateyajji, the elderly storyteller.
How Do Tales Travel?
Tales travel mainly through oral tradition.
Grandparents tell stories to children.
Parents share stories with their families.
Storytellers narrate tales during village gatherings and festivals.
Travellers carry stories from one place to another.
As stories move from one generation to another, small changes are added, but the main message remains the same.
In this way, folktales survive for hundreds of years.
The author shows that stories are not just entertainment. They preserve history, culture, values, and collective memory.

Hirikateyajji is an old woman who knows many ancient stories. She becomes the guardian of traditional knowledge.
Her stories are filled with imagination, village history, beliefs, heroes, and moral lessons.
She tells stories naturally, just as elders have done for centuries.
Through her storytelling, children and young people learn about their ancestors and cultural traditions.
Hirikateyajji symbolizes the living memory of the village.
Even though modern life is changing rapidly, her stories continue to inspire people.
She reminds everyone that culture survives only when stories are remembered and shared.
Importance of Hirikateyajji
Hirikateyajji represents the voice of tradition. She connects the past with the present. Her storytelling protects the cultural heritage of the community.
The author respects her wisdom and shows that oral literature is as valuable as written literature.
Conclusion
Tales travel through oral storytelling from one generation to another. Hirikateyajji is the symbol of this living tradition. Through her character, Ravikumar Neha highlights the importance of preserving folklore, cultural memory, and the wisdom of the elders. Kirubunkal reminds readers that stories are the true treasure of every society.
What makes Hirikateyajji come to the conclusion, “The fort of tales must grow”?
Introduction to the Author and the Novella
Ravikumar Neha is a famous contemporary Kannada writer. His works focus on village life, folklore, oral traditions, and the changing values of modern society. He combines imagination with reality and presents the richness of Karnataka’s cultural heritage.
Kirubunkal is a novella that celebrates the importance of storytelling. It shows how folktales preserve the history, culture, and identity of a community. Through the character of Hirikateyajji, the author highlights the value of oral tradition.
Hirikateyajji is the oldest storyteller in the village. She has spent her life listening to and narrating stories. She believes that stories are the greatest wealth of a community.
As she grows old, she notices that the younger generation is becoming more interested in modern life than in traditional tales. They spend less time listening to elders and more time following new ways of living. This worries Hirikateyajji.
She realizes that if stories are forgotten, people will also forget their culture, traditions, and ancestors. Therefore, she says, “The fort of tales must grow.”
The “fort of tales” is a symbol of the rich collection of stories that protect the identity of the village. Just as a fort protects people from enemies, stories protect society from losing its culture and values.
Hirikateyajji encourages everyone to collect, remember, and share stories. Every new story strengthens this symbolic fort. She believes that storytelling should continue from one generation to another.
Her statement also suggests that stories bring people together. They teach moral values, entertain listeners, and preserve the wisdom of the past.
Ravikumar Neha uses this idea to remind readers that modern development should not destroy traditional culture.
Conclusion
Hirikateyajji concludes that “The fort of tales must grow” because stories preserve the identity, culture, and memory of the community. Through her, the author emphasizes the importance of protecting folklore for future generations.

The Puranapunya tale is unique because of its sacred and religious nature. Its aloofness reflects its spiritual importance. Through this tale, Ravikumar Neha shows the diversity of storytelling traditions in village culture.
