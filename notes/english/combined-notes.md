## 01. The Overcoat – Summary (Easy 10-Mark Answer)
### About the Author
Nikolai Gogol (1809–1852) was a Russian writer known for his satirical and
humorous style. His works often criticize social injustice and bureaucracy in
Russia.
### Summary
The Overcoat is one of Nikolai Gogol’s most famous short stories. It tells the
tragic and touching story of Akaky Akakievich, a poor government clerk in St.
Petersburg. Through his life, Gogol highlights the problems of poverty, social
injustice, and the cruelty of the bureaucratic system.

Akaky Akakievich is a simple and innocent man who works as a copyist. He has
no interest in anything except copying official documents. His life is very dull and
lonely, and he is regularly mocked by his colleagues. He accepts all insults quietly
without complaining, which shows his helplessness and gentle nature. He earns
very little money and lives in extreme poverty.

The main event of the story begins when Akaky’s old overcoat becomes
completely worn out. The winter in St. Petersburg is harsh, and he desperately
needs a warm coat. When the tailor Petrovich tells him that the coat cannot be
repaired and he must get a new one, Akaky becomes worried because he cannot
afford it. However, he decides to save money by cutting down his expenses. For
months, he sacrifices small comforts such as food, candles, and tea. His
determination shows how important the overcoat becomes in his life.

When the new overcoat is finally made, it brings Akaky great joy. His colleagues
admire it and even throw a small celebration in his honour. For the first time, he
feels noticed and respected. But this happiness does not last long. On the night of
the celebration, two thieves steal his overcoat.

Akaky tries to seek help from the police and even approaches a high-ranking
official, but no one helps him. The “Important Person” even scolds him harshly,
which shocks Akaky deeply.

The loss of the overcoat breaks his spirit. He falls sick and dies soon after. Later,
his ghost appears on the streets, taking overcoats from others, including the
Important Person. This supernatural ending symbolizes society’s guilt.

Gogol uses Akaky’s tragic life to criticize the insensitivity of society and the
corruption of officials. He shows that even a small act of kindness could save a
life, while cruelty can destroy a person completely. The story reminds us of the importance of compassion and human dignity.

### Themes
a) Poverty and Struggle, b) Social Injustice, c) Human Dignity, d) Bureaucracy
and Power Abuse, e) Supernatural Element.

## II. Character Sketch of Akaky Akakievich Bashmachkin

Akaky Akakievich is the central character of Nikolai Gogol’s The Overcoat. He is
a poor government clerk who works as a copyist. His life is simple and
monotonous. He enjoys copying documents and finds peace in his work. Akaky
has no friends, no family, and no social life, which shows his lonely existence.

He is extremely humble, shy, and gentle. He never argues with anyone, even when
his colleague’s insult or mock him. Instead of reacting, he quietly continues his
work. This shows his patience and innocent nature. He is also honest and
hardworking, but his sincerity is not appreciated in the office.

Akaky lives in poverty. His clothes are old, his room is bare, and he eats very
little because he earns a very small salary. His old overcoat becomes a big
problem in the cold winter. When he decides to buy a new one, it becomes the
only excitement of his life. His dedication to saving every small coin for the
overcoat shows his determination and sacrifice.

The new overcoat brings Akaky rare moments of joy and respect. For the first
time, his colleagues notice him and treat him kindly. But this happiness is shortlived, as the overcoat is stolen, and Akaky receives no help from the officials. The
loss leads to his emotional breakdown and death.

Akaky’s character symbolizes the suffering of the poor and the cruelty of society.
Through him, Gogol shows how an ordinary, harmless man can be destroyed by
neglect and injustice.

## III. Character Sketch of Petrovich (the tailor)

Petrovich is the tailor who stitche Akaky’s new overcoat. He is an important
minor character in the story. He is described as a skilled but moody tailor. He
often drinks, which affects his behaviour, and sometimes he argues with his
customers.

When Akaky brings his old overcoat for repair, Petrovich honestly explains that
it cannot be fixed and a new one must be made. This shows that he is
straightforward and practical. He charges a high amount for stitching the
overcoat, which seems unfair, but it also reflects the economic condition of the time and the exploitation faced by the poor.

Despite his rough behaviour, Petrovich completes the overcoat with great care.
The new overcoat becomes a symbol of hope and happiness in Akaky’s life.
Petrovich’s role is small but meaningful because he indirectly brings the only joy
that Akaky experiences.

## IV. Themes in The Overcoat

Nikolai Gogol’s The Overcoat is not only a story about a poor clerk and his stolen
coat; it is a powerful work rich in themes that criticise society, expose human
suffering, and highlight the need for compassion. The story deals with several
major themes such as poverty, social injustice, bureaucratic cruelty, human
dignity, and the supernatural.

One of the strongest themes in the story is poverty. Akaky Akakievich lives a life
of extreme hardship. His salary is so small that even buying a new overcoat
becomes a life-changing struggle. Gogol shows how poverty traps people and
forces them to sacrifice their basic needs. Akaky’s months of saving and suffering
for a simple coat represent the harsh reality of the lower class.

Another important theme is social injustice and cruelty. Throughout the story,
Akaky is ignored, insulted, and mistreated. His colleagues mock him for their
entertainment, and no one supports him. Society sees him as insignificant
because he is poor. Even when his overcoat is stolen, the authorities show no
concern. This highlights how the poor are often denied justice and respect.

The story also criticises the bureaucratic system. The “Important Person”
represents the arrogance of those in power. Instead of helping Akaky, he scolds
him and uses his position to show superiority. Gogol mocks the Russian
bureaucracy by showing how officials care more about their status than the
people they are supposed to serve.

A key thematic idea in the story is human dignity. For Akaky, the overcoat is not
just a piece of clothing; it is a symbol of hope, confidence, and respect. The way
people treat him differently after he gets the new coat shows how society values
appearance more than character. When the coat is taken away, Akaky loses all
dignity and hope, which leads to his death.

Finally, the supernatural element at the end adds a symbolic theme. Akaky’s
ghost stealing overcoats shows the return of justice in a dark, ironic way. It
suggests that the cruelty and neglect of society do not disappear—they come back
to haunt the guilty.

Through these themes, Gogol reveals the pain of ordinary people and teaches the
importance of kindness, empathy, and humanity.

## 02. Kirubunkal – Long Answer (10 Marks)
“Kirubunkal,” written by Dr. Ravikumar Neha, is a powerful story that
combines reality, imagination, village gossip, and human psychology. The story
exposes how rumours and false tales can destroy families and disturb the cultural
harmony of a community. It also highlights how modern technology and
changing values affect relationships.

The story revolves around Seena (Sreenivasa), a careless young man who is
addicted to mobile phones and reels. He compares his dark-skinned wife with the
fair and attractive girls he watches online, which shows the influence of
unrealistic digital images on the minds of today’s youth. Seena’s parents, Eeri
and Mudli, represent hardworking rural people who struggle to maintain dignity
in society.

The turning point of the story is the mysterious death of Mudli, who is found
dead near Chandranna’s farm. No one knows the truth, but villagers quickly
invent their own versions of the story. Every person adds imagination and turns
the incident into gossip. Some say Mudli insulted Chandranna’s family; some
accuse him of an affair; others blame Chandranna for murder. These rumours
show how society enjoys lies more than truth.

An interesting and unique feature of the story is that the tales themselves become
characters. They complain that human beings no longer create meaningful
stories based on experience, but only spread lies, dirty jokes, and sensational
rumours. The tales decide to take revenge by creating even more confusing
stories that make society unstable.

The Kirubunkal tree, an ancient and sacred tree, symbolizes the traditional
culture of storytelling. It observes how human values are declining and warns
that society is slowly losing kindness, unity, and truthfulness.
Through these elements, “Kirubunkal” teaches that rumours can destroy lives,
technology must be used responsibly, and traditional storytelling should be
protected. The story encourages readers to think critically, to respect truth, and
to preserve moral values in society.

Themes in the story: 1. Theme of Rumour, Gossip, and False Tales,
2. Dehumanisation in Modern Society,  3. Caste, Class, and Oppression,
4. Corruption of Traditional Culture, 5. Symbolism of Kirubunkal,
6. Breakdown of Moral and Social Values, 7. Psychological Decline of Seena.

Conclusion: Kirubunkal is not just a story about a village—It is a critique of the entire modern world, where: truth is replaced by lies, culture is replaced by cheap
entertainment, technology controls human minds, caste and power destroy justice, and
old storytelling traditions are dying. Through powerful symbols and raw language, the
story warns that unless society changes, both humans and their culture will continue to collapse.

## II. Short Notes on for 05/10 marks

## 1. Seena’s Obsession in Life

Seena grows up as a lonely and confused young man after the mysterious death
of his father, Mudli. Raised by his mother Eeri, he develops an unhealthy
attachment to his mobile phone. Instead of involving himself in real life,
relationships, or responsibilities, Seena spends all his time watching reels, videos
of beautiful girls, and pornographic content. This makes him dissatisfied with his
dark-skinned wife Shalini and even with himself. His obsession with fair skin and
beauty leads him to constantly wash his face, hoping to become fair. The phone
becomes his escape from reality, and he stops caring about family, marriage, or
village life. He behaves like a child even after marriage, sharing intimate details
of his life openly. Seena’s obsession symbolises how technology and fantasy
distance young people from reality, pushing them into illusion and
irresponsibility.

## 2. Indian Tradition of Telling Tales

The story highlights the rich Indian tradition of storytelling, where tales—called
“kathe” or “stories”—become part of the community’s living culture. In the
village, tales are not just entertainment; they shape beliefs, identity, and
collective memory. Elders like Hirikateyajji carried these tales from one
generation to another, giving life to nature, people, and culture. Tales travelled
across villages, changing form over time, mixing truth and imagination. They
explained strange events, moral issues, and social relations. However, in
Kirubunkal, the sacred tradition weakens. Modern influences, false rumours,
and sensational stories replace meaningful tales. Instead of wisdom, tales start
spreading fear, lust, division, and lies. Through talking tales and mythical
elements, the story shows how India’s storytelling heritage once guided society
but is now threatened by misinformation and loss of values.

## 3. Eeri’s Life After Mudli’s Death

After Mudli’s mysterious and violent death, Eeri’s life becomes filled with
hardship and loneliness. As a widow, she must raise Seena single-handedly. She
deeply loves her son because he is the only memory of her dead husband. Eeri
works hard to fulfil all of Seena’s wishes, even buying him a mobile phone,
hoping it will make him happy. She hopes for government compensation after
Mudli’s death, but it never arrives, adding to her worries. Her life becomes
centered entirely around caring for Seena, who gradually becomes irresponsible
and mentally unstable. Later, she arranges Seena’s marriage to Shalini, hoping
marriage will bring stability, but Seena continues his unhealthy habits. Eeri
silently suffers as village tales accuse her of immoral relationships, damaging her
dignity. Her life represents the struggles of widows in rural India, who face
poverty, gossip, and emotional burden without any support.

## 4. Attack on Mudli

Mudli’s death forms the central mystery of Kirubunkal. On the dark night of the new moon, when he goes searching for his missing son Seena, he hears strange
cries and moves towards Kirabunkal. Later that night, he is attacked by a group
of men—followers of Sahukar Chandranna. They beat him brutally, leaving him
speechless and half-dead. The reason for the attack is unclear, but many
contradictory tales spread across the village: some claim Mudli had an affair
with Chandranna’s wife, others say he misbehaved with Chandranna’s daughter
Vimli, and some say Chandranna was involved with Eeri. These tales create
multiple possibilities, showing how truth gets buried under rumours. Mudli
collapses near Chandranna’s farm and dies. His death becomes the starting point
for new tales, gossip, and fear in the village. The attack symbolises violence
caused by caste politics, rumours, and power imbalance.

## 5. Importance of Hirikateyajji

Hirikateyajji is portrayed as the oldest and most respected elder among the
“tales.” She represents wisdom, tradition, and the ancient storytelling culture of
the village. While the other tales begin to spread sensational, immoral, and
harmful stories, Hirikateyajji recognises the danger these new tales pose to society. She speaks with authority and concern, reminding the tales of their
original duty—to guide humans, bring harmony, and create meaningful stories.
Her warnings express the fear that the village is losing its moral compass. She
also approaches Kirabunkal, the sacred origin of tales, seeking solutions to restore the village’s lost values. Through her character, the text conveys the
message that elders and traditional knowledge are necessary to maintain balance
in society. Hirikateyajji becomes a symbolic grandmother figure who tries to protect culture from being destroyed by modern distortions and irresponsible
storytelling.

## 6. Importance of Sahukar Chandranna

Sahukar Chandranna is an influential and powerful figure in the village. His
presence shapes many events in the story, especially the mysterious death of
Mudli. Several tales revolve around him—some accuse him of having an affair
with Eeri, others say Mudli was involved with his wife or daughter, and some
tales even imply he killed Mudli to protect his family’s honour. Although the real
truth is never known, Chandranna’s social status allows him to manipulate
narratives and hide facts.
He uses the village’s fear during the sickness outbreak to claim Mudli died of
disease, quickly burying him without proper rituals. This makes him a symbol of
how people in power can control stories, spread misinformation, and influence
public belief. Chandranna represents authority, fear, and the role of upper-class
men in shaping the fate of weaker families like Eeri’s. His character exposes the
injustice in rural power structures.

7. The Tales of the Village

In Kirubunkal, tales are personified as living beings who move through the
village, interact with each other, and influence human lives. Earlier, tales were
meaningful, moral, and imaginative, giving life to the land and shaping cultural
identity. They helped people understand their world. But over time, the tales
change. They become sensational, vulgar, and harmful—focusing on rumours,
sexual fantasies, and false accusations. They confuse truth and lies, causing fear,
conflict, and misunderstanding among the villagers. The death of Mudli becomes
a fertile ground for new tales to be born every day. These tales invade the lives of
Mudli, Eeri, Chandranna, and Vimli, destroying their dignity. The text shows
how stories can uplift society or corrupt it, depending on how they are used. The
village becomes a factory of false storytelling, reflecting today's world of
misinformation and viral content.

## 8. Morality and the Village Tales

The story highlights how village tales shape the community’s morality. Earlier,
tales carried wisdom, ethical lessons, and respect for nature and people. They
preserved cultural values. However, in the modern context of the story, morality
collapses. Tales no longer teach good behaviour; instead, they spread lies, lust,
and violence. They distort reality to entertain people, satisfying hidden desires
rather than guiding them. False narratives lead to moral chaos in the village—
people judge Eeri, imagine sinful affairs, and gossip without concern for truth.
Men fantasize about women mentioned in tales, and women fear for their safety
and dignity. The tales show how morality weakens when a community loses
empathy, responsibility, and truth. Through this shift, the author criticizes how
modern society replaces genuine stories with corruption, fake news, and
sensational gossip, leading to the decline of social values.

9. The Village Life in ‘Kirubunkal’

Village life in Kirubunkal is portrayed as a mix of tradition, poverty,
superstition, and gossip. The village depends heavily on stories and rumours to understand events, especially mysterious ones like Mudli’s death. People’s lives
are deeply influenced by caste hierarchy, economic inequality, and social

tales, celebrations, and old customs, but it is slowly collapsing under modern
pressures and the spread of harmful gossip. The story reveals both the charm
and the dark side of rural Indian life.

## 10. Indian Ethos and Folktale

Kirubunkal reflects core Indian ethos through its deep connection with folktales,
communal storytelling, nature, and moral lessons. Indian culture traditionally
views storytelling as a sacred art that teaches values, preserves heritage, and
strengthens community bonds. The personification of tales and Kirabunkal, the
ancient tree, highlights the Indian belief that nature and stories hold spiritual
power. Folktales often mix the real and the supernatural, just as the story blends
everyday life with mythical elements. The text also reflects Indian ethos through
themes like respect for elders, social hierarchy, belief in destiny, and the
importance of preserving truth. However, the story also critiques the loss of this
ethos in modern times. When tales turn immoral or misleading, society loses
direction. Thus, Kirubunkal shows both the richness of Indian folktale tradition
and the danger of forgetting its original purpose—guiding people toward wisdom
and harmony.

## 03. On the Way to Goregaon – Summary (10 Marks)

“On the Way to Goregaon” is an autobiographical incident from Dr. B. R.
Ambedkar’s childhood. It describes the painful experience he and his brothers
faced due to caste discrimination during a journey.

Ambedkar begins by introducing his family. His ancestors worked in the East
India Company’s army, and his father was a Subhedar in the British Indian
Army. His mother had passed away, so Ambedkar and his siblings were taken
care of by their aunt in Satara. Although she was physically disabled, she lovingly
looked after the children.

During their summer vacation, Ambedkar’s father, who worked in Koregaon,
asked the children to visit him. They were very excited because they had never
travelled by train before. Their aunt prepared food and made new clothes for
them. A tonga took them to the railway station, and they boarded the train to
Masur.

However, when they reached Masur, their difficulties began. No one came to receive them. The station master initially behaved kindly, thinking they were
Brahmin children. But the moment Ambedkar revealed that they were Mahars
(considered untouchables), the station master’s behaviour changed completely.
He walked away and refused to help them.

The children tried to hire a bullock cart to go to Koregaon. But every cartman
refused to carry them because untouchables were not allowed to sit in or touch
upper-caste carts. Finally, one cartman agreed but on the condition that the boys
themselves would drive the cart while he walked beside it. After some time, the
cartman left to have food and later returned. He sat in the cart due to fear of the dark but still kept distance from the children. The boys were frightened and
cried, suspecting he might harm them.

Late at night, they reached the toll-collector’s hut. Even there, they were not
allowed to drink water because of caste rules. Hungry, thirsty, and tired, they had
to wait until their father finally arrived and took them home.

This whole journey deeply affected young Ambedkar. He realised how cruel and
inhuman the practice of untouchability was. The experience shaped his
determination to fight against caste discrimination and work for equality and
justice throughout his life.

## II. Short Notes
1. Ambedkar’s Father
Ambedkar’s father was a disciplined, responsible, and principled man who
served in the Army of the East India Company. He rose to the respectable rank of
Subhedar, which shows his dedication and leadership qualities. After retirement,
he worked as a cashier at Koregaon, supervising the government’s famine-relief
work. Although he could not visit his children often due to his duties, he
maintained strong communication with them. He carefully planned their journey
to Koregaon and instructed them about the travel details, showing his care and
concern as a parent. His values of honesty, hard work, and discipline had a deep
influence on young Ambedkar. Even though he was physically absent during the
incident, his presence is strongly felt through the children’s efforts to reach him.
Ambedkar remembered his father as a person who balanced duty and affection,
and his disciplined background shaped Ambedkar’s own personality and later
commitment to social reform.

## 2. Ambedkar’s Aunt
Ambedkar’s aunt played a significant role in his childhood, especially after the
death of his mother. She was extremely loving and gentle, but physically weak.
She was a dwarf and had trouble walking, which made daily tasks very difficult
for her. Despite her limitations, she took care of Ambedkar and his brothers with
great affection. Since she could not cook properly, the children had to manage
their own meals and often prepared simple dishes like pulav. Before their
journey, she lovingly arranged food for them with the help of neighbours. Her
emotional reaction when the children left for Koregaon shows her deep
attachment to them. Although she could not offer physical help, her presence
gave the children emotional support and comfort. Ambedkar remembered her
kindness throughout his life. Her character represents the silent, caring women in families who support children with love, even when they cannot contribute
physically. She added warmth and humanity to Ambedkar’s early life.

3. The Station Master at Masur
The station master at Masur is an important character who reflects the caste
attitudes of society. When Ambedkar and his brothers arrived at the station, the
station master treated them kindly, assuming they were Brahmins because of
their clean clothes and polite behaviour. However, when Ambedkar innocently
revealed that they were Mahars (untouchables), the station master’s expression
changed immediately. He withdrew from them and hesitated to help further,
showing the deep-rooted social prejudice of the time. Yet, when he noticed that
the children were helpless and stranded, he felt sympathy and tried to arrange a
bullock cart for them. However, even his authority could not convince the
cartmen because none of them wanted to carry untouchable children. Finally, he
came up with a solution where the children would drive the cart themselves. His
mixed behaviour—kindness followed by discrimination—clearly shows how even
well-placed people were victims of caste-based thinking. He represents society’s
inability to rise above untouchability.

4. The Cart Driver

The cart driver symbolizes both the cruelty and helplessness created by caste
rules. At first, he refused to take Ambedkar and his brothers because carrying
untouchables would “pollute” him. He only agreed when he learned that the
children themselves would drive the cart, allowing him to walk beside it. This
shows how deeply caste restrictions controlled everyday life. During the journey,
however, his behaviour changed. When darkness surrounded them, he jumped
into the cart out of fear, forgetting his earlier concerns about pollution. This
frightened the children, who suspected that he might harm them for their gold
ornaments. Still, he continued guiding them and finally brought them to the tollcollector’s hut at midnight. The cart driver also advised Ambedkar to lie about
his caste to get water, which shows that he understood the harshness of social
rules but still followed them. His divided behaviour reflects the contradictions of
caste society—fear of untouchability mixed with human concern.

## 5. Water Issues and Ambedkar

Water plays a symbolic and practical role in Ambedkar’s journey. Throughout
their travel, Ambedkar and his brothers struggled to get clean drinking water
simply because they were untouchables. At the riverbed, the only available water
was filthy, mixed with mud and cattle waste, and unfit for drinking. Later, at the
toll-collector’s hut, Ambedkar tried to get water by pretending to be a Muslim,
speaking in Urdu to sound convincing. But even then, the toll collector refused to help, showing how deeply prejudice controlled basic human needs. The children
were forced to sleep hungry, despite having plenty of food, because they could not
drink water. This helplessness made a strong impact on young Ambedkar’s mind.
He realised that untouchability was not just social humiliation—it affected basic
survival. The denial of water became a powerful symbol of social oppression, and
this painful memory stayed with Ambedkar throughout his life, inspiring him to fight against caste injustice.

## 6. Ambedkar and His Brothers
Ambedkar and his brothers began their journey with excitement. It was their
first train journey, and they wore new clothes, carried food, and even bought
lemonade at the station. However, their joy soon turned into fear and suffering.
At Masur station, no one came to receive them, and after learning their caste, the
station master and cartmen refused to help. The children had to drive the cart
themselves, travelled through darkness, and faced loneliness and hunger. Their
fear increased when the cart driver suddenly sat beside them in the dark. They
even suspected that he might harm them for their ornaments. At the toll hut, they
were denied water again because of untouchability. Despite all these hardships,
the children remained united and courageous. The incident taught them the
harsh reality of caste discrimination at a young age. This painful journey shaped
Ambedkar’s understanding of social injustice and influenced his future fight for
equality.

## 7. Social Injustice and Ambedkar – 05/10 Marks

The essay clearly exposes the cruelty of social injustice faced by Ambedkar and
other untouchables. Even as a nine-year-old boy, Ambedkar faced humiliation at
every step—at the railway station, on the road, and even when asking for water.
The station master hesitated to help him, cart drivers refused to carry him, and
the toll collector denied him water. The journey became a painful lesson about
the caste system’s inhumanity. Ambedkar realised that untouchability was not
limited to school rules or daily restrictions—it followed people everywhere, even
during emergencies. This incident left an indelible mark on his mind. It
awakened in him the desire to challenge social discrimination. He understood
that untouchability denied dignity, safety, and basic human needs. This early
experience contributed greatly to shaping his life’s mission to fight for equality,
justice, and the rights of the oppressed. The essay shows how personal suffering
became the seed of future social revolution.

## 8. Ambedkar’s Train Journey – 05/10 Marks

Ambedkar’s first train journey began with great joy and excitement. He and his
brothers wore new clothes, carried delicious food prepared by neighbours, and
even enjoyed lemonade at the station. They eagerly boarded the train and looked

## Briefly narrate young Ambedkar’s experiences at Masur railway station.

After waiting long hours at Masur, the children were in despair. Finally, one Muslim cartman, who was kinder than the others, agreed to take them to Goregaon. Though it was humiliating, the children had no choice but to accept it. The cart took them through dusty roads, and the young Ambedkar realized how even a simple journey could turn into an ordeal for the so-called “untouchables.”
The experience left a strong impression on his mind. It was not just the distance they travelled but also the distance society created through caste discrimination that became unforgettable.

At Masur station, caste discrimination was very clear. When Ambedkar approached people for help, they first showed sympathy but withdrew as soon as they heard his caste. No one offered water, food, or a place to rest. They were treated as untouchables, as if their presence polluted others.

This cruel treatment showed Ambedkar, at a young age, the harsh realities of caste system—how it stripped people of humanity and compassion. He realized that untouchability was not just about social status but also about deep humiliation and denial of basic human needs.

## Explain briefly how Ambedkar experienced ritual purity and caste hierarchy in his journey to Goregaon.
Ambedkar’s journey to Goregaon exposed him directly to the ideas of ritual purity and caste hierarchy. Later, even the cartman, though kinder than the others, forced the children to sit separately in his cart. This was another reminder that in society’s eyes they were “polluting.” The cartman was afraid that if they sat with him, he too would be considered impure by others.
Thus, throughout the journey, Ambedkar learned how caste rules governed social behavior in India. Even the simplest human actions—like drinking water, sitting in a cart, or asking for help—were controlled by the hierarchy of caste.

The painful journey to Goregaon became a turning point in young Ambedkar’s life. He learned that society was deeply unjust and that the caste system denied dignity and humanity to millions of people. The humiliation at Masur station and the cart journey taught him that the problem was not just personal but social.
From these bitter experiences, Ambedkar developed a strong sense of determination. He realized that education and courage were the only ways to fight against discrimination. These childhood lessons shaped his future mission to bring equality, justice, and self-respect to the oppressed communities of India.
Later in life, as a great leader, thinker, and the chief architect of the Indian Constitution, Ambedkar often recalled this journey. It gave him the fire to fight against untouchability and to build a society based on liberty, equality, and fraternity

## INTRODUCTION
Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, or feelings between
persons. It involves both sending and receiving messages. The purpose of communication is to achieve understanding on both the sides of the sender and the receiver. Though there are many
types of communication, the written communication is of utmost importance. It is done through
letters and emails.
Business communication is the process of exchanging information between buyers and sellers to achieve business goals. When direct interaction is not possible between a buyer and a seller, letters
and emails are used for interaction. By means of business letters and mails, business activity is
conducted. Effective business communication is crucial for clarity, collaboration, decision-making,
building business relations, and promoting business.  An enquiry is made, quotation is received,
order is placed, goods are received, and payments are made. Thus, all these business activities
related to the act of buying and selling are done by means of business letters and emails. Inland
and foreign trade are also conducted through letters and emails. Hence, it is necessary for every
educated individual to have the basic knowledge of business communication.
## The Principles of Writing Business Letters:

The principles of writing business letters are somewhat different from the principles of
writing general letters.  Business letters are used primarily to communicate with external
stakeholders such as consumers, customers, intermediaries, government, bankers, etc. Business
letters are much more formal than general letters.  Let us look briefly at some of these principles:
- Structure: The format (arrangement), the layout (design), and the overall look of the letter
should be pleasing and appealing to the reader.  Attention should be paid to the quality of
paper used. All the rules of typing should be observed. The margins should be appropriate,
including one inch on each side and one and a half inches on the left.
- Content: Utmost importance should be given to the content of the business letters. It is
the most important of all the principles which brings in the expected result. The content
should be clear, brief, and to the point. There should be no room for ambiguity and different
meanings. It should be precise and definite.
- Clarity and Brevity: Business letters should be clearly worded. It is better to avoid the
use of jargon, technical terms, and slang words.  Concrete words should be used so that
there is no ambiguity. Example: Instead of saying ‘I received your communication recently,’
it is better to be more precise and say, ‘I received your letter yesterday.’ The letter should
include a single main idea and paragraphs should be used to elaborate it with sub-ideas
and supporting points. Business letters should be brief and to the point, avoiding
unnecessary details and round about expressions.  They should give maximum information
to the reader, using minimum words.
- Formal Courtesy: It is very important to retain the goodwill of customers, other people
concerned with business, and the general public. A discourteous letter can make you lose
business. Therefore, business letters should be extremely polite at all times and mindful of
the words ‘please,’ ‘thank you,’ and ‘sorry.’  The customer should never be made to feel
unimportant or unworthy. Every customer, small or large, must be respected and treated
courteously. You must respond politely even to a rude letter from a customer in order to retain the business relation.

If the business company has been at fault, it is important to apologise to the customer for
the mistake and for the inconvenience caused.  The overall tone should be positive.  For
example, instead of saying ‘We cannot grant your request,’ state it in a more tactful way,
explaining the reasons for not being able to grant the request.  You can write, ‘As the
warranty period has expired, we are unable to grant your request of servicing the product
without any charges.’
- You Consideration: It means that you should look at the communication from the reader’s
point of view and appeal to the reader’s interests.  It is very important to stress the you
attitude rather than the me attitude.  This is similar to the language of advertisements,
which talk about the benefits of the product to the end user. For example, instead of saying
‘We will be open 24 hours,’ say ‘You can avail round-the-clock service.’

## The Structural Parts of Business Letters:

The structure and format of business letters is as important as the content. Most business
letters contain the following structural parts at the right places:
- Letter Head: The heading of a business letter usually contains the name and postal
address of the business firm, along with its Email address, Web-site address, Telephone
number, Fax, etc.  The Heading appears on top of the page.  Usually, business organisations
use printed letter heads which include the name, logo, and address of the company. Some
business houses get their letter heads designed by commercial artists.
- Reference: This usually indicates the letter number, the department/section of the company from which the letter is being sent, and the year.  This helps in future reference
and it is given on the left-hand corner after the heading.  Usually, it is specific to the writer
of the letter.  We can write reference number as: 224/SD/2025. Sometimes, the reference
number of the letter to which you reply is also given. Some business houses give these
numbers in line with the date as:
Our Ref. No.:                                            Your Ref. No.:                                            Date:
- Date: This must be located in the upper left-hand corner after the Reference number.
Clearly mention the number of the day, the name of the month, and the year in full. No
abbreviations should be preferred.
- To Address:  This includes the name and full address of the person or the firm to whom
the letter has to be sent.  This is written on the left-hand side below the date.
- Attention: The Attention line is usually written above the salutation, in the centre of the letter, but sometimes, it may be included below the salutation, in order to ensure prompt
action.  For example, ‘Attention: D K Sharma, Sales Manager.’
- Initial Greeting/Salutation: This is used to greet the reader before beginning the subject
of the letter in order to avoid abrupt beginning and to create a congenial mood in the reader.
When addressing a firm, Messrs should be used before the name of the firm.  Since business
letters are formal, the appropriate salutation when addressing an individual is ‘Dear
Mr/Ms,’ followed by the last name, rather than the first name, which is informal.  If the
gender of the reader is not known, it is better to use a neutral salutation such as ‘Dear
Customer’ or ‘Dear Investor.’
- Subject: It is a brief one-line statement that indicates the matter to which the letter
relates.  It attracts the attention of the receiver immediately and helps him understand
quickly what the letter is about.  Subject line can be placed after the salutation and before
the body of the letter.
- Body: This includes an explanation of the main idea(s) of the letter. It is better to introduce
the main message, explain it in detail, and conclude it.

- Closing Greeting: This is the ending of the letter and should be polite and friendly. It is
like a polite leave taking. A standard closing for a business letter is ‘Yours faithfully,’
followed by the sender’s signature, name, and designation. ‘Yours sincerely’ is sometimes
used to show some intimacy between the writer and the reader.
- Signature: The person who writes the letter should sign it at the end. It shows that the
writer is aware of and is completely responsible for everything written in it.  Some people
use facsimile signatures. But it is always better to sign the signature as it gives a personal
touch to the formal business letters.
- Enclosure: Sometimes, a business letter may include one or two enclosure/s such as a
pamphlet and/or a brochure. This should be indicated at the end of the letter by listing the
name/s of the enclosure/s.
Special note: There are no hard and fast rules in letter-writing. There may be minor variations

## 2. QUOTATIONS

Quotations are generally the replies to requests/enquiries for the same. A quotation is a statement
quoting the price of goods and services.  Quotations usually contain a list of products, their
specifications and descriptions of their type, size, quality, quantity, price, warranty/guarantee, tax,
terms of trade, delivery, mode of payment, transportation costs, etc. The price quoted in the
statement can be the price declared by the producing company or the price decided by the retailer
including the other expenses in the procurement of goods. While the company price is the same
everywhere (except service charges), the retailer price can be different from place to place and from
shop to shop. A buyer should be charged only the price quoted. Variation in the price is against the
norms. If something other than what is quoted is charged, the buyer feels uncomfortable and will
most likely not place a repeat order.

## Note: While sending quotations, the following points are to be kept in mind:
1. A clear description of the products must be given;
2. Wherever necessary/possible, samples should be sent;
3. Prices and terms must be specified;
4. Discount, if any, must be mentioned;
5. Terms for delivery must be stated, e.g., carriage paid/carriage forward;
6. Lead time should also be mentioned;
7. Limiting period can be specified if essential, e.g., subject to acceptance within 10 days.

## 3. ORDERS

Placement of orders for goods and their execution are very significant business activities. When
orders are correctly placed and they are handled effectively, it will result in achieving not only
success but also good will. It is therefore important that order letters and responses to orders are
written with great care. Despite the fact that many business organisations have started using
electronic and printed order forms, order letters are still used in businesses.

Order letters follow the general format of business correspondence and give a clear description of
the items ordered, their quantities, catalogue numbers, and prices.  They should also specify the
address and date of delivery, the mode of transport, the terms of payment (according to earlier
discussions), and whether the final price includes delivery charges or not.

Note: Orders must be promptly acknowledged and confirmed.  A letter to the buyer
acknowledging, confirming, or accepting the order puts the buyer at ease about the order placed
and improves customer relations. The seller can also inform the buyer about items from the order
that are out of stock, changes in prices, date of delivery, etc., if the order is received after the
scheduled date. Such letters are necessary in order to create a feeling of goodwill and trust.
## Points to remember:
- Thank the customer for the business and show your appreciation.
- Confirm all the details about the products ordered, the mode of transportation, payment,
and date of delivery.
- Use reference numbers like product codes and order numbers.

## 4. COMPLAINTS AND REPLIES TO THEM
Sometimes after an order has been accepted, it is possible that the customer is not completely
satisfied with the transaction. On delivery, the buyer may be unhappy with the quality of the goods, damaged goods delivered, unwanted goods, excess goods, wrong goods, different goods,
incorrect bill, excess prices charged, or with the delay in delivery. In such cases, the customer can
let the supplier/service provider know about his/her discontentment as it is the consumer’s right
to receive satisfactory goods/service. When making a complaint, it is important to be polite, to avoid
aggression or anger, and give the seller a chance to rectify the problem. After all, it is human to err.
It is important that a business organisation handles complaints promptly and with tact. The way
in which the complaint is attended to will decide whether the buyer will do business with the seller
again or not. The goodwill of any business organisation depends on the satisfaction of the customer.
Acceptance of error; honest, right, and immediate action to correct it; and politeness are very
important.
## Points to remember:
- Complaints should be clear and detailed, pointing out exactly what has gone wrong in the
transaction and how it has caused you inconvenience.
- If terms agreed upon mutually have not been kept, mention these and refer to prior
correspondence.
- Give the complaint letter a reference number for future reference in case the need for followup arises.
- Do not be angry or abusive as this might cause anger and resentment. Your complaint is likely
to be redressed more effectively if the tone of the letter is polite but firm.
- Provide necessary information such as order number, product code, delivery date, payment
details, quality of the product and its condition on receipt. This will give the seller an
opportunity to understand the problem better and solve it immediately and properly.
- If you are the seller, handle the complaint promptly and politely to retain your good will.

## 5. CIRCULARS
A circular or circular letter can be defined as a letter meant to convey information (which is of
common interest and importance) about a business firm to all of its customers, friends,
partners, and others concerned with it. Circulars could be written for communicating the
following:
1. Announcement of a reduction/discount/clearance sale.
2. Introduction of new products to market.
3. Shifting of business to new premises.
4. Establishment/opening of a new business/branch/shop.
5. Renovation/expansion of a business shop.
6. Amalgamation of business, purchase of a new business, transfer of business, taking up of agency, conversion of firm, etc.
7. The change of address, personnel, powers of members; death of a partner, retirement of a partner, admission of a new partner, granting of the power of attorney, etc.
Note:
- Circulars must have a personal touch and a friendly tone.
- The language should be attractive and polite.
- They should be clear and complete with all the details.
- The beginning should arouse interest, the message should sustain it and
the conclusion should motivate immediate and positive response.

## 6. COMMERCIAL TERMS

1. Agenda: A list of items of business to be considered at a meeting.
2. Arbitrage: Buying securities in one stock exchange and selling them in another in order to make profit out of the difference in the two markets.
3. Bear: A speculator on the stock exchange, who sells shares when prices are expected to fall in order to make a profit by buying them back again at a lower price later.
4. Bill of exchange: It is a written, unconditional order signed by one party (the drawer) directing another party (the drawee) to pay a specific sum of money to a third party (the
payee) or to the bearer of the instrument, either on demand or at a fixed or determinable
future time
5. Bill of lading: A document issued by the ship owner or his agent acknowledging receipt of goods taken on board and the terms under which they have to be carried.
6. Blockchain: It is used in crypto currency. A blockchain is essentially a continuously growing list of records called ‘blocks,’ which are linked and secured using
cryptography. Each block contains a set of verified transactions. Once a transaction
is recorded on the blockchain, it is virtually impossible to alter.
7. Blue chips: Equity shares of companies that are considered to be safe and sure to make a profit.
8. Bucket shop: The office of a broker who buys or sells on behalf of someone else on commission.
9. Bull: A speculator on the stock exchange, who buys shares when prices are expected to rise in order to make a profit by selling them back again at a higher price later.
10. Bullion: Gold or silver in the form of mass or bars.
11. Catalogue: An information booklet or brochure published by the seller which includes pictorial description of the goods available along with prices, make, qualities, and terms of trade.
12. COD: Abbreviation of cash on delivery; the price mentioned in the invoice is payable by the buyer as soon as the goods are delivered.
13. Credit Score: It is a numerical representation of an individual’s creditworthiness.

In simpler terms, it is a number that tells lenders (like banks, credit card companies,
etc.) how likely you are to repay barrowed money on time.
14. CIBIL (Credit Information Bureau (India) Limited) Score: It is another name for
Credit Score. It is a three-digit number (ranging from 300 to 900) that reflects a
person’s creditworthiness. It summarises a person’s credit history, including past
loan repayments, credit card usage, and other borrowing activities. A higher CIBIL
score indicates a lower risk for lenders, making it easier to get loans and credit cards
with better terms.
15. Credit or Status enquiry: An enquiry made by the seller about the buyer who has placed a credit order with him. The seller writes to the referees seeking information about
the buyers’ financial and business status, which would be kept confidential.
16. Crypto Currency: It is a digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for security. Unlike traditional currencies (like USD, INR), it is decentralised, meaning
it is not issued or controlled by a central authority like a government or bank. Crypto
currencies exist purely as digital entries in an online database. There are no physical
coins or bills. Crypto refers to the advanced encryption techniques used to secure
transactions and control the creation of new units. This makes them highly secure
and difficult to counterfeit.
17. Demat Account: It (Short for ‘Dematerialised Account’) is an electronic account that holds your financial securities like shares, bonds, mutual funds, government
securities, and Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) in a digital format. Just as a bank
account holds your money, a Demat account holds your securities electronically.
18. Demurrage: The daily charge made for retention of a ship, a vessel, or a cargo at a port, terminal, or other location because of the delay in loading or unloading beyond the agreed upon free time.
19. Dispatch note: Intimation of delivery of goods to the buyer, including invoice and parcel waybill.
20. Dividend: Share of profits given to the shareholders.
21. Dunning letters: Dunning letters (collection letters) or notices are formal payment reminders written to customers, who fail to make due payment within the stipulated time, making demands for the payment of the due debt.
22. Firm offer: An offer made by a seller whereby he/she promises to execute an order for specified goods at specified prices within a specified period.
23. FOB: Abbreviation of free on board, sometimes of freight on board; a price quotation which includes the cost of loading the goods onto the ship, but does not include the freight.
In this, it is the seller’s risk and responsibility till the goods are loaded onto the ship.
24. FOR: Abbreviation of free on rail; a price quotation which includes the cost of transportation of goods to the railway station and loading them into the railway wagon,
but it does not include the freight. In this, it is the seller’s risk and responsibility till the
goods are loaded onto the railway wagon.
25. Futures: Futures or futures contracts are standardised legal agreements to buy or sell a specific asset at a predetermined price on a specified future date. Futures are goods or
shares bought or sold at an agreed price for delivery at a specified future date.
26. Hammering: The process of declaring a member of the stock exchange a defaulter.
27. Hedging: It is a risk management strategy used to reduce or offset potential financial losses from an investment. It is a way of protecting oneself against loss by investing in
opposing ways. The practice of covering the risks in transactions in cash market by contra
transactions in the futures market. Thus, it is protecting one’s investment against loss
by making balancing or compensating contracts or transactions.

28. Hundi: A negotiable instrument of the nature of a bill of exchange. It is a traditional financial instrument that originated in Medieval India and has been used for centuries,
primarily in trade and credit transactions. Reserve Bank of India defines hundi as ‘an
unconditional order in writing made by a person directing another to pay a certain sum
of money to a person named in the order.’
29. Invoice: An account or bill (sent by a seller to the buyer) of goods sent or services provided with particulars of quantity, quality, and the total amount for payment at a
later time. It may also include the buyer’s order number, the mode of dispatch, and the
terms of payment.
30. Liquid assets: Assets that can be easily, quickly, and securely converted into cash without a significant loss in their market value. For example, stocks, shares, bonds, mutual funds, exchange traded funds, etc.
31. Quotation: An offer to sell goods or services at fixed prices, under specific terms and conditions, fixed number of goods/services, and before a fixed period of time.
32. Repo Rate: (short for Repurchase Agreement Rate) is the interest rate at which a country’s central bank (like the Reserve Bank of India) lends money to commercial
banks. Commercial banks sell their government securities (treasury bills or bonds)
to the central bank, with an agreement to buy them back later at a slightly higher
price. The difference between the selling price and the repurchase price is essentially
the interest paid by the commercial bank, and this interest is the repo rate.
33. Scrip: A provisional certificate of money subscribed to a bank or company, entitling the holder to a formal certificate and dividends. It also refers to an issue of additional share
to shareholder, in proportion to the shares already held, or instead of a dividend.
34. Sensex: It is a portmanteau (combination) of ‘sensitive’ and ‘index.’ It is the benchmark stock market index of India. It stands for Stock Exchange Sensitive
Index and is primarily associated with the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). It is
widely considered a barometer of the Indian stock market, and, by extension, the
overall health of the Indian economy.
35. Slump: A period of abnormally low economic activity with falling prices, sales, profits, market value, and generally low business.
36. Stag: A speculator (investor) who applies for a new issue of shares not with the intention of taking them up, but in anticipation of a rise in their value, which would enable him to
sell at a profit before he has to complete his purchase.
37. Tariff: Tax or duty imposed on certain goods of import.
38. Warranty: A written promise issued by a manufacturing company to a buyer to repair or replace a product, if necessary, within a specified period.
39. Wash sales: A fictitious transaction in which a speculator sells a share and then buys it at a higher price through another broker to create an artificial opinion about its price in the market.

## 1. LETTERS OF JOB APPLICATIONS WITH RESUME (CV)
After completing your studies, when you decide to take up a job that suits your
qualifications and skills, the first thing that you will be required to do is to write
an application for a job. But getting a job that suits you takes more than just
sending out a few applications. First of all, you need to know your own
qualifications and skills and your expectation from the job. Then, on the basis of
this, you can identify the employers who may be in search of a person with the
qualifications and skills that you possess for the kind of job that interest you.

The letter of application is written when you get to know of a specific job opening,
and you want to apply for it directly. It is usually accompanied by a resume
(pronounced re-zyu-may) also known as curriculum vitae (CV) or bio-data. Résumé
is a French word meaning ‘summary’ whereas curriculum vitae is Latin which
means ‘course of life.’ Both are similar in structure and content.

There are two types of applications: 1. Solicited applications are written in response to the advertisements which appear in the newspapers and 2.
Unsolicited applications are written not in response to the advertisements but
when a candidate comes to know of a vacancy from a reliable source.  Job
applications are incredibly important for several reasons even in today’s digital
age.

## DRAFTING AN APPLICATION (with a covering letter and a resume):

A letter of application is your very first communication with the prospective
employer, who will form his/her first impression about you from it. That is why it
is very essential that your application letter be neat, well structured, and provide
all the necessary information at a glance. Your application is a form of
advertising, so make the letter persuasive.

Your cover letter allows you to introduce yourself in a personal and compelling
way. It is your first impression that goes beyond the bullet points of your resume.
Your resume provides a factual overview of your qualifications, skills, and work
experience.

The main object of an application is to get you an interview. Therefore, it is
essential that your letter stimulates prospective employers’ interest in your skills
and accomplishments and give them a clear idea about you can satisfy the needs
of their establishment. Project your skills with confidence. This is possible only
when you believe in your capabilities and yourself.

## In your job application, your focus must be on:
- The job requirements specified by the employer—qualifications, skills,
experience, etc.
- The special qualities you possess that will make you stand out from the rest.
- Your knowledge about the organization.
- Your communication skills, problem-solving ability, soft skills, etc.
- The style and layout are also important. Avoid grammatical and spelling
errors. Use good quality paper and printing. Take care not to crumple the

edges or stain the paper.
- Omit personal information that is not relevant to the job.
- Edit the application carefully to make sure that there are no errors and it is
effective.

## An effective covering letter should have the following features:
- It should be informative and to the point.
- The tone of the letter should be friendly but formal.
- It should be a single page—three paragraphs ideal.
- Same covering letter and resume should not be used for different jobs.
- The covering letter shows that you have taken time to research the company
and the specific job, indicating your genuine interest and enthusiasm.
- The covering letter is an opportunity to show how your skills and experiences
match with the requirements of the job by highlighting your relevant
achievements.
- While the resume is a list of your skills and accomplishment, the covering
letter subtly demonstrates your soft skills of communication, critical thinking,
and problem-solving through your writing style and well-structured format.
- The covering letter should make you stand out from the rest. Many applicants
might have the same or higher qualifications than you. It should showcase
your unique personality, passion, and suitability for the post.
- Your covering letter should not appear overloaded with information. Leave
enough white space, so that it looks appealing to the eye.

## Writing a Resume/CV:
After the covering letter, we come to the second part of letter of application, which
is the personal data sheet, referred to as resume as well as curriculum vitae. Even
in walking interviews, the candidates are expected to carry her/his resume/cv
along with other related documents for the purpose of verification. Take great
care while drafting your resume/cv. If required, write several rough drafts before
writing the final draft. This will help you make your resume/cv attractive,
comprehensive, and compact.

## An effective resume/cv should have the following features:
- It should not be more than one page. But if you have a long list of qualification,
skills and experiences, you may have to prepare a somewhat longer resume/cv.
- It should be in an appropriate format. It should present all the important
information in a visually appealing manner. It helps the reader to easily grasp
and verify the facts.
- It should not include much information.
- It should have only facts and not opinions.
- It should not mention your desired salary, work schedule, or any unnecessary
information.
- The format, style, and the contents of the resume/cv should show that you are
result-oriented, capable, a go-getter, adaptable, optimistic, have personal
standards or excellence and strong communication skills.

## Specific guidelines for writing a resume/cv:

- Your resume/cv must be neatly printed or typed and appealing to the eye. The
placement and sequencing of information should be attractively designed. Subtitles should
be easily visible. Do not cram too much material on a single sheet.
- Give your contact details, such as the address at which you are presently living, a
telephone number that will always be answered during business hours and your email
address.
- Be sure to clearly list your academic qualifications and your work experience (if any). With

regard to work experience, give details of organizations where you worked earlier, with dates
and responsibilities. Highlight your achievements; for example, ‘headed a sales team of
twelve and exceeded sales targets for three consecutive years.’
- Mention any other job-related skills (these include computer and language skills) that you
think will add value to your resume/cv.
- Mention an area of expertise, for example ‘budget planning,’ to tell your prospective
employers about your professional skills. Your resume/cv should not only say what you
have done but it should indicate that you have what it takes to excel in the position
concerned. However, do not exaggerate or mention things that are not true.
- Avoid giving information that is not relevant to the application. Personal information such
as marital status, family and hobbies are strictly optional unless asked for.
- Do not use a single resume/cv for a variety of jobs. Your resume/cv should be shaped to the
requirements of each job.

## 2. E-MAIL LETTERS
Electronic mail, popularly known as e-mail, is electronic, global and interactive. Besides being fast,
it can carry pictures, videos and audio materials, publish both text and hypertext in cyberspace
and be stored on the web. E-mails are easily accessible anytime and anywhere in the world. Audio
and video clips easily fit into this format and can be sent as an attachment or within the message.
Technically speaking, e-mail is a method of communication whereby an individual or a company
uses a computer or some other electronic device (like a cell phone) to compose and send a message
to another individual or a group of individuals. Messages may be sent through computer systems
linked by a network or through modems using telephone lines. E-mails may also be sent through
wireless transmissions. They can be sent to one user at a time or broadcast to several users at the
same time and usually take only a few minutes to arrives at their destination.
Every e-mail user has a unique address, such as john@gmail.com or disha02@rediffmail.com.
Companies, institutions and governments create addresses for their employees to transact
business related to their work and for easy identification.
## Writing an E-mail:
Unlike a letter which is written on paper, put into an envelope and then sent through a post office
or a courier, an e-mail is sent through a mail server electronically, read on the computer screen
and is generally replied to immediately by clicking the reply button. Once it has been typed on
screen, it is proof-read for errors or omissions and then sent as a reply to the sender of the letter.
It should therefore be written like a letter and not like a telegram or an SMS (short message
service), both of which have space constraints.
E-mail is fast, flexible and reliable, though not always secure. Fully computerized companies use
it extensively both for internal and external communication. It is used for all kinds of business
communication including business enquiries, confirming orders, sending press releases,
announcing sales promotions, explaining price changes and updating scripts, besides a host of
other purposes.
Because of their speed and convenience, e-mail messages are increasingly being used not only for
personal but also in academic and business communication. Here are some important points to note about e-mail correspondence.
- Most businesses encourage their customers to write to them by providing a ‘Contact us’ button
on their website. Many research departments and universities have also begun to use e-mail to send assignments to students and receive their responses.
- The tone you use should be appropriate to the relation you have with the recipient of your
message. When you write to close friends, using informal or even causal language is acceptable.
But when you are writing about an official or on a serious matter, use language appropriate to the topic and the person addressed.

## An e-mail letter has the following components:
- From: The sender of the message will type his or her e-mail address here.
- To: This is for the e-mail address of the recipient; more than one address can be written,
separated by commas.
- Date: This is automatically tagged on to the message by the service provider through which the
email is sent. There is usually no need to write the date in the body of an email, unlike a formal
letter.

- Cc (Carbon copy, or copy to): This is for a list of addresses to which you want to send copies of
your message.
- Bcc (blind carbon copy): The recipients’ addresses typed here will not be seen by other
recipients. Use this if you do not want the addressee to know the identity of those you are
keeping informed.
- Subject: This is for the subject of the message. Do not leave this blank; give a specific title that
will help the recipient know what the message is about.
- Attachments: These are files which go with your message. The recipient has to download them
in order to read or see them.

Replying to a message: Replying to an e-mail is very easy. If the reader wishes to respond to an
e-mail message, he/she can do so by clicking the ‘reply’ button without retyping the e-mail address
of the sender. However, one should be careful in replying when a common e-mail has been sent to a list so as to reply only to the sender and not all the recipients.
Junk mail or spam: These are unwanted e-mails, usually of commercial nature sent out in bulk.
They come into the inbox in the form of chain letters, unsolicited advertisements, fake warnings
about viruses or files, and other non-business information. Such e-mails should not be opened and
read as they are likely to corrupt our files or hanging the system.
Spamming is unsolicited commercial e-mail. Because of the very low cost of sending e-mail,
spammers can send hundreds of millions of e-mail messages each day at very low cost. Spamming
results in information overload as this uncalled-for e-mail takes a lot of time to read and discard.
Though most e-mail services like Google, Yahoo, Hotmail, etc. have set filters to check spamming,
spammers outsmart these filters and penetrate the system.
E-mail worms are the viruses that find a way of replicating themselves into vulnerable
computers. These worms travel through e-mail and corrupt your computer. They are capable of
crashing the system totally. Many countries including US, Canada and Australia have passed laws
to take action against those who create virus systems.
## SOME FEATURES:
Netiquette: Netiquette is derived from the words ‘Internet etiquette’ and refers to the unsaid rules
to be kept in mind when using shared services and mailing lists on the Internet. Using these rules
for sending and receiving e-mails helps us to be socially responsible members of the community of
Internet users or ‘netizens.’ Netiquette, therefore, is a set of socially acceptable rules of behaviour
on the Internet. They help us to behave correctly and acceptably on the net just as rules of social
etiquette do in society. For example, interrupting a speaker, and not introducing people you are
with to others are examples of bad etiquette.
Never think you’re talking to computer: There’s a real live person at the other end. Remember
that when you consider the wording, style, and tone of your message.
Ensure you follow the rules of good writing: Just as a business letter is an ambassador for
your company, so is your e-mail. Don’t allow the speed and ease of e-mail to make your messages

abrupt, abbreviated and error-filled. Write your messages with care, considering all the rules of
modern business writing.
Take off the caps lock. DON’T SHOUT! Even though you want to get noticed, please do not use
capitals in e-mail messages—this is like shouting. It is rude and will usually be counterproductive.
And also …. NEVER RESORT TO EXCESSIVE PUNCTUATION @!! ?!!!!
Greetings and Signing off: Replace formal salutations like ‘Dear Susheel’ with ‘Hi Susheel’ or
even just ‘Susheel’ and sign them off with ‘best regards’ or ‘regards’ when writing informal emails.
Use formal greetings such as Dear Mr. Susheel’ while writing business emails. Similarly, replace
‘Yours sincerely’ with ‘Best wishes,’ or ‘Thanks’ while signing off an email.
Question your subject heading: People are most likely to read important-looking e-mails first.
Give your messages a clear and specific subject heading. Also don’t ‘compose’ a new message every
time you need to reply as it will break the thread of the subject and probably cause the recipient
to misplace important information quite easily.
Use short sentences and short paragraphs: The shorter your messages, the more likely it is
that they will be read and understood. But do not make them so short that they are abrupt or
unclear. Use full sentences and explain clearly. Remember to paragraph just the same as in other
business documents.
Enumerate with numbers or bullets: Present your messages attractively. Use numbers, bullets
or sub-headings if possible. This will add to the clarity of your message.
Tidy up long sentences: It is better to use short sentences in order to make the message compact
and clear. While writing long sentence, use punctuation to make the meaning clear. Read through
your message carefully and improve clarity and understanding.
Take pride in your finished message: Make sure your message is accurate, brief, and clear as
well as attractively presented. In this way, it will be understood and will achieve the desired
results.
Ensure everything is right before you hit ‘send’: Once sent, you cannot call an e-mail back
for second thoughts! So, remember to double check and proof read your email for any grammatical
or factual errors before sending it. Also remember to check the email addresses it is being sent to,
to ensure that your message reaches only the people concerned and not the entire organization.
Don’t spam or forward chain mails: Ensure that you never forward any personal chain mails
or other personal emails to business colleagues from your business account. This is considered
spamming and is rude. It will also create an unprofessional impression about you in your workplace
or with your clients.
Protect company confidentiality: Always check what kind of information you are allowed to send over emails as you could, mistakenly, reveal confidential company information to people who
might misuse it. With hacking becoming very common, it is of the utmost importance that
information be protected and not just sent over common, unprotected e-mail services.
## REMEMBER:
- Write a SMART subject line after you’ve written your message.
- Include an appropriate greeting and closing.
- Use modern business language and simple sentences.
- Never use ALL CAPITALS for any part of your message unless it is an acronym.
- Structure your messages logically.
- Write as if you are having a conversation with the recipient.
- Consider the other person’s feelings and make sure you use the appropriate tone.

- Arrange messages attractively, using full words, full sentences, and with a space between
each paragraph.
- Use e-mail as a tool to enhance communication and not to replace it.
- If an e-mail exchange is getting rather long or complicated, it may be more effective to pick
up the phone.

## 3. BANK CORRESPONDENCE
Bank is a place for money. It is an organization that provides various financial services like keeping
or lending money. It is very essential for the public, business people, and for the students of
commerce to know the functions of banks and understand the services they provide. Bank
correspondence, broadly speaking, refers to communication between banks and their clients,
between different banks, or among different branches of the same bank. It is very important for
the operations of the country’s economy. In fact, it acts as the lubricant oil for the wheels of the country’s economy. The main functions of the banking system are accepting deposits and granting
loans.
There are mainly two types of accounts through which usually banking transactions are made: a)
Savings Account and b) Current Account.
1) Savings account (SB A/C): This is a type of savings by the public. The interest rate is fixed
and less than the rate for fixed deposits. From the savings account the customer can draw money
whenever he requires, as per rules of the bank. Now a days, as per the instructions of the RBI, any
student can open an SB A/C at zero balance. There is no necessity for minimum balance in it.
2) Current account (CA A/C): Through this account withdrawals may be made through cheques
at any time during working days and hours without notice. The bank usually pays only nominal
interest on current accounts. This is for business people.
A student, an individual, a company, or a business firm may correspond with a bank for several
reasons:
- To open a savings account
- To open a current account
- To request for overdraft (OD) facility
- To request for a loan
- Complain about negligence etc.

A bank has to correspond with its customers for several reasons:
- Debt collection or loan recovery
- Reminders
- Legal notices or threatening letters etc.

There are several types of bank correspondences. But this chapter will focus on the types of
business letters sent by customers to banks and vice versa.

## 4. MARKET SURVEY REPORTS

Market survey report is a marketing and management device used by businesses to help them take
crucial decisions about product, brand perception, impact on the market, etc. Today there is a stiff
competition between local and multinational companies to occupy a maximum share of the market.
In such situations like these, market survey reports play a vital role in the expansion of business,
finding the reasons for poor performances, and to take steps for improvement, etc.

Generally, to have a report prepared, the following points have to be borne in mind:

1) Appointment of a reporter
A reporter is appointed orally or by means of a letter, and he is instructed about the subject-matter,
on which a survey is to be conducted and the report is to be prepared. He/she is also given a time
frame within which the report must be ready for submission.

2) Fact finding

The reporter then will collect the relevant data through interviews, meetings, consultations with
professionals, visiting local businesses/industries, surveys, investigation, etc. each fact must be
checked and rechecked to ensure accuracy in the final report.

Once the data is collected, the reporter will prepare a number of drafts before submitting the final
report. This is to ensure that the findings of the report are consistent with the data already
gathered. An unchecked and unverified report is of no use to a business.

3) Suggestions or recommendations
The reporter usually includes his/her own suggestions or recommendations based on his/her
findings unless he/she is specifically told not to include any personal views on the subject by his/her
employers.

4) Conclusion
The concluding part of a report contains the merits of the fact finding or the general view of the matters concerned. If anything extra is to be mentioned along with the report, the reporter
mentions it in this part of the report.

## 5. ESSAY WRITING
An essay is a piece of writing, often used to explore a topic, present an argument, or convey
information. It is aimed at presenting a convincing thought on any chosen subject. It has come
from the French word ‘essai’ meaning attempt or trial. The essay originated as a form in English
during the 16th century. It has enlarged its scope, meaning, and relevance through centuries. Now
we speak of the ‘Essay’ in all the branches of knowledge.
## There are the different types of Essays:
(i)
Descriptive Essay: As the word suggests, this essay is a clear description of a place, a
person, a temple, an object, a poem, a town, etc. These essays focus on describing a specific
subject in detail, allowing the reader to visualize or experience it through the author’s words.
They may use vivid language and imagery to create a strong impression.

(ii)
Narrative Essay: Here the essay is used to narrate some event, a journey, an accident, a
story, a natural calamity, a function, and all such events. These essays tell a story, often
personal, and aim to engage the reader through storytelling. They focus on the sequence of
events and may explore themes or lessons learned.

(iii) Reflective Essay: This kind of essay brings out the writer’s inner thoughts, feelings, and
emotions. It may also cover other areas including social, domestic, political, philosophical,
and religious themes and topics. These essays encourage personal reflection on experiences
and perspectives. For example: courage, patriotism, love, trust, friendship, marriage, social
customs, the meaning of immortality of the soul, God, and all such others.

(iv) Expository Essay: The word expository means expound / explain / explicate / speak of a
theory, an idea, a doctrine, etc. These essays explain or inform the reader about a specific
topic, often in a straightforward and objective manner. They may involve research, analysis,
or comparison to help the reader understand the subject matter. It includes articles on
scientific research, religious themes, philosophic topics, etc.

(v)
Argumentative/Persuasive/Discursive Essays: These essays aim to persuade the reader
to agree with the author’s point of view or position on a topic. The writer must state his/her
stand in clear terms, bring evidence to support his/her stand, and present his/her arguments
in a logical manner. The supporting arguments and evidence to back up the thesis statement
are very important in this kind of essays.

(vi) Imaginative Essays: These are essays written on imagined topics and situations. The
writer places himself in that position and goes on ruminating his inner self and writes down.
Examples: If I become a millionaire. If I go on a space tour. If I land in a desert.

How to write an Essay: Broadly speaking an essay is a statement on a topic of interest. One has to introduce the idea. So that is Beginning. The idea is to be continued citing examples. It is the
Middle and what is selected is to be Concluded. That is the end. So, each essay should have a
beginning, a middle and an end/Conclusion. Page limit depends on the topic and the writer.
Following are a few useful points to keep in mind while writing an essay:
- An essay must have the unity of theme; it means that the writer should have his/her aim
clearly set down before starting to write.

- Emphasis should be given to the main idea in the essay by placing it in a prominent position
(the beginning or the end), either by giving more space to it or by directly stating it.
- The beginning of the essay introduces its topic, the middle develops it, and the conclusion
sums it up.
- The introduction (beginning) sets the stage for the essay and is typically a paragraph long.
It should grab the reader’s attention and give a clear idea of what the essay will be about. It
sets the tone and structure of the essay, forming the foundational component of the overall
essay.
- The body should dive deeper into the topic and present arguments and evidence. Each
paragraph should focus on a single aspect of the topic. Each paragraph should lead on the
next paragraph and so on.
- The conclusion should sum up the essay. It is usually one paragraph. It should summarize
the main points of the essay and leave the reader with something to think about.
- The essay should be a balanced one. The points that are included in it must be placed in the
order of their importance.
- Most essays are written in a formal style. Therefore, complete sentences should be used and
colloquial expressions and slang are to be avoided.
- The essay should be readable with convincing thoughts and an appealing style. The reader
should not be made to go to a dictionary at the end of each sentence. The style is the dress of
thought. The essay should be dressed in good style with the use of good language.
- The writer should be careful not to take diversions and deviations. They will diminish the
interest of the reader.
Follow the steps given below:
Step 1: Specify the topic.
Step 2: Jot down the available points.
Step 3: Select the relevant points and delete the irrelevant ones.
Step 4: Order the main points as headings.
Step 5: Arrange the remaining points under the headings to get an outline.
Step 6: Develop the outline into a complete draft.
Step 7: Revise the draft, if necessary, and write the final complete essay.
