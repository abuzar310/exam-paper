Justify the Title of the Story “The Overcoat”
Introduction to the Author – Nikolai Gogol
Nikolai Gogol (1809–1852) was one of the greatest writers in Russian literature. He is famous for his realistic and satirical stories that describe the lives of ordinary people. His writings expose the problems of society, poverty, corruption, and injustice with a touch of humour and deep emotion. His famous works include Dead Souls, The Government Inspector, and The Overcoat. Gogol’s stories influenced many later writers.
Introduction to the Story – The Overcoat
The Overcoat is one of Gogol’s most famous short stories. It tells the story of Akaky Akakievich Bashmachkin, a poor government clerk who spends his life copying documents. He lives a lonely and simple life. His old overcoat becomes worn out, so he saves money for a new one. The new overcoat brings him happiness and respect, but it is stolen shortly after he buys it. The loss of the coat leads to his illness and death. Through this story, Gogol shows the hardships of poor people and the cruelty of society.
Justification of the Title
The title “The Overcoat” is very suitable because the overcoat is the central object around which the whole story revolves.
At the beginning of the story, Akaky’s old coat is torn and cannot protect him from the severe winter. His tailor tells him that it cannot be repaired. This becomes the turning point in Akaky’s life. He decides to save every possible coin to buy a new overcoat.
The overcoat becomes a symbol of hope, happiness, and dignity. Akaky sacrifices his comfort by eating less, walking carefully, and spending very little money. His dream is not wealth or luxury but simply owning a warm coat.
When he finally gets the new overcoat, his life changes. His colleagues praise him and invite him to a party. For the first time, he feels respected and accepted. The overcoat gives him confidence and happiness.
However, this happiness is short-lived. The overcoat is stolen by thieves while Akaky is returning home. The loss of the coat destroys his hopes and dreams. The authorities refuse to help him, and an important official treats him harshly. Akaky becomes ill and dies.
After his death, his ghost is said to wander the streets, stealing overcoats from others. Even after death, the overcoat remains the centre of the story.
The overcoat also has a symbolic meaning. It represents:
Human dignity and self-respect.
The dreams of poor people.
Society’s respect based on appearance.
The struggle for survival.
The cruelty and injustice faced by ordinary people.
Every important event in the story begins or ends with the overcoat. Without it, the story would not exist. It changes Akaky’s life, causes his suffering, and continues to influence the events even after his death.
Conclusion
The title “The Overcoat” is fully justified because the overcoat is not merely a piece of clothing. It is the heart of the story and a symbol of hope, identity, and human dignity. Gogol uses the overcoat to reveal the loneliness, poverty, and injustice experienced by ordinary people. Therefore, the title perfectly reflects the meaning and message of the story.

Introduction to the Story – The Overcoat

After many sacrifices, he buys one, but it is stolen. The loss of the coat leads to his illness and death. The story shows the suffering of poor people and the lack of kindness in society.
Themes of the Story
The main theme of The Overcoat is poverty and social injustice.
Akaky is poor and works honestly, but he is ignored and insulted by everyone. His low salary does not allow him to live comfortably. His struggle to buy a simple overcoat shows how difficult life is for poor people.
Another important theme is human dignity and respect. People do not value Akaky because he is poor and quiet. When he gets the new overcoat, everyone suddenly notices and respects him. This shows that society often judges people by their appearance rather than their character.
The story also explores the theme of loneliness. Akaky has no close friends or family. His work is the only thing that gives him comfort. His lonely life makes his suffering even more painful.
Dreams and hope form another major theme. Akaky’s dream of buying an overcoat gives meaning to his life. It motivates him to work hard and save money. Even a small dream can bring happiness to an ordinary person.
The story also criticizes bureaucracy and corruption. When Akaky seeks help after the theft, government officials ignore him. Instead of showing sympathy, they insult him. Gogol criticizes a system that lacks humanity and justice.
Another theme is the lack of compassion. The people around Akaky fail to understand his pain. They laugh at him, neglect him, and refuse to help him. Gogol reminds readers that kindness and sympathy are essential qualities.
The supernatural ending introduces the theme of justice. After death, Akaky’s ghost steals overcoats from others, including an important official. This suggests that justice may come in unexpected ways when society fails to protect the weak.
Finally, the story teaches the importance of human values. Money, position, and appearance should never be more important than kindness, honesty, and respect for others.
Conclusion
The main theme of The Overcoat is the suffering of ordinary people in an unfair society. Through Akaky’s life, Gogol highlights poverty, loneliness, injustice, bureaucracy, and the need for compassion. The story teaches that every person deserves dignity and respect, regardless of wealth or social status. Its powerful themes make The Overcoat a timeless and meaningful work of literature.

Gogol uses Akaky’s life to show the hardships faced by ordinary people.
The Social Situation of Gogol’s Time
During Gogol’s lifetime, Russia had a strict social system. Society was divided into different classes. The rich and powerful people enjoyed a comfortable life, while the poor struggled for survival. Government officials had authority, and common people were often treated unfairly.
One of the major problems was poverty. Many people worked hard but earned very little. Akaky is a good example of this. Although he works honestly as a government clerk, his salary is so low that he cannot even afford a new overcoat without years of saving.
Another important feature of Russian society was the bureaucratic system. Government offices had many levels of authority. Junior clerks had little respect, while senior officers enjoyed power and privileges. People were judged by their rank rather than by their character.
The society was also marked by social inequality. Wealthy people lived comfortably, while poor people lacked basic necessities. The rich ignored the problems of the poor. This difference between the rich and the poor created injustice and suffering.
Corruption and misuse of power were common. Many officials were proud and arrogant. Instead of helping the poor, they cared only about maintaining their status. When Akaky seeks help after losing his overcoat, the important official insults him instead of helping him.
Another social problem was the lack of human compassion. People laughed at the weak and ignored their suffering. Akaky becomes the target of jokes because of his simple nature. Only after he gets a new overcoat do his colleagues show him respect.
The harsh Russian winter also affected poor people. Without warm clothing, survival became difficult. The overcoat in the story becomes a symbol of protection and human dignity.
Gogol also criticizes a society where material possessions determine a person’s value. Akaky receives respect only after buying a new overcoat. This shows that people valued appearance more than goodness.
Conclusion
Gogol presents the social conditions of nineteenth-century Russia realistically. Through Akaky’s life, he shows poverty, inequality, bureaucracy, corruption, and lack of kindness. The Overcoat is not only the story of one poor clerk but also a criticism of an unjust society. Its message remains relevant even today because it teaches the importance of humanity, equality, and compassion.

Narrative Technique
The story is written in the third-person narration. The narrator knows everything about the characters and describes their thoughts and feelings.
Gogol uses realistic descriptions to present the life of Akaky and the social conditions of Russia. The details make the story believable.
He also uses satire to criticize government offices, bureaucracy, and the pride of officials. Through humour, he exposes serious social problems.
Another important technique is irony. Akaky finally achieves his dream of owning a new overcoat, but he loses it on the very same day. His greatest happiness becomes the cause of his greatest sorrow.
The story ends with a supernatural element. Akaky’s ghost wandering through the streets creates mystery and symbolizes justice.
Gogol also uses symbolism. The overcoat itself represents hope, dignity, protection, and human self-respect.
His language is simple but expressive. He mixes humour with sadness, making readers both smile and sympathize with Akaky.
Conclusion
Gogol’s narrative technique makes The Overcoat a memorable story. By combining realism, satire, irony, symbolism, and the supernatural, he creates a powerful criticism of society while telling a touching human story.

(e) St. Petersburg's Cold
Introduction
In The Overcoat, the cold winter of St. Petersburg is not just part of the setting. It plays an important role in the story. Gogol uses the severe cold to show the hardships faced by poor people like Akaky.
St. Petersburg's Cold
St. Petersburg is famous for its long and freezing winters. The cold wind is so strong that people find it difficult to walk on the streets. Gogol describes the weather in detail to make readers understand the suffering of ordinary people.
Akaky is a poor government clerk who has only an old and torn overcoat. His coat cannot protect him from the freezing cold. Every day, while walking to the office, he suffers from the cold wind.
The severe winter forces Akaky to think about buying a new overcoat. His old coat has become too thin and worn out. Without a warm coat, he cannot continue his daily life comfortably.
The cold also symbolizes the hardness and cruelty of society. Just as the winter is harsh, the people around Akaky are also cold-hearted. His colleagues laugh at him, and government officials refuse to help him.
After his new overcoat is stolen, Akaky once again has to face the freezing weather. Without proper protection, he falls seriously ill with fever, which eventually leads to his death.
Thus, the cold is not only a natural condition but also a symbol of poverty, loneliness, and human suffering.
Conclusion
The cold of St. Petersburg is an important symbol in the story. It creates the need for the overcoat and reflects the harsh realities of life. Gogol uses the winter to emphasize the difficulties faced by poor people and the lack of compassion in society.

(f) The Person of Consequence (The Important Official)
Introduction
The Person of Consequence, also called the Important Person, is one of the major characters in The Overcoat. Though he appears only for a short time, his behaviour greatly influences the tragic ending of the story.
The Person of Consequence
The Important Person is a high-ranking government official. He is proud of his position and expects everyone to treat him with great respect. He believes that his authority makes him superior to others.
When Akaky's new overcoat is stolen, he goes to this official hoping for help. Instead of listening to Akaky's problem, the official becomes angry because Akaky has not followed the proper official procedure.
He shouts at Akaky, insults him, and frightens him. The poor clerk leaves the office deeply hurt and disappointed.
The official's behaviour represents the arrogance and pride of the Russian bureaucracy. He cares more about his authority than about helping a helpless man.
After Akaky dies, his ghost appears and steals the Important Person's overcoat. This incident frightens the official and changes his attitude. He becomes kinder and more sympathetic towards others.
Thus, Gogol shows that even powerful people can learn the value of kindness.
Conclusion
The Person of Consequence represents the proud and insensitive bureaucracy of nineteenth-century Russia. Through this character, Gogol criticizes the misuse of power and teaches that authority should always be accompanied by compassion.

(g) Reception of Akaky's New Overcoat
Introduction
The arrival of Akaky's new overcoat is one of the happiest moments in The Overcoat. It completely changes the way people treat him.
Reception of the New Overcoat
After months of saving money, Akaky finally receives his new overcoat from Petrovich. He proudly wears it to the office.
As soon as he enters the office, his colleagues notice the beautiful overcoat. They gather around him, admire its quality, and congratulate him warmly.
For the first time in his life, Akaky becomes the centre of attention. People who had previously ignored him now speak politely and show him respect.
His colleagues decide that such a wonderful occasion should be celebrated. One of the officers invites everyone to a party in honour of Akaky's new overcoat.
Akaky feels happy, proud, and confident. The overcoat gives him a new identity and a sense of belonging.
However, this joyful reception is short-lived. While returning home from the party, thieves steal the overcoat. The same object that brought him happiness soon becomes the cause of his sorrow.
Gogol uses this incident to show that society often judges people by their appearance and possessions rather than by their true character.
Conclusion
The reception of Akaky's new overcoat shows how quickly people's attitudes can change. The overcoat earns him respect and friendship, but this respect disappears with the loss of the coat. Gogol criticizes a society that values appearance more than humanity.

(h) Loss of Akaki’s New Overcoat
Introduction
The loss of Akaki’s new overcoat is the most important event in The Overcoat. It changes his life from happiness to sorrow and leads to the tragic ending of the story.
Loss of Akaki’s New Overcoat
After many months of saving money, Akaki finally buys a new overcoat. It is the happiest moment of his life. His colleagues admire the coat and invite him to a party to celebrate.
Akaki attends the party and enjoys the company of his colleagues. It is the first time he feels respected and accepted by society.

Late at night, while returning home through a lonely street, two thieves suddenly stop him. They threaten him, throw him to the ground, and steal his new overcoat. Akaki cries for help, but no one comes to save him.
The next day, he goes to the police to report the theft, but they do not show much interest. Later, he seeks help from the Important Person, hoping to recover his overcoat. Instead of helping him, the official insults and humiliates him.
The loss of the overcoat deeply affects Akaki. He becomes sad, helpless, and hopeless. Soon he falls seriously ill with fever and dies.
The loss of the overcoat symbolizes the loss of hope, dignity, happiness, and security. It also shows how cruel society can be towards poor and helpless people.
Conclusion
The theft of Akaki’s overcoat is the turning point of the story. It destroys all his dreams and leads to his death. Through this incident, Gogol criticizes the injustice and lack of compassion in society.

The Philosophy Behind The Overcoat
Introduction
The Overcoat is not just a story about a poor clerk and his coat. It contains a deep philosophy about life, society, human dignity, and compassion. Gogol uses Akaki’s life to teach valuable lessons.

The Philosophy Behind the Story
One of the main ideas of the story is that every human being deserves respect. Akaki is poor and ordinary, but he is honest, hardworking, and harmless. Society ignores him because of his low position, showing that people often judge others by their wealth and status instead of their character.
The overcoat symbolizes hope and self-respect. For Akaki, it is more than clothing. It gives him confidence, happiness, and acceptance in society.
The story also teaches that small dreams can bring great happiness. Akaki’s dream is simple—to own a warm overcoat. This dream gives purpose and meaning to his life.
Gogol also criticizes bureaucracy and misuse of power. The Important Person fails to help Akaki because he is proud of his position. The story reminds us that people in authority should serve others with kindness.
Another important message is the need for compassion. If Akaki had received sympathy and help, his tragic death might have been avoided.
The supernatural ending suggests that justice finally triumphs. Although Akaki suffers during his lifetime, his ghost forces the Important Person to realize his mistake.
Conclusion
The philosophy of The Overcoat teaches us to value humanity, kindness, equality, and justice. Gogol reminds readers that true greatness lies not in wealth or power but in compassion and respect for others.

Akaki’s Likes and Dislikes
Introduction
Akaki Akakievich is the central character of The Overcoat. He is a simple, honest, and hardworking man with very few desires. His likes and dislikes reveal his gentle personality.
Akaki’s Likes
Akaki loves copying official documents. He performs his work with great care and accuracy. Copying gives him satisfaction and peace of mind.
He likes a quiet and simple life. He does not enjoy luxury, entertainment, or unnecessary spending. He is happy with whatever he has.
Akaki also likes discipline and regularity. He follows the same daily routine without complaint.
After deciding to buy a new overcoat, he becomes deeply interested in everything related to it. He enjoys saving money, discussing the cloth with Petrovich, and imagining how the new coat will look.
He values honesty, hard work, and simplicity more than wealth or social status.
Akaki’s Dislikes
Akaki dislikes unnecessary arguments and conflicts. Whenever his colleagues tease him, he does not fight or take revenge.
He dislikes wasteful spending because his income is very small. He avoids buying unnecessary things and lives carefully.
He dislikes the harsh winter because his old overcoat cannot protect him from the cold.
He also dislikes being disturbed while doing his work. Copying documents is his greatest pleasure, and he prefers a peaceful environment.
Although he is often insulted, he never develops hatred toward others. His dislikes are simple and natural, showing his innocent nature.
Conclusion
Akaki’s likes and dislikes reflect his humble and gentle personality. He finds happiness in honest work and a peaceful life. Gogol uses his character to show that true contentment comes from simplicity, not from wealth or power.
