# Kirubunkal summary

Kirubunkal  
1. Opening quarrel
Eeri (the mother) scolds Seena for being lazy, useless, and lost in his own world.
She’s frustrated with her poverty and his behavior.
2. Seena’s obsession with reels
Seena wastes time watching reels of pretty girls and couples on his phone.
He compares them with his own dark-skinned wife Shalini, whom he finds unattractive.
He regrets marrying her and wishes he had married one of the “modern girls” he sees online.
3. Seena’s insecurity about his looks
Seena himself is also dark and unattractive.
He tries different soaps (Lux, Santoor, Pears, Dove) to lighten his skin but fails.
He curses his dark complexion.
4. Family background
Seena is the only son of Eeri (a fair, attractive woman) and Mudli (a very virile man).
Mudli mocked religious people, saying children are born simply through sex, not rituals.
Mudli was famous for his handsomeness and lusty nature.
One dark new-moon night, Mudli mysteriously died after following strange noises at Kirabunkal (a place outside the village).
5. Village tales
In the past, the village was full of folk tales told and retold by generations.
But now, with TV and mobile phones, people have no time for stories.
The old tales feel neglected and near death.
6. Meeting of the tales
All tales (animal tales, human tales, Puranic tales, etc.) gather under the village peepal tree (Jagtikatte).
Hirikateyajji, the grandmother tale, warns that tales are dying because no one listens.
They decide to adapt: instead of moral stories, they will create sensational tales (sex, gossip, scandals) to survive.
7. Seena and Vimli
Seena starts a secret romance with Vimli (Chandranna’s daughter).
Chandranna catches them, beats Seena brutally, and drags his daughter away.
Seena collapses near Kirabunkal.
Mudli finds him half-dead and takes him home.
When asked what happened, Seena mutters “Kirabunkal.”
That same night, Chandranna and his men attack Mudli and beat him to death.
8. Mudli’s mysterious death
Officially, people say he died of disease during an epidemic.
He is buried quickly, with no chance to reveal the truth.
Eeri receives no government compensation.
She focuses on raising Seena lovingly, giving him a mobile phone, which he misuses (porn, reels, wet dreams).
9. The spreading of tales
After Mudli’s death, gossip tales multiply.
Villagers invent different scandalous versions of why Mudli died:
Affair with Chandranna’s wife.
Affair with Chandranna’s daughter Vimli.
Affair between Chandranna and Eeri.
These tales fuel the men’s sexual fantasies and women’s anger/concern.
10. Hirikateyajji complains
She laments that tales have become corrupted.
Once, tales gave wisdom and unity. Now they only spread filth, lies, and lust.
She begs Kirabunkal (the ancient place/tree/spirit) to intervene.
11. Kirabunkal speaks
Kirabunkal says it is exhausted after centuries of watching human selfishness and destruction.
The world is dying—air, water, and earth have lost life.
People think only of themselves.
Unless humans unite, the world will collapse.
12. Conclusion
Kirabunkal once protected the village, but now it is weak and decaying.
Tales no longer bring truth, but false, sensational gossip.
Seena remains obsessed with his phone, Shalini is neglected, and Eeri is helpless.
The whole village is drowning in lies, lust, and meaningless tales.
What was once a land of living, breathing stories has become a breeding ground for falsehoods.
✅ In short:
The story mixes village gossip, sexuality, caste/class tensions, and technology’s impact.
Seena represents the new generation—lazy, addicted to reels/porn, detached from reality.
Mudli represents lust, strength, and a violent end.
Eeri represents a struggling, abandoned woman.
Tales are a metaphor for culture and truth, now corrupted into lies and gossip.
Kirabunkal is the spirit of the land, lamenting human selfishness and decay.
